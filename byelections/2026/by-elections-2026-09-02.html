"All the right votes, but not necessarily in the right order"

One by-election on Wednesday 2nd September 2026:

Kingswood

Stroud council, Gloucestershire; caused by the resignation of Liberal Democrat councillor Holly Simkiss.

A quick look at the diary reveals that Andrew's Previews will have 20 local by-elections to bring you in September 2026. Two of these are scheduled for Wednesdays, because why not? The convention is that local by-elections are held on Thursdays, but that's only a tradition and in practice any working day will do. Sometimes the polling station has already been booked for the Thursday, or the election staff are going on holiday, or the council have a meeting on the Thursday and want their new councillor to hit the ground running (although that doesn't seem to be the case here), or there's some other reason why a Wednesday poll is more convenient for everyone involved.

Anyway, we are here on the first Wednesday of September in the smaller of the two Gloucestershire settlements called Kingswood. This is not the eastern Bristol suburb of that name which gave its name to a parliamentary seat until 2024: this Kingswood is a small village on the western slopes of the Cotswolds, just to the south of Wotton-under-Edge. Quite why this village name hasn't been disambiguated from the larger Kingswood to the south isn't clear, but one factor may well have been that until 1844 the two Kingswoods were in separate counties: this Kingswood was until that point a detached part of Wiltshire. As we shall see, strange things are still happening with boundaries around here.

The ex-Wiltshire Kingswood was built around a Cistercian abbey which was founded in 1139 by William of Berkeley. All that remains of this today is the 16th-century gatehouse, which still stands at the north end of the High Street - although the arch is now the access to some houses.

The Berkeley family continued to patronise the area, and in 1384 Katharine Lady Berkeley obtained a royal licence from Richard II to found a school in nearby Wotton-under-Edge. This is still in operation today as the local secondary school, but Katharine Lady Berkeley's School moved to a new site just outside Kingswood in the 1970s. Famous former pupils include the Bible translator William Tyndale and the vaccination pioneer Edward Jenner, while the rugby union player Ben Morgan who won 35 England caps in the early 2010s was educated by Katharine Lady Berkeley's School after the move to Kingswood.

The 1844 transfer of Kingswood to Gloucestershire wasn't the last change in county boundaries around here. The 1974 reorganisation kept Kingswood parish in Gloucestershire but drew the new county of Avon around it; then in 1991 a boundary change moved part of the parish of Hawkesbury from Avon back into Gloucestershire, with the transferred area becoming a new parish of Hillesley and Tresham. This parish was added to the Wotton and Kingswood ward of Stroud district and to the Wotton-under-Edge division of Gloucestershire county council. The county division of that name still exists, and it was safely Lib Dem at the last county elections in 2025.

However, the transfer of Hillesley and Tresham left the existing Wotton and Kingswood ward oversized. This situation was resolved in 2002 with the creation of a single-member Kingswood ward to cover the parishes of Alderley, Hillesley and Tresham, and Kingswood. In 2016 this ward was expanded to take in part of the parish of Wotton-under-Edge, including Katharine Lady Berkeley's School and the hamlet of Wortley; this was designated as the South parish ward of Wotton-under-Edge. The 2016 boundaries are still shown by Ordnance Survey as the ward boundary, and other online services such as MapIt (from whom the map below is taken) have picked those lines up. However, for reasons which will become clear this appears to be a mistake by Ordnance Survey.

In 2018 or 2019 Stroud council commenced a review of its parish governance arrangements, and one of the recommendations made by the review was that the South parish ward of Wotton-under-Edge be removed from that parish and added to Kingswood parish. The original parish boundary ran close to the edge of Kingswood village, and Kingswood parish council were unhappy that they were not being consulted on planning applications for areas in Wotton-under-Edge parish which directly affected their village.

This proposal did not go down well at all with Wotton-under-Edge town council, which issued threats of legal action. Eventually a compromise was reached whereby Wortley and the area of Wotton Community PARC sports centre (which is partly funded by the town council) would remain within Wotton-under-Edge parish and the rest of South ward, including Katharine Lady Berkeley's School, would be transferred to Kingswood parish.

Once this was agreed and passed by Stroud council, the Local Government Boundary Commission were informed and they were asked to make an order to realign the ward boundaries to reflect the new parish boundaries, which they duly did: the Stroud (Electoral Changes) Order 2020 was the result. The intention was clearly that the two areas retained within Wotton-under-Edge parish should be transferred from Kingswood ward back to Wotton-under-Edge ward, but for some reason the Ordnance Survey have never updated their record of the ward boundary to match. Perhaps they were confused by the 2020 Order, which it has to be said wasn't entirely clear as to what was going on.

Now I might never have noticed all this if it weren't for the fact that Kingswood is the only Stroud ward included within the South Cotswolds parliamentary seat, which has been held since its 2024 creation by Lib Dem MP Roz Savage. Wotton-under-Edge ward, meanwhile, is part of the Stroud constituency. For some reason the Ordnance Survey's record does show the correct constituency boundary here, ie with Wortley and the Wotton Community PARC within Stroud constituency rather than South Cotswolds.

The returning officer for Stroud has also set up the four polling districts for Kingswood ward on the basis of the realigned parish boundary. (There are only three polling stations: the voters in Alderley parish are allocated to the polling station in Hillesley.)

Whatever the boundaries, Kingswood has returned the Lib Dem candidate at every election since its 2002 creation with the exception of a Conservative win in 2016. On the basis of the last contest in May 2024 this is one of the safest Lib Dem wards in the land: Holly Simkiss was returned for her first term of office with a massive 82% of the vote against Conservative, Green and Labour opposition. She stood down in August for personal reasons, prompting a by-election which the Lib Dems need to hold in order to keep group status on Stroud council, which only has one other Lib Dem councillor. The 2024 Stroud elections returned 22 Greens, 20 Labour councillors, 7 Conservatives and 2 Lib Dems, and the Green Party run a minority administration.

Defending this by-election for the Lib Dems is Fred Hulls, who is a restaurateur and Wotton-under-Edge town councillor. He is up against Louise Tandy for the Conservatives, Alasdair Liddell for the Greens, former Bristol city councillor Claire Cook for Labour and current Gloucestershire county councillor Michael Rees for Reform UK.
