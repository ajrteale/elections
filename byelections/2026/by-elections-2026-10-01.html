Amble; and
Hirst

Northumberland council; caused respectively by the death of Arty Hume and the disqualification of John Allen. Both were Reform UK councillors. Our four English by-elections today all share a feature which up to now has been unusual, but which might get more common as time goes on: a candidate from Restore Britain, the far-right group associated with the Great Yarmouth ex-Reform UK Rupert Lowe. To date Restore Britain have only contested three local by-elections (the Rupert Lowe-supported candidates who won seats in Great Yarmouth in May were elected under the banner of Great Yarmouth First, not Restore Britain). Their début came in July with a score of 3.5% and 6th out of 7 in the Old Catton division of Norfolk county council; in August Restore Britain have polled 6.5% in the Barnsley ward of Penistone East (4th out of 8) and 10.7% in the Dover council ward of Guston, Kingsdown and St Margaret's-at-Cliffe (5th out of 6). All of those by-elections saw Reform UK lose council seats that they were defending, and in the Guston case Restore Britain's 284 votes were clearly decisive in the final result: Reform UK lost that seat to the Green Party by 767 votes to 733, a Green majority of 34 votes. Lowe appears to see these results as a promising start, and we can see from the candidate lists for October that Restore Britain is clearly getting its act together sufficiently to regularly nominate candidates in local by-elections. Two of today's English by-elections are Reform UK defences, both of which are for seats on Northumberland council. Amble by the Sea is a small port on the North Sea coast, located at the mouth of the River Coquet, which was dependent for its prosperity on fishing, the coal industry and the RAF Acklington airbase; but the last two of these are long gone, leaving fishing and tourism. The beaches here are wide and sandy, while only ¾ mile off the coast is Coquet Island which is managed by the RSPB as a nature reserve. Coquet Island has tens of thousands of puffins nesting there, together with the UK's largest population of the rare roseate tern. The area's human population has been very little affected by immigration: in the 2021 census Amble ward, which was larger than, made the top 50 wards in England and Wales for White British ethnicity (97.8%) and the top 90 for residents born in the UK (97.9%). Hirst ward is rather different in character but shares a coalmining history. This is one of 6½ Northumberland wards covering the town of Ashington, which was traditionally divided into two by the railway line: Hirst was the east of the town. Hirst Park lies at the southern end of this ward, surrounded by miners' terraced houses which still make up the majority of this ward's housing stock: in the 2021 census Hirst ward, which then had slightly different boundaries, made the top 40 wards in England and Wales and had the second-highest figure in the North East for terraced housing. The ward also had the highest figure in the North East, and in the top 40 nationally, for residents with 5+ GCSE passes or equivalent. The local economy is now underpinned by the healthcare sector, and Wansbeck General Hospital is just outside the ward boundary. The northern half of Hirst ward is the open space of Queen Elizabeth II Country Park, which includes the surviving pithead buildings of Woodhorn Colliery. Next to these is a new museum building, opened in 2006, which houses the Northumberland county archives and has a permanent exhibition showing the work of the Ashington Group, pitmen with a passion for art who met weekly in Ashington from 1934 to 1983 and painted scenes from their own lives. Ashington's connection with the coal industry is such that the town's Labour MP since 2010 has been Ian Lavery, who had succeeded Arthur Scargill as president of the NUM in 2002. Lavery originally represented the Wansbeck constituency, which was redrawn in 2024 with the name of Blyth and Ashington. Amble has a very different parliamentary history as a long-standing part of the sprawling Berwick-upon-Tweed constituency, which was expanded in 2024 and given the new name of North Northumberland: its first-ever Labour MP, elected in 2024, is David Smith who defeated the outgoing Berwick MP Anne-Marie Trevelyan. Reform UK narrowly polled the most votes in the 2025 election to Northumberland council - 29.3%, against 28.8% for the Conservatives and 22% for Labour - but it's seats that count, and the Conservatives ended up with a 26-23 lead over Reform UK on Northumberland council. The remaining seats were split 8 to Labour, 7 to independents, 3 to the Lib Dems and 2 to the Greens. The Conservatives continue to run the show here as a minority administration. Reform UK have already lost one by-election to the Conservatives this year, in Cramlington South West in April, and they have two more seats to defend today. Arty Hume, who was elected in Amble ward last year with 44% of the vote against 38% for Labour and 17% for the Conservatives, died in July at the age of 71 following a career as an electrician in the mines, as a police officer and as a part-time steward for Newcastle United football club. He was described by his family following his death as "a humble, polite, respectful man that was well liked cross party in the chamber". The same cannot be said of John Allen, who won Hirst ward for Reform UK last year by the similarly narrow margin of 50-43 over Labour. Hirst was thrown out of Reform UK later that year for online comments saying that "I'd shoot Starmer myself if I had the weapon and opportunity", and he then lost his seat on the council in August for failing to turn up to any meetings of the council in six months. John Allen is seeking re-election in the Hirst ward by-election caused by his own disqualification, this time as an independent candidate. The official defending Reform UK candidate is Ashington town councillor Kerry Davison, who unsuccessfully stood for Northumberland council in Ashington Central ward last year. Another town councillor on the ballot is Labour's Melanie Fox, who is a provider of creative wellbeing workshops. Also on the Hirst candidate list are David Ferguson for the Conservatives, Calvin Clark for the Lib Dems and Eden Webley - who had been the Brexit Party candidate for Wansbeck in the 2019 general election - for Restore Britain. There is a shorter ballot paper in Amble with two ladies, one man and Warren Ladyman who is the defending Reform UK candidate: he stood in the extremely far-flung Bellingham ward last year. Labour have the left half of the field all to themselves with their candidate Amy Spriggs, who will be hoping for better than her fifth-place performance in the neighbouring Amble West with Warkwarth ward in 2025. The Conservatives have nominated Jenny Richards and the Restore Britain candidate is Michael Joyce.

Claremont

Blackpool council, Lancashire; caused by the death of Labour councillor Ivan Taylor. For this week's Labour defence we're off to the Blackpool Illuminations. Claremont ward is basically Blackpool's North Shore, running from the North Pier up to the Grand Hotel (ex-Hilton), which is a strong contender for the title of ugliest building in the North of England. The seafront in between is filled with hotels, including the Metropole which has recently been cleared of its asylum seekers. The ward boundary includes the Blackpool North railway terminus which is one the main points of entry to the town for tourists and holidaymakers; this is also now a stop for the town's trams, with a branch line opening in 2024 from the railway station to the existing line at North Pier. None of this has much to do with the Claremont name: the Claremont area lies at the eastern end of this ward around Claremont Road and Claremont Park, well away from the beach. The 2021 census return, taken when Claremont ward had slightly different boundaries to those of today, lays bare the area's social problems and its dependence on the tourist industry. Claremont ranked 5th of the wards in England and Wales for residents who were long-term sick or disabled, at 14.3% of adults; this was the highest figure for any ward in north-west England. It ranked in the top 20 wards for residents employed in the accommodation and food service sector (17.9%), in the top 70 wards for unemployment (7.5%) and in the top 80 wards for private renting (51.4% of households). The late Labour councillor Ivan Taylor had the job of trying to address those problems for an extraordinarily long time. Taylor was one of the very few remaining councillors from before the 1974 reorganisation, having been first elected to Blackpool county borough council all the way back in 1966. He had 53 years of continuous service on Blackpool council from 1973, rising to a total of 59 years once his two non-consecutive spells on the old county borough council are added in. When he died in July at the age of 86, he was thought to be the UK's longest-serving councillor. Ivan Taylor was leader of Blackpool council from 1991 to 2000, during which the council became a unitary authority independent of Lancashire county council, and he also served in multiple roles in the council's cabinet. He was mayor of Blackpool in 2002-03. Ivan's wife Sylvia, who died in 2021, was also his Claremont ward colleague for 20 years from 1995 to 2015, and she had her own mayoral year in 2012-13. In all Ivan Taylor won twelve terms of office, with his final re-election for Claremont coming in 2023 alongside the present council leader Lynn Williams. On that occasion the winning Labour slate polled 52% of the vote against 22% for the Conservatives and 14% for an independent candidate. Boundary changes for the July 2024 general election transferred Claremont ward into the Blackpool South constituency. This is represented by Labour MP Chris Webb, a Blackpool lad who had previously served for three years on Manchester city council and then for three years as deputy police and crime commissioner for Lancashire. Webb had had two previous attempts to get into Parliament for Blackpool North and Cleveleys before he won the May 2024 parliamentary by-election in Blackpool South, which did not then cover Claremont ward. There have been no elections here since July 2024, so this is the first opportunity for the voters of Claremont ward to have their say in the political changes which have occurred during the current Labour government. Defending Ivan Taylor's seat for Labour is Katie Donnelli-Hunn, who works as a House of Commons caseworker and is standing in her first election. The Conservatives have selected John Thomson, who stood unsuccessfully in the neighbouring Brunswick ward in 2023. We have an independent candidacy from Kim Knight, who stood in the 2024 general election as the Alliance for Democracy and Freedom candidate in Blackpool South; Knight had previously stood under that banner in the 2024 Blackpool South by-election, and she has previously stood as a Reform UK candidate for Blackpool council and as the Brexit Party parliamentary candidate for Bootle in 2019. The Lib Dem candidate is Kevan Benfold; Restore Britain have nominated Paula Brooksbank; Reform UK have selected Mark Butcher, who was runner-up in Blackpool South in July 2024 and third in the by-election two months earlier when this column noted his track record of charity work in the town; and the Green Party candidate is Rob Jones. Long Whatton and Diseworth

North West Leicestershire council; caused by the death of Conservative councillor Nick Rushton. Our final ward today is in a location where many pass through but few stop. The villages of Long Whatton and Diseworth lie to the north-west of Loughborough and to the south-east of Derby, straddling the junction of the M1 motorway and the A42 road which is a major part of the East Midlands' infrastructure. This junction lies next to the Donington Park motorway service area and to East Midlands Airport, which this ward covers part of (although not the passenger terminal). East Midlands, which is run by the Manchester Airport Group and thus partly owned by the ten Greater Manchester borough councils, is a reasonably busy passenger airport favoured by the low-cost airlines. Its busiest route is to Alicante, with 369,000 passengers last year. However, its main importance to the British economy is as an air cargo hub: within the UK only Heathrow handles more freight, and cargo flights regularly take off and land here from as far away as China and Alaska. As a result of this the area around East Midlands Airport is filled with business and distribution parks which have obliterated the old parish boundaries here: the boundary of this ward takes a slice through the Nestlé warehouse to the north of the runway in a rather complicated way which no longer makes any sense on the ground. The airport has a Derby DE postcode, and there was a failed attempt a few years back to rename it after Nottingham, but this area is covered by Leicestershire county council and by the North West Leicestershire local government district. Long Whatton and Diseworth's excellent road connections to all three of the major East Midlands cities gives the area a strong commuter demographic, and for much of the 21st century this has been a heavily Conservative area. At the most recent poll for this ward in 2023, the Conservatives' Nick Rushton was re-elected with a 60-33 lead over Labour. North West Leicestershire council is hung after the Conservatives lost overall control in 2023 after 16 years: that year's election returned 17 Labour councillors, 12 Conservatives, 5 Lib Dems and 4 independents. With 20 seats required for a majority, the Lib Dems found themselves holding the balance of power. Unusually, the North West Leicestershire branch of the Lib Dems decided to throw their lot in with the Conservatives and an anti-Labour Tory-led coalition was formed, which continues to run the council. The district has almost the same boundaries as the North West Leicestershire parliamentary seat, a key marginal which has voted for the government at every election since its creation in 1983. Its 2010-24 Conservative MP Andrew Bridgen went somewhat mad towards the end of his term: among other controversies he got into a long legal dispute with his own family over a potato and vegetable business, and he was eventually thrown out of the Conservative Party in 2023 for antivax views. Bridgen sought re-election as an independent candidate in 2024, finished sixth out of seven candidates and lost his deposit; his vote total of 1,568 was larger than the 1,012-vote majority over the Conservatives of North West Leicestershire's new Labour MP Amanda Hack. She had been a Leicestershire county councillor, representing Braunstone, before entering Parliament. The most recent poll here was the 2025 Leicestershire county council election, in which Long Whatton and Diseworth ward is part of the Valley division (quite which valley this is isn't obvious from the map). This was a relatively narrow Reform UK gain last year, with their candidate Michael Squires defeating Conservative county councillor Nick Rushton by 36-29; the Greens were best of the rest on 17%. Rushton was a very long-serving and high-profile figure who had been first elected to Leicestershire county council all the way back in 1989 in Ashby de la Zouch: after losing that seat in 1993 he was elected for Breedon in 2001 and continuous service since, transferring to Valley division following boundary changes in 2005. He served for nine years as deputy leader of the county council and was then leader of the county council for 12 years from 2012 until 2024, eventually standing down from the top job to undergo treatment for cancer. Rushton died in July at the age of 70. Nick Rushton had an even longer career in local government at district council level, where he was first elected to North West Leicestershire council in the former Ivanhoe ward in 1987: he lost his seat in 1995 in Breedon ward, got it back in 1999 and had continuous service since then, transferring to Long Whatton and Diseworth following boundary changes in 2015. Rushton had served in North West Leicestershire's cabinet since 2007, and he was alao active on the town council for Ashby de la Zouch which had recently voted to give him the title of honorary alderman. Hoping to pick up some of Rushton's personal cote will be the defending Conservative candidate Craig Smith, who is the county councillor for Coalville North and was the losing Conservative parliamentary candidate here in 2024: in 2023 Smith contested Hugglescote St Mary's ward. The Labour candidate is Damilola Ojuri who has had a couple of unsuccessful elections in this district recently: in the 2025 county election she was the Labour candidate for Ashby de la Zouch, presumably following in the footsteps of Pandora Braithwaite. Also standing are Ian Bradley for the Lib Dems, Reform UK's Charles Pugsley who is the county councillor for Castle Donington and Kegworth, Drew Timms for the Greens and Simon Tunnicliffe for Restore Britain.
