Six by-elections on 29th October 2026:

Mid Argyll

Argyll and Bute council, Scotland; caused by the death of independent councillor Douglas Philand. We're going to start our journey around this week's by-elections in a rather remote corner of the Scottish Highlands. The Mid Argyll ward covers a number of settlements along the western shore of Loch Fyne, a sealoch which extends for 40 miles from the Sound of Bute and effectively cuts this area off from the Central Belt. The main road connection to this area is an extremely indirect route via the western shore of Loch Lomond and the mountain pass of the Rest and Be Thankful, which has a recent history of landslips and is often impassable in winter. The largest centre of population in Mid Argyll ward is Lochgilphead, with 1,841 electors on the roll according to the notice of poll for this by-election. This is the seat of Argyll and Bute council, which is based in the 19th-century manor house of Kilmory Castle. Argyll's previous county town was the royal burgh of Inveraray, which is lcoated further up Loch Fyne and owes its existence to the presence of Inveraray Castle, a Gothic Revival pile which is still the home of the Duke of Argyll. The present castle and town date from the 18th century, with the town being relocated at that time to give the castle a more secluded setting. It is sometimes claimed to be Scotland's second-smallest town (after New Galloway), with 492 electors on the roll. Inveraray was the birthplace in 1863 of Neil Munro, who became a journalist and newspaper editor in Glasgow but is now remembered as the author of a number of humorous stories about the Clyde puffer the Vital Spark and its captain Para Handy. The stories have been adapted for television on a number of occasions, most recently in 1994-95 with Gregor Fisher (of Rab C Nesbitt fame) as Para Handy. Clyde puffers would have been the main link from here to Glasgow until the 20th century, and they could also cross from Loch Fyne to the Atlantic Ocean and the Inner Hebrides via the Crinan Canal, which opened in 1801 between Ardrishaig on the Loch Fyne coast and Crinan on the Sound of Jura. The canal avoids a very long detour for shipping around the Kintyre peninsula. In summer months Jura is linked to this ward by a fast passneger catamaran, which runs four days a week from the small sailing centre of Tayvalich on Loch Sween: crossings take around an hour each way. This area most recently went to the polls in May for the Scottish Parliament election. The Argyll and Bute Holyrood constituency has been in SNP hands since 2007, and its MSP since 2021 has been Jenni Minto who was a junior health minister going into that election; she is now the convenor of Holyrood's standards committee. Second place here in May went to the Lib Dems' Alan Reid, who was the Westminster MP for Argyll and Bute from 2001 to 2015 and has been trying to get back into public office ever since then. Reid has now lost four Westminster elections in a row to the SNP's Brendan O'Hara, who was re-elected in 2024 in the redrawn seat of Argyll, Bute and South Lochaber. The Scottish National Party also finished as the largest group on Argyll and Bute council following the last election in 2022, with 12 seats against 10 Conservatives, 7 independents, 5 Lib Dems, and one seat each for Labour and the Scottish Greens. The present administration is a coalition of the SNP, the Lib Dems and some of the independent councillors. Mid Argyll ward was created in 2007 and returns three members to the council chamber in Kilmory Castle. One of them was independent Dougie Philand, who was provost of Argyll and Bute when he died in August at the age of 64 following a long illness. Philand was described in the council's tribute as "a well-kent face around Lochgilphead, Ardrishaig and the wider Mid Argyll area", who had had a long career in local mental health services Dougie Philand had sought election in 2003, the last Argyll and Bute council election held under first past the post, as a Labour candidate in Lochgilphead ward: he lost to independent candidate Donnie Macmillan. In the 2007 election for Mid Argyll ward Philand stood as an independent and started in fourth place with 16% of the vote, but overtook the SNP candidate in the final round on transfers from another independent to win the ward's final seat by 796 votes to 735. Philand had no trouble in his three subsequent re-elections, topping the poll and being elected on the first count on each of those occasions. In 2007 the other two seats in Mid Argyll went to the Lib Dems and to independent Donnie Macmillan, who also died earlier this year. The Lib Dems lost their seat to the SNP in 2012, and Macmillan lost his seat to the Conservatives in 2022. In the 2022 election first preferences here were 36% for Philand, 31% for the SNP's Jan Brown and 12% for the Conservatives' Garret Corner, who started the race for the final seat with a big lead over the field but eventually held off independent candidate Andy Cameron by just five votes in the final count. Cameron had done well to get that far, having started the count in sixth place on 4.4%: he overtook Labour on transfers from the Independence for Scotland Party candidate, and then picked up transfers from Labour to knock out Donnie Macmillan and get into the final four. If we re-run the 2022 count for a single seat, it would go to Dougie Philand with a 59-41 margin over the SNP after transfers. Interestingly, if we eliminate Philand and redistribute his votes, then the result after transfers is a win for the SNP's Jim Brown at the 3-candidate preferred stage, with 53% of the vote to 24% for Donnie Macmillan and 22% for the Conservatives. This is perhaps not a promising sign for the new independent candidate in this by-election, David Moncrieff. The SNP have selected Andy Buntin, who is chair of Lochgilphead community council. Standing for the Conservatives is Colvin Osborn, a former Royal Navy officer who lives in Tayvalich; Osborn was in 2022-24 the Commissioner's Representative in the British Indian Ocean Territory, a role which is normally held by the commander of the UK forces on Diego Garcia, and he describes himself on LinkedIn as an "international security consultant, veteran, company director, brewery owner and yachtsman". A ballot of seven candidates is completed by Douglas Macdonald for the Lib Dems, George Love who is the first election candidate in Scotland for Restore Britain, Kaine McConnell for Reform UK and Fiona Nelson for the Independence for Scotland Party. Beechwood and Heath

Halton council, Cheshire; caused by the death of Liberal Democrat councillor Margaret Ratcliffe. We now cross south of the Border to reach Runcorn, a Cheshire New Town which lies on the south bank of the Mersey estuary. The Mersey and the Ship Canal squeeze through a rather narrow gap here, which is overlooked by the summit of Runcorn Hill: this is one of a number of sandstone summits here, although it is not on the same scale as Frodsham Hill or Helsby Hill to the south-west. Running south-west from here we find the housing of Runcorn Heath next to the railway line from Liverpool, and then the Beechwood Estate next to the M56 motorway junction. The land here falls away rapidly towards the Weaver Navigation, which is crossed by high viaducts on the motorway and on the adjacent Chester to Warrington railway line. Much of Runcorn is New Town development built as Liverpool overspill, but Beechwood and Heath ward is different. Beechwood's housing in particular was developed privately. In the 2021 census Beechwood and Heath was in the top 100 wards in England and Wales for owner-occupation (88.5%), and in general it is a far more middle-class area than Runcorn as a whole. This area also votes in a far more middle-class way than Runcorn as a whole. In local elections this is Runcorn's Lib Dem hotspot and has been for many years. Beechwood and Heath has consistently voted Lib Dem since its creation in 2021; of the predcessor wards, the Lib Dems won Heath every year from 2002 to 2019 with the exception of a Labour win in 2015, while the smaller Beechwood ward voted Lib Dem until the formation of the Coalition and Labour thereafter. The political context in Runcorn changed with the extremely narrow Reform UK gain in the Runcorn and Helsby by-election last year. The by-election winner Sarah Pochin, who was the first and still the only woman to be elected as an MP on the Reform UK ticket, subsequently had her win upheld by the Election Court following an ill-advised legal challenge by the candidate of the English Constitution Party. She took the seat over from Labour's Mike Amesbury, who had resigned following an assult conviction. Reform UK went on to win all but one of the wards in Runcorn at the May 2026 Halton council election, with Beechwood and Heath being the one that got away: the Lib Dems held off Reform UK here by 45-40. It may be a significant factor in that result that Labour - who had finished a strong second here between 2021 and 2024 - did not field a candidate in Beechwood and Heath in May. The Halton Lib Dems have big shoes to fill here after their group leader died in August. Margaret Ratcliffe was a long-serving councillor who had been on Halton council for all but four years since it became a unitary council in 1997: she represented Heath ward from 1997 until 2015 when she lost her seat to Labour, but returned in 2019 and then transferred to Beechwood and Heath following boundary changes in 2021. Ratcliffe was mayor of Halton in 2013-14. Defending this seat for the Lib Dems is Diane Inch, who has regularly stood in Norton North ward in recent years. Reform UK have selected Neil Thornton, who has written a number of books on military history including a biography of local First World War hero Thomas "Todger" Jones VC, whose statue stands in this ward opposite the town's war memorial. Also on the ballot are independent candidate Dan Clarke who has stood in this ward on the Conservative, Liberal Party and Libertarian tickets in the last three local elections, Owen Ashworth for Labour, Ash Corcoran for Restore Britain and Luke Hanson for the Conservatives. Worksop East

Bassetlaw council, Nottinghamshire; caused by the resignation of Labour councillor Jo White. Our four remaining by-elections are all in territory in the English Midlands where Reform UK are involved in government - something which may well come as a bit of relief to Reform UK, whose progress in local by-elections has stalled a little in recent weeks. Three of these polls are in Nottinghamshire, where Reform UK currently have two MPs: Lee Anderson by election, and Robert Jenrick by defection. The other Nottinghamshire MPs are all Labour and they include Jo White, who gained the Bassetlaw constituency at the northern end of the county from the Conservatives in 2024. Her husband John Mann (now Lord Mann) had represented the same seat from 2001 until he was elevated to the peerage a few days before Parliament dissolved for the 2019 general election. The Bassetlaw constituency and local government district take their name from an ancient wapentake of Nottinghamshire. The largest town here is the former coalmining centre of Worksop, where Mann and White (who was not only his wife but also his office manager) had a very effective political machine going. Bassetlaw council has had a Labour majority continuously since 2011, and Jo White had been part of that council majority since May 2012. She first obtained elected office on the same day as her identical twin sister Deb Davies, who still serves as a Labour councillor in Newport. The list of previous election results below reflects that the council used the thirds electoral system in 2015 and also reflects that the ward map here is now very old, with the current boundaries of Worksop East ward dating from 2002. Jo White joined the council in 2012 by being elected for Worksop East ward, a very working-class segment of the town: in the 2021 census Worksop East made the top 70 wards in England and Wales for routine occupations, at 26.0% of adults. Although manufacturing remains important to the local economy the ward's largest employer is Bassetlaw Hospital, which is the main general hospital for northern Nottinghamshire. The ward boundary also takes in a large rural area around the village of Scofton in the Ryton valley to the east of Worksop, stretching as far as the A1 road: this is in fact part of Worksop parish. At the last census Worksop East was also in the top 40 wards in England and Wales for semi-detached housing (71.2% of households). One of those semis, at 5-7 Blyth Grove, is an Edwardian-era property which has been little altered since the Straw family moved in in the 1920s. On the owner's death in 1990 it was left - complete with its collection of clutter from the previous seven decades - to the National Trust, which has opened it to the public as Mr Straw's House. Pre-booking is required for visitors. Labour have not been seriously challenged here in Bassetlaw council elections for many years. The Conservatives didn't even bother to contest Worksop East ward until 2015, and they only finished second here for the first time in 2023 by dint of the ward being a straight Labour veruss Conservative fight that year. Labour held the three seats here in 2023 by a 72-28 margin, with Jo White going from bottom of the alphabetical ballot paper to top of the poll - something which is normally a sign of a strong personal vote. Second place to Labour here in previous local elections had gone to independent candidates up to 2012 (with the excepsion of 2004, when the independent won) and to UKIP in 2014, 2015 and 2019. So it probably shouldn't have been a surprise that Worksop became fertile territory for Reform UK when the Mann/White Labour machine here broke down in the face of the Starmer government's unpopularity. In the May 2025 Nottinghamshire county elections Reform UK won the Worksop East county division, which is larger than this ward, by over 20 percentage points. Reform UK also gained two Bassetlaw council by-elections on that date and they have won another two since, making them the main opposition to Labour on the council. Jo White had kept her Bassetlaw council seat following her election to Westmsinter in 2024, and she was likely intending to see out her council term until it was due to expire next year. However, once Andy Burnham became Prime Minister in July White joined the ranks of government as a junior minister in the Home Office, with responsibility for the ever-popular subject of migration and citizenship. She has now stood down from Bassetlaw council to concentrate on her Westminster frontbench role. Defending this seat for Labour is Cameron Holt, who is making his entry into adult politics after previously representing Bassetlaw in the Youth Parliament. The Conservative candidate is Ben Storey, who stood unsuccessfully in Worksop North ward in 2023. This time Worksop East will not be a straight fight, with interventions from Restore Britain candidate Anne Froggatt, the Greens' Anne Kean and Reform UK's Nancy Wright, who appears to be a first-time candidate. Nottinghamshire county council; and

Dover Beck

Newark and Sherwood council, Nottinghamshire; both caused by the death of Conservative councillor Roger Jackson. Our second Nottinghamshire preview this week covers two polls for the price of one, in the countryside between Nottingham and Newark. Here we find the market town of Southwell, which is home to one of England's more obscure cathedrals. Southwell Minster, which mostly dates from the 12th and 13th centuries, was promoted to a cathedral in 1884 with the creation of the diocese of Southwell: following later Church of England reorgnisations and a renaming in 2005, the cathredral is now the seat of the see of Southwell and Nottingham. The 12th and present Bishop of Southwell and Nottingham is Paul Williams, who was installed in 2015 and sits in the House of Lords as one of the 26 Lords Spiritual. Five of those places are reserved for the Archbishops of Canterbury and York and the Bishops of London, Durham and Winchester, with the remaining 21 Lords Spiritual normally being the remaining diocesan bishops with the longest service. Around the turn of this century, a newly-elected diocesan bishop could expect to wait about three or four years for their place on the red benches to open up. However, since 2015 transitional rules have been in force (for a period which has recently been extended to 2030) under which newly-elected women bishops go to the front of the queue whenever a vacancy opens up among the Lords Spiritual: the effect of this was that Williams had to wait until 2022 to take his place in the Lords chamber. The waiting time does seem to be down to around three or four years again now (the Bishop of Rochester is due to be introduced to the Lords today, and he was elected in 2022), but this might change in the future: there are rather a lot of vacant sees in the Church of England at the moment, with nine out of 42 dioceses (including London) currently without a bishop. Despite its cathedral Southwell has never had the status of a city, and its population of 7,491 makes it only a minor town within the Newark and Sherwood district. It's not even big enough for its own county council division: the Southwell division takes in eleven other parishes in the surrounding area. Six of these are combined into the Dover Beck district ward, named after the watercourse which mostly forms its southern boundary: the largest of these parishes is Gunthorpe, which lies on the River Trent and is the location of the only road bridge over the Trent between Newark and Nottingham. Its name has nothing to do with guns: Gunthorpe is a Norse name referring to a farm associated with a woman called Gunnhildr. The Southwell county division also takes in the large village of Lowdham, which lines on the railway line between Nottingham and Lincoln. The railway station here opened in 1846, and its early days the Midland Railway appear to have had trouble finding the right person to run it: Lowdham's first two stationmasters both ended up in prison for emblezzlement, while the third committed suicide. This area is divided between parliamentary constituencies. Dover Beck and Lowdham wards are included within the Sherwood Forest constituency, which (under its previous name of Sherwood) has voted for the government at every election since its creation in 1983. Its current MP is Labour backbencher Michelle Welsh, who also sits on Gedling council and would appear to be seeing out her term there: she defeated the former Conservative cabinet minister Sir Mark Spencer in 2024. Southwell, meanwhile, is part of the sprawling Newark constituency which has been represented since a 2014 by-election by another former Cabinet minister, Robert Jenrick; he defected to Reform UK earlier this year and is now the Treasury spokesman for his new party. The last Nottinghamshire county council elections happened while Jenrick was still in the Conservative Party and they suggest that his political machine was still in pretty good working order then: although Reform UK gained overall control of the county council, they didn't make much headway against the Tories in the Newark and Sherwood district. Southwell's Conservative county councillor Roger Jackson was re-elected fairly easily, polling 38% of the vote against 24% for Reform UK, 20% for the Liberal Democrats (Southwell town is the district's Lib Dem hotspot) and 13% for Labour. Roger Jackson, who died at the end of July at the age of 70, had first been elected to the county council in 2013 for Farnsfield and Lowdham division, transferring to Southwell in 2017 following boundary changes. He had been chairman of the county council in 2022-23, using his position to raise tens of thousands of pounds for the Nottingham Ukrainian Cultural Centre. Jackson had even longer service on Newark and Sherwood council, where he was first elected for Lowdham ward in 2003 and transferred to Dover Beck in 2015. Such was the Conservative strength in this corner of Nottinghamshire that in his 23 years on the district council Jackson only faced two contested elections, in 2019 and 2023: on the latter occasion he despatched the Labour candidate by 71-29 in a straight fight. Latterly he found himself in the opposition on Newark and Sherwood council, which at the time of writing is run by a Labour-Green coalition; but Jackson was still dialling into council meetings up until a week before he finally succumbed to cancer. The two by-elections to replace Roger Jackson have completely different candidate lists. Hie county council seat in Southwell is defended for the Conservatives by Penny Rainbow, who already sits on the district council for Southwell ward (which has rather different boundaries) and on Southwell town council. Reform UK have selected Edward Stubbs, who missed out on being elected to the county council by just one vote last year which he stood in Newstead division. (Don't let anybody ever tell you your vote will never change anything.) The Lib Dem candidate is Keith Melton who is the district councillor for Trent ward, which covers one of the parishes in this division (Upton); he last turned up in this column in July 2021 when he stood in a by-election in Bridge ward in Newark town. Labour have reselected Tracey Jevons-Hazzard who was their candidate here last year. Matthew Spoors for the Green Party completes a ballot paper of five. The smaller by-election in Dover Beck ward is defended for the Conservatives by Scott Carlton, who is trying to get back onto Newark and Sherwood council after losing his seat in Edwinstowe and Clipstone ward to Labour in 2023. Carlton is also a former county councillor, gaining Sherwood Forest division from Labour in 2021 and losing it to Reform UK in 2025. The Labour candidate is Kevin Scoles, who lives in Lowdham and stood in Lowdham ward in 2023. This by-election has as many candidates as the last three ordinary elections in Dover Beck put together, with interventions from the Lib Dems' Stephen Andersen, Reform UK's John Hart (who was last seen on a ballot paper in 2017 when he was a UKIP candidate for the county council) and the Greens' Lucy Spoors.

St James's

Dudley council, West Midlands; caused by the resignation of Reform UK councillor Ivor Robinson. We finish for the week in the heart of the industrial Black Country. Welcome to the town centre of Dudley, a place whose Wikipedia entry makes for unusually grim reading. In 1851, with the Industrial Revolution in full swing, a Board of Health inspector's report named Dudley as "the most unhealthy place in the country" with the town's water and sewerage supply having failed to keep up with its population growth. This has been sorted out, but in today's era we have a picture of a town centre which is clearly failing - partly the result of competition from the out-of-town Merry Hell shopping centre in nearby Brierley Hill. Even the church which gave its name to this ward - St James', Eve Hill - has recently been declared redundant, and divine service ceased there in 2024. If you think that's rude, that paragraph is actually a toned-down version of what I wrote when I first profiled St James's ward in February 2016: my Preview then included the word "decrepit". Perhaps this stung Dudley council and the other powers that be into action, because some regeneration work is currently going on in the town centre. Notably, a new branch of the Midland Metro tram network is nearly ready to open which will connect the town centre to the existing Metro line at Wednesbury; work is continuing on a further extension from Dudley to Merry Hell. The future Dudley Interchange tram stop lies within the boundary of this ward. St James's is a wedge-shaped ward, with the town centre at its eastern, pointed end. To the west is the Russells Hall estate which was mostly developed by the council in the 1950s and 1960s, as a replacement for slum housing nearer the town centre. The south-west corner of the ward is occupied by Russells Hall Hospital, the main NHS general hospital for Dudley borough. The ward as a whole is the seat of Dudley council, with Dudley Town Hall located on St James's Street. At the start of this century St James's ward of Dudley was a place where the Lib Dems were competitive. Indeed the winning candidate here in 2002 was Lorely Burt, who went on to serve as the Liberal Democrat MP for Solihull from 2005 to 2015 and now has a seat in the Lords. Burt's departure for Solihull appears to mark the end of the Lib Dem era here, as although the party won all three seats in St James's in 2004 (the first contest on the current boundaries) their vote fell away shortly afterwards. This column was first here for a by-election in February 2016 (Andrew's Previews 2016, page 48) at which I described this ward as "generally a tight Labour/UKIP marginal, although UKIP have only won the ward once (in 2008)". I had missed at the time that the then UKIP councillor for St James's, (Peter) Malcolm Davies, had previously been a Lib Dem councillor for the ward. You don't get many Lib Dem to UKIP defectors, but it takes all sorts to make a world. The main challenge to Labour here passed to the Conservatives in 2018, and they broke through in 2021 and 2022 to take two of the ward's three seats from Labour - on the second occasion by a margin of just seven votes, 1218 to 1211. Dudley Labour got their act together for the May 2023 election, holding their final seat in St James's by a 54-41 margin over the Conservatives. There was then a by-election in August 2023, at which Labour candidate Caroline Reid recovered one of the Conservative seats. Dudley council got a new ward map in May 2024, which was very much a minimum-change effort with the majority of wards - including St James's - seeing no change to their boundaries. Despite this an all-up election was required, at which Labour won all three seats. Two months later the ward went to the polls as part of the recreated Dudley parliamentary seat, which was won by physiotherapist and Labour candidate Sonia Kumar: she defeated the Conservatives' Marco Longhi, who had represented the predecessor seat of Dudley North. Marco Longhi is now in Reform UK which performed very well at the 2026 Dudley council elections, winning 21 wards out of a possible 24 - including Longhi himself in Upper Gornal and Woodsetton ward. This has left what was already a hung council even more hung than it was before. The latest composition has the Conservatives running a minority administration with 27 councillors, against 22 seats for Reform UK plus this vacancy, 15 Labour, 4 Lib Dems, 2 seats for the Black Country Party which is a Labour splinter group, and 1 recent defector from Reform UK to Restore Britain. In St James's ward the Reform UK candidate Ivor Robinson won very easily in May, polling 49% to just 22% for Labour and 14% for the Conservatives. Ivor Robinson tendered his resignation in September for personal reasons, triggering the third St James's ward by-election ten years. Defending this seat for Reform UK is Chris Chivers, who was one of the few unsuccessful Reform UK candidates for Dudley council in May when he contested St Thomas's ward. Labour have reselected Caroline Reid, who won the 2023 by-election here but lost her seat in May. The Conservatives' Sam Firmstone appears to be standing in his first election. The Greens' Katherine Poole and Restore Britain's Tristian Owens complete a list of five candidates.
