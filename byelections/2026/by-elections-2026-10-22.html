"All the right votes, but not necessarily in the right order"

Five by-elections on 22nd October 2026:

Saron

Carmarthenshire council, West Wales; caused by the death of Labour councillor Peter Cooper.

This week Labour are defending two seats in local by-elections, with the other three seats being previously held by the Conservatives, the Greens and an independent councillor. We're going to start and end the week with the Labour defences, the first of which is in rural Carmarthenshire.

The Saron ward covers a series of former coal-mining villages to the west of Ammanford, including Saron itself. This village is named after its Baptist chapel, which was founded here in the 1810s: the present chapel, however, dates from 1913. Also named after a chapel is Capel Hendre which is the largest centre of population in this ward: this is a pit village of quite recent vintage, with the Lindsay Colliery being sunk in 1962 and operating (latterly as part of Cwmgwili Colliery) into the 1990s. These days the colliery site is an industrial estate, and energy exports from Saron ward are cleaner thanks to the presence of several solar farms.

Saron ward previously featured in this column in July 2018: the result on that occasion was an easy hold for Plaid Cymru. In this century the ward's two seats on Carmarthenshire council had normally been split between Plaid Cymru and Labour councillor (Alan) Peter Cooper, who was first elected in 2004, lost his seat in 2017 and got it back in 2022. Cooper had also served this area as a magistrate, as secretary and director of the Ammanford Citizens Advice Bureau, and as chair of Llandybie community council which covers this area. This by-election is to replace him following his death in August.

Vote shares here at the last Carmarthenshire council election in 2022 were 40% for Plaid Cymru, 39% for Labour candidiate Cooper and 13% for an ex-Plaid independent who appeared on the ballot as "Nigel Evans (Pontyclerc)". The rather low number of surnames in rural Wales means that disambiguators like that are often necessary here, and a few candidate names like this turn up at every Carmarthenshire election. Ballot papers in other wards here in 2022 included Louvain (Midwife) Roberts and Anthony (Whitey) Davies, both of whom were elected, and unsuccessful independent Kim (Cross Hands) Thomas; while in previous years this century we have seen runs at office in Carmarthenshire from Rob Evans Paramedic, Dai (Disco) Hughes, Frederick (Fred the Garage) James, Audrey (Paper Shop) Jones, Chris (Corgi) Jones, John (Blacksmith) Wigley and Chris (The Bus) Wilson.

In Westminster elections this ward is part of a parliamentary seat which is perhaps most commonly known as Caerfyrddin, but for which the Parliamentary Constituencies Order 2023 gives the alternative English-language name "Carmarthen". Of Wales' 32 Westminster constituencies, 25 have different names in Welsh and English; Carmarthen is the only one where the Welsh-language name is listed first in the 2023 Order. The MP here since 2024 is first-term Plaid Cymru representative Ann Davies, who had been a Carmarthenshire councillor before her election to Westminster.

The Senedd boundaries introduced in 2026 avoid this problem by having names in the Welsh language only. In May the Sir Gaerfyrddin constituency returned three MSs each from Plaid Cymru and Reform UK. This seat helpfully has the same boundaries as Carmarthenshire council, which in 2022 returned 38 Plaid Cymru councillors - an overall majority of one - against 23 Labour and 14 independents. The Labour total is now down to 14 as a result of defections and two by-election losses to Reform UK. One of the Reform UK by-election winners, Carmelo Colasanto, was subsequently elected to the Senedd and had to resign his seat, prompting a second by-election in Llangennech ward which was won by Plaid Cymru.

So the defending Labour candidate in Saron has quite a lot of work to do to hold this seat: he is Anthony Jones, who was runner-up in 2022 in the neighbouring Ammanford ward where he lives. Plaid Cymru have selected Nicholas Davies, who already represents Saron on Llandybie community council and is now hoping to double up at county level. Owen Jenkins, who gives an address in Capel Hendre, is standing as an independent. Also on the ballot are Caryl Tandy for the Lib Dems, Richard Galvin for Reform UK and Craig Griffiths for the Conservatives.

Hartlebury

Wychavon council, Worcestershire; caused by the resignation of Conservative councillor Antony Hartley.

This week's Conservative defence is in the Worcestershire countryside. The village of Hartlebury lies on the main road between Worcester and Kidderminster, about four miles south of the latter. It has a fairly long history thanks to the presence of Hartlebury Castle, a mediaeval manor house which was the residence of the Bishops of Worcester from the 13th century all the way up to 2007; today the castle's state rooms are open to the public, while one wing houses the Worcestershire County Museum. Not far away is the nature reserve of Hartlebury Common, an area of heathland lying just outside the town of Stourport-on-Severn.

This area may look rural, but in fact there's a fair amount of history here. Next to Hartlebury railway station, which is half a mile to the east of the village on the Birmingham - Kidderminster - Worcester line, is a large industrial estate together with a brickworks that has been in operation since 1840. This is run by Wienerberger, an Austrian firm which claims to be the world's largest producer of bricks, but perhaps not for much longer. Reports last month suggest that Wienerberger are looking to close their Hartlebury site down, much to the dismay of the GMB union which has complained that the UK's construction firms are currently sitting on a stockpile of 500 million bricks - enough to build two Kidderminsters.

Meanwhile the northern boundary of this ward cuts through the large Summerfield site to the south of Kidderminster. This was originally a Second World War munitions factory, and since 1952 it has been a research centre for the development and testing of new types of rocket motors. Summerfield is now the British base of Roxel, an Anglo-French defence company.

Don't worry, this Preview is not rocket science - but that doesn't mean this Hartlebury by-election is necessarily easy to understand. We can fairly say that there is a long-running blue streak here. The ward is part of the large Droitwich and Evesham parliamentary seat represented by the Conservatives' Nigel Huddleston, who was first elected in 2015 for the predecessor seat of Mid Worcestershire. Huddleston never got beyond junior ministerial level while the Conservatives were in government, but he did make it into Badenoch's shadow cabinet in 2024 as co-chairman of the Conservative Party. In 2025 Huddleston was reshuffled to become shadow culture, media and sport secretary; in 2026 he was reshuffled to the backbenches.

The Droitwich and Evesham parliamentary seat has similar boundaries to the Wychavon local government district, which is one of the few second-tier districts where the Conservatives managed to keep their majority in the 2023 local elections. Hartlebury ward has been a true-blue area in Wychavon elections this century, and even in 2023 the Tories had no problem holding this ward with a 58-20 lead over the Greens.

Following the 2025 Worcestershire county council elections the Tory trifecta here remains intact. Hartlebury ward is part of the Ombersley county council division, which appeared in this column under different boundaries in August 2016; here the Conservatives held off a challenge from Reform UK last year by 44 votes, which meant that Reform UK finished two seats short of the 29 required for an overall majority. Since then Reform UK have lost one seat to the Lib Dems in a by-election, five more seats to defection and control of the county council: in May the Reform UK minority administration was deposed and replaced by a coalition of Conservative, Lib Dem, Green and independent councillors, under a Green leader.

So the Conservatives may well be confident of holding this by-election, which is to replace Antony Hartley who had served the ward since 2023. Hartley submitted his resignation from the council in August, just before he was due to be disqualified for not attending any meetings in six months.

This Hartlebury by-election has a ballot paper of just three candidates, which may reflect that it has flown under the radar a bit. Usually your columnist gets at least a month's notice of upcoming by-elections, but I didn't find out about this one until Monday of this week. That's very unusual. Anyway, the defending Conservative candidate is Tony Miller, who has been the county councillor for Ombersley since 2021 and now has the chance to double up at county and district level. Miller has sat on Wychavon council before, representing Lovett and North Claines ward from a December 2007 by-election until 2023 when he stood down. None of the parties which opposed the Conservatives here in 2023 (including the second-placed Greens) have returned, so Miller's opposition comes from Susan Evans for Labour and first-time Reform UK candidate Jake Richardson.

Hertford Kingsmead

East Hertfordshire council; caused by the resignation of Green Party councillor Vicky Glover-Ward.

In the database of the Local Elections Archive Project, which your columnist maintains in his increasingly disappearing spare time, the present version of Hertford Kingsmead is ward number 24601. (As of the latest update, the ward numbers go up to 26590.) This was, of course, the prisoner number of Jean Valjean in Victor Hugo's novel Les Misérables. No English-language rendering of this title has stuck, although previous translations of Les Misérables have been published under titles including "The Wretched Poor", "The Victims" and "The Dispossessed".

Not that all this has anything to do with life in a prosperous London commuter town. Kingsmead is the eastern of Hertford's four wards, taking in the railway terminus at Hertford East which has half-hourly trains to Liverpool Street in the City. The ward includes housing along the Ware Road, the Pinehurst area along the Stanstead Road, and industrial areas to the north of the railway line and along a modern distributor road to the south. There aren't many signs of the wretched poor or the dispossessed in the area's census return: Kingsmead ward has high levels of full-time employment, particularly in professional and managerial roles.

The King's Meads of the name are a series of watermeadows in the north of the ward, next to the Lee Valley Navigation, which are now maintained as a nature reserve. This area also marks the start of the New River, a 20-mile long aqueduct which was opened in 1613 to supply drinking water to London: it still fulfils that function today for Thames Water.

Hertford gives its name to a county and it is still the location of Hertfordshire county council, but it's fair to say that this is not the pre-eminent economic centre north of London. It's not even the largest town in East Hertfordshire district: that's Bishop's Stortford, so the name of the Hertford and Stortford parliamentary seat should probably be the other way round to reflect its population distribution. This was a very safe Conservative seat until it fell to Labour in the 2024 landslide. The local Labour MP Josh Dean, who was previously a Hertford town councillor in 2023-24, is still in his mid-20s, and he was elected to the Commons before he had finished his degree in politics and international relations at the University of Westminster.

For some indication of just how true-blue this area was before the Conservative vote here fell apart, in the 2015 local elections the Conservative Party won a perfect 50 out of 50 seats on East Hertfordshire council. From there the only way was down, and the Conservatives have certainly fallen a long way from those heights: the Tories not only lost control of East Hertfordshire in 2023 but were beaten into second place by the Green Party, who formed a coalition with the Lib Dems to run the council. The councillors returned in 2023 were 19 Greens, 16 Conservatives, 10 Lib Dems and 5 Labour.

The Green surge included Hertford Kingsmead ward, which was expanded from two councillors to three in 2023 following boundary changes: shares of the vote here three years ago were 37% for the Green slate, 26% for the Conservatives and 21% for the Liberal Democrats. Kingsmead had voted Conservative at every previous local election this century. At county council level the ward is divided between the Hertford All Saints and Hertford St Andrews divisions: in 2025 All Saints was an easy Green hold, while the Greens also narrowly gained St Andrews from the Conservatives.

Now the Green Party will have to turn from attack to defence following the resignation in September of Vicky Glover-Ward, who had been the council's cabinet member for planning. Her resignation letter attacked proposed government changes to planning policy.

The Green defence of the resulting Hertford Kingsmead by-election falls to Drew Waller-Downes, a Hertford town councillor who works in data science. The Conservative candidate is Rosemary Bolton, who previously represented this ward from 2019 to 2023: she lost her seat here three years ago, and wants it back. Also returning from 2023 is the Lib Dems' Elif Lewis: she is described as a Turkish-born acrylic painter and ceramicist. The Labour candidate is Dawn Newell, and Reform UK have selected Christopher Bush who was unsuccessful in a Hertfordshire county council by-election in May.

Grantham St Wulfram's

South Kesteven council, Lincolnshire; caused by the resignation of independent councillor Tim Harrison.

This week's independent defence is just off the Great North Road, in Grantham. St Wulfram's ward covers Grantham town centre and the north of the town along the road towards Manthorpe. It takes its name from the town's parish church, whose spire rises to a height of 84 metres; only five churches in the country have taller spires. (They are Salisbury Cathedral, as any Russian spy knows, Norwich and Old Coventry cathedrals, the Catholic St Walburge's church in Preston, and St James' church in Louth.) The namesake St Wulfram was a Frenchman who served in the late 7th century as archbishop of Sens, a city to the south-east of Paris. Long after his death Wulfram's arm ended up in Grantham as a holy relic, and the church housed a shrine to him, but both the shrine and the arm disappeared at some point during the Reformation.

Grantham is a town with a lot of history, notably in the form of the King's School. This is a grammar school which is still going today, and its former pupils include the great scientist Isaac Newton who was educated here during the late 1650s. A signature attributed to Newton is carved into the school wall.

Isaac Newton was from a village in the area, but another very consequential scientist - and Conservative politician - comes from the town-centre Grantham St Wulfram's ward. On 13th October 1925, Grantham alderman Alfred Roberts and his wife Beatrice had a baby girl called Margaret, who was born in the family shop on the corner of North Parade and Broad Street. Under her married surname of Thatcher, Margaret went on to serve as Prime Minister for over a decade and transform the UK.

Grantham has recently taken delivery of a statue of Margaret Thatcher, which was erected in the town in May 2022 after Westminster council refused permission for it to stand in Parliament Square. Westminster had worried that a Thatcher statue would be a focus for civil disobedience and vandalism, and their fears would appear to have been well-founded given that the Grantham statue has been subjected to repeated attacks. We're not talking vandalism on the scale of the Gävle Goat here, but the council have had to put the Bronze Lady under CCTV surveillance.

Grantham is the home of South Kesteven council, and Mrs Thatcher might well be spinning in her grave given what happened here in the May 2023 local elections. This true-blue corner of England now has a council under no overall control; the seats split 24 to the Conservatives, 22 to independent candidates, 4 to the Green Party, 4 to the Lib Dems and 2 to Labour. The independent councillors are split between four groups on the council, but the Tories couldn't peel enough of them off to keep control; a coalition has been formed to run the council between two of the independent groups, the Greens and the Lib Dems, under an independent leader.

St Wulfram's ward is now holding its third by-election in four years. In November 2022 the Conservatives successfully defended a seat here (Andrew's Previews 2022, page 496); however, the Tory councillor elected then, Mary Whittington, lost her seat in May 2023 to independent candidate Tim Harrison who had finished second in the by-election. Shares of the vote in were 41% for Harrison, 37% for the Conservatives and 22% for Labour.

The ward's remaining Conservative councillor Ray Wootten then died three months later at the age of 71, forcing another by-election in November 2023. This was also a Conervative hold, although with a distinctly unimpressive vote share: the new Conservative candidate Matt Bailey won with 34% against 22% for independent candidate Susan Swinburn, 16% for Labour and 14% for the Greens.

Boundary changes for the 2024 general election united this ward within the Grantham and Bourne parliamentary seat. This re-elected its Conservative MP Gareth Davies, who had represented the predecessor seat of Grantham and Stamford since 2019 and was a junior Treasury minister at the time of the general election. Davies has stayed on the Conservative frontbench in opposition, abd he currently shadows both Treasury and business roles.

In the 2025 county elections Reform UK won an overall majority in Lincolnshire, but they failed to crack the two county divisions which cover St Wulfram's ward. Most of the ward is within Grantham North, which also takes in a large rural area outside the town and where the Conservatives held off Reform UK by 79 votes; the Tories held on to Grantham West more comfortably.

Having held a seat in the 2023 by-election, the Conservatives now have the chance for a gain at the 2026 Grantham St Wulfram's by-election. This was triggered in July by the resignation of independent councillor Tim Harrison, who was first elected here in 2023; the following year he became the first chairman of the newly-created Grantham town council. He stepped down from both Grantham town council and South Kesteven council in July for personal reasons.

Two new independent candidates have come forward to replace Harrison. Wayne Hasnip, who already represents most of this area on Grantham town council, was an independent candidate in Grantham North at the 2025 county elections; before then he was in the Labour Party, and he was an unsuccessful Labour candidate for this ward in 2019. Marie Reid is the town council chairman and is plugged into a large number of local community networks, including presenting a show on the town's local radio station. The Conservative candidate Ben Lavender has recently completed his degree in British politics and legislative studies at one of the great universities (Oxford, Cambridge, Hull); during his studies he was a candidate for Hull city council in May this year and previously in 2024. Associated with a different educational institution is Labour candidate Tim Galsworthy, a historian who lectures in history and military history at Lincoln Bishop University. The Green Party's Ian Simmons is contesting Grantham St Wulfram's ward for the third time, after finishing as a rather distant runner-up in 2019 and placing fourth and last in the 2022 by-election. And a ballot paper of six candidates is completed by Reform UK's Andy McKinlay, who has run an IT business in the town for over 30 years.

Calverley and Farsley

Leeds council, West Yorkshire; caused by the resignation of Labour councillor Peter Carlill.

Our final by-election comes in a corner of Yorkshire which has turned into a hard-fought marginal ward in recent years. The attractive commuter village of Calverley can be found overlooking the Aire valley to the north-west of Leeds, on the road between Leeds and Shipley. Nearby Farsley, meanwhile, marks the western end of Leeds' built-up area.

Calverley's parish church, dedicated to St Wilfrid, is the final resting place of Alfred Blunt. He was Bishop of Bradford when he gave a speech to his diocesan conference on 1st December 1936 criticising Edward VIII, saying of the King's need of divine grace: "We hope that he is aware of his need. Some of us wish that he gave more positive signs of his awareness." Blunt's speech, which was reported by a journalist from the Bradford Telegraph and Argus, became front-page news and set off the final stages of the political and constitutional crisis that led to the King's abdication ten days later.

Farsley was a more industrial area centred on the wool trade, and textiles remain important to the local economy. The Sunny Bank Mills complex, which is still owned by the Gaunt family which once ran it as a worsted mill, is now in use as an arts centre and textile archive: since 2022 it has also been the filming location for the Great British Sewing Bee.

Calverley and Farsley were part of the borough of Pudsey before they were incorporated into Leeds in 1974, and at the southern end of the ward we find the events space of Pudsey Civic Hall, Pudsey police station and New Pudsey railway station, which lies on the line between Leeds and Bradford Interchange. (The name of New Pudsey station comes from the fact that it was opened by British Rail in 1967 to replace two earlier Pudsey stations on lines which were closed by Beeching.) Until recently Lloyds Banking Group had a large call centre next to the civic hall, but Leeds council have recently approved a plan to redevelop the call centre site for housing. The nearby road junction at Dawson's Corner, where the Leeds outer ring road meets the A647 road from Bradford, is already being rebuilt to handle the extra traffic.

Pudsey has been on the Parliamentary map continuously since 1885, but there were major boundary changes here in 2024 which broke up the bellwether Pudsey constituency that had existed since 1950. Pudsey proper - including Calverley and Farsley ward - was effectively merged with the safe Labour seat of Leeds West to create a notionally-Labour constituency of Leeds West and Pudsey, a move which left the outgoing Conservative MP for Pudsey Stuart Andrew with little option but to do the chicken run: he now represents Daventry in Northamptonshire. This move left Leeds West and Pudsey clear for the previous Leeds West MP Rachel Reeves, who was Chancellor of the Exchequer throughout the Starmer premiership but is now on the backbenches. Reeves, who has been an MP since 2010, had originally come to Leeds in 2006 to work for HBOS.

Leeds city council has been run by Labour since 2010, although not always with a majority. The council fell into no overall control following the 2026 election, with a minority Labour administration presently holding 46 seats plus this vacancy against 14 Conservatives, 10 Greens, 8 Reform UK councillors, 6 Lib Dems, 3 councillors for the continuing SDP and 11 independents of various stripes. Leeds is the last Labour-run council in West Yorkshire: following the 2026 elections Reform UK hold overall majorities in Wakefield and Calderdale, and they run minority administrations in Bradford and Kirklees.

Since the last Leeds council boundary review in 2018, Calverley and Farsley has been closely fought between Labour and the Conservatives. The outgoing Labour councillor Peter Carlill was first elected in 2018 and re-elected in 2019 and 2023, and Labour gained a second seat in the ward in 2024.

However, the 2026 election here was an easy win for long-serving Conservative councillor Andrew Carter CBE, who is one of the very few remaining councillors whose service goes back before the 1970s reorganisation. Carter was first elected to Pudsey borough council all the way back in 1971, and he has represented Calverley and Farsley ward on the modern Leeds council - and the predecessor ward of Pudsey North - continuously since 1973. He was leader of the Conservative group on Leeds city council from 1983 to 2023, and served as joint leader of the council while Leeds was run by a Conservative and Lib Dem coalition between 2004 and 2010. At his last re-election five months ago Carter polled 37% of the vote, against 27% for Labour, 17% for Reform UK and 16% for the Greens.

The Conservatives will now be hopeful of a second gain after Peter Carlill resigned in September on health grounds. The defending Labour candidate is Kathryn Penny, who was runner-up here in May: on my research trip she was the only candidate whose lamppost posters I saw. The Conservatives have put up Amanda Carter, who is Andrew Carter's wife and is also a long-serving former councillor for this ward: she was first elected for Pudsey North ward in 1999, transferred to Calverley and Farsley ward on its creation in 2004, resigned in 2008, returned to the council in 2015, lost her seat to Labour in 2024 and is now seeking to return again. The Reform UK candidate is Craig Gabbitas, who stood in May on the far side of the city in Adel and Wharfedale ward. The Greens' Daisy Gilpin, who works in sales and has an allotment in Calverley, is fighting her first election campaign. A ballot paper of seven candidates is completed by Stuart McLeod for the Liberal Democrats, Bob Buxton for the Yorkshire Party and Bradley Holmes for Restore Britain.
