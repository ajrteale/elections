"All the right votes, but not necessarily in the right order"

One by-election on Wednesday 23rd September 2026:

Cranbrook, Sissinghurst and Frittenden

Tunbridge Wells council, Kent; caused by the resignation of Tunbridge Wells Alliance councillor Nancy Warne.

It's the last by-election week of September and we're going to start early, with an unusual Wednesday poll taking place in the countryside of Kent. The small town of Cranbrook lies in the High Weald, roughly halfway between Maidstone to the north and Hastings to the south, and like much of the High Weald it was a centre for industry: in mediaeval times both clothmaking and ironworking were carried out here. The presence of other industries can be seen from the presence of the Union Mill, which dates from 1814 and is described as the UK's tallest surviving smock windmill; its curious name comes from the fact that in 1819 it fell into the hands of a union of the original owner’s creditors.

Kent's ironstone reserves had already been exhausted by the time of the Industrial Revolution, and Cranbrook consequently never grew into a great urban centre. In fact, Cranbrook's parish is sometimes cited as the smallest town in Kent worthy of the name, with a population of 6,717 in the 2011 census. (The actual smallest town in Kent is Fordwich to the north-east of Canterbury, which with a population of under 400 is the smallest community with a town council not just in Kent but in the whole of England.) But this number of 6,717 also includes contributions from a number of other villages which are part of the parish, including Sissinghurst - which in recent years has been added to the parish's name.

Sissinghurst is best known for Sissinghurst Castle and its extensive garden, which was created after the castle and its grounds were bought in 1930 by the writer Vita Sackville-West. She lived here until her death in 1962 alongside her husband Sir Harold Nicolson, a former diplomat who also served one term in the House of Commons: he was the National Labour MP for Leicester West in the extended 1935-45 Parliament. Sir Harold donated Sissinghurst Castle and its gardens to the National Trust in 1967, and this is now one of the National Trust's most-visited properties.

The third village named in the ward is Frittenden, which can be found on lower ground to the north-east of Sissinghurst and forms a parish of its own. This is one of a number of UK locations which were said to be the location of treacle mines, although it has to be said that no evidence of these mines exists on the surface. Extensive research has also failed to find any record of any Frittenden branch of the National Union of Mineworkers.

Cranbrook gives its name to a hymn tune, which was written in 1805 by one Thomas Clark who was a shoemaker from Canterbury. This was once a very popular tune for the Christmas hymn While shepherds watched their flocks by night: here is a recording of Cranbrook with those lyrics sung by the choir of Ely Cathedral. In local government Cranbrook, Sissinghurst and Frittenden are administered from Tunbridge Wells, some distance to the west of here. Tunbridge Wells council is run by the Liberal Democrats, but this rural corner of the district is a weak area for them - the last time the Lib Dems won a district council seat in this area was in 2010 when they carried the predecessor ward of Benenden and Cranbrook. Otherwise this area has generally voted Conservative, but this pattern was broken in 2019 when Benenden and Cranbrook was gained by Nancy Warne of the localist Tunbridge Wells Alliance. This party is not to be confused with the Independents for Tunbridge Wells, in the same way that it would be inadvisable to get the Judean Peoples Front and the Peoples Front of Judea mixed up. Warne was re-elected for a second term of office in 2023.

The present ward was created in 2024 and its first election returned two Conservative councillors and Nancy Warne, who was elected in second place giving her a three-year term of office. She quickly found herself wearing the mayoral chain of office, as mayor of Tunbridge Wells for 2024-25. In May the Conservatives easily defended one of their seats in this ward, polling 46% to 25% for Reform UK and 14% for the Greens.

The Conservatives also represent this ward at other levels of government, although only just in the case of the county council: last year the Conservatives successfully defended the wider Cranbrook county division just 17 votes ahead of the Reform UK candidate. The local MP here is first-term Conservative Katie Lam, who was elected for the Weald of Kent seat in 2024 and was promoted to the shadow cabinet last month as shadow housing, communities and local government secretary.

Nancy Warne resigned from the council last month and the Tunbridge Wells Alliance are not defending the resulting by-election. We have a free-for-all! The Conservatives will fancy their chances of a gain here, and they have selected Nicholas Lees. For this by-election Reform UK have recruited David Somers, who was elected here in third place as a Conservative candidate in 2024 but didn't seek re-election to the council when his two-year term ended in May. The Lib Dem candidate is first-timer Harry Wormington. Max Chesters completes an all-male ballot for the Green Party.
