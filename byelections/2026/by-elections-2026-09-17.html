"All the right votes, but not necessarily in the right order"
Three by-elections on 17th September 2026:

Avondale and Stonehouse

South Lanarkshire council, Scotland; caused by the death of independent councillor Margaret Cooper.

This week's set of three by-elections is rather outside the political mainstream, with one independent seat up for election in Scotland and the Green Party defending two seats south of the Border. So maybe we will learn something from this week's results about parts of our politics which get overlooked by the national polling. Or maybe we won't. Local by-elections can be like that sometimes.

The Scottish seat up for election today is in a rural corner of Lanarkshire. The Avondale in the ward name refers to the upper valley of the Avon Water, which rises in the hills of southern Lanarkshire, flows north-east to Larkhall and then turns north, passing through a deep gorge at Chatelherault before entering the Clyde between Hamilton and Motherwell.

The largest settlement in the Avon Water's upper valley is Strathaven, which is a pronunciation trap for those who follow British elections: it's pronounced STRAYven. The home in later life of the music hall entertainer Sir Harry Lauder, Strathaven is an old market town built around the ruin of Strathaven Castle, which like much else in this general corner of Lanarkshire ended up in the hands of the Dukes of Hamilton.

The 13th Duke of Hamilton lived at a hunting lodge further up the valley at Dungavel, which was the intended destination for Rudolf Hess' ill-fated peace mission in 1941: Hess was trying to get into contact with the 14th Duke. After the war the Hamiltons sold Dungavel to the National Coal Board, and it subsequently became a prison before taking on its current role in 2001 as Scotland's immigration detention centre.

One local Hamilton who was not associated with the ducal family was Alexander Hamilton, a businessman who had made his name and fortune by manufacturing carpets. He was originally from the textile town of Stonehouse further down the Avon Water, which has not forgotten the connection: the Alexander Hamilton Memorial Park continues to look out over the Avon valley to the north of the town. It has a fine bandstand.

Stonehouse has a population of between 5,000 and 6,000. It could have been more. In 1973 Stonehouse was designated as a New Town to take Glasgow overspill, with responsibility handed to the East Kilbride Development Corporation. (Avondale and Stonehouse ward does include a couple of areas on the southern fringe of East Kilbride which have spilled over the boundary.) But this designation was cancelled four years later with very little progress having been made. All that was ever built of Stonehouse New Town were 96 houses on Murray Drive on the southern edge of the town together with a piece of public art - the Stonehouse Elephants, which on my visit last year had been painted in bright colours. The Murray Drive development as a whole, however, turned out to be a white elephant.

During my visit to Murray Drive last year I was chased all the way around this estate by a Labour Party loudspeaker car. Stonehouse is one of the three towns in the Hamilton, Larkhall and Stonehouse Holyrood constituency, whose SNP constituency MSP Christina McKelvie died in March 2025; the resulting June 2025 by-election was gained by the Labour candidate Davy Russell. However, Russell failed to secure re-election in May's Holyrood election at which Hamilton, Larkhall and Stonehouse was won by the SNP's national secretary Alex Kerr. Kerr is also seeing out the final year of his term on Glasgow city council, where he was elected in 2022 for Baillieston ward.

For Holyrood purposes the rest of Avondale and Stonehouse ward is included in the Clydesdale constituency, which has been represented since 2021 by high-profile SNP constituency MSP Màiri McAllan. She has served in the Scottish Cabinet since 2023 with a break for maternity leave in 2024-25, and following May's Scottish Parliament election McAllan took on the education, culture and Gaelic portfolio.

The Westminster constituency map treats this ward very differently. Avondale and Stonehouse is the Strathaven part of the East Kilbride and Strathaven constituency represented by first-term Labour MP Joani Reid, granddaughter of the Red Clydeside trade union leader Jimmy Reid. She served for eight years on Lewisham council in London before being elected here in 2024 by a comfortable margin over the SNP. Reid resigned the Labour whip earlier this year after her husband was arrested in March on suspicion of spying for China, but she was readmitted to the party after Andy Burnham took over in July.

Avondale and Stonehouse ward was created in 2007 and was reduced from four seats to three in 2017 as a result of boundary changes. So, when the first preferences here in 2022 split evenly four ways - 28% for the SNP, 24% for outgoing Labour councillor Margaret Cooper seeking re-election as an independent, 21% for the Conservatives and 20% for Labour - someone was going to lose out. Cooper was first to reach the quota of 1,718, the SNP's Elise Frame won the second seat after her running-mate was eliminated, and the SNP surplus then gave the final seat to Labour's Susan Kerr who defeated the Conservatives' Gary Burns by 1,619 votes to 1,462. Compared with the 2017 result, this is an independent gain from the Conservatives.

South Lanarkshire council has a majority for unionist parties overall, and Labour - as the largest unionist party - run the council as a minority. However, following the 2022 election the title of Provost - equivalent to the mayor of an English council, but with a longer term of office and more of an expectation of political leadership - went to Margaret Cooper as leader of the council's independent group. Cooper, a retired teacher who died in June following a long illness, had been first elected for Avondale and Stonehouse ward in 2012.

Margaret Cooper's legacy includes an annual charitable appeal which has raised more than £100,000 to date, and the erection of a statue in Strathaven to Rifleman Khan, a German Shepherd dog who had been awarded the Dickin Medal for rescuing his handler Lance-Corporal Jimmy Muldoon from drowning during the 1944 attack on Walcheren in the Netherlands. Muldoon and Khan lived together in Strathaven after the war ended.

Cooper would have easily won a single-seat count in Avondale and Stonehouse in 2022: after transfers she would have enjoyed a lead of 57-43 over the SNP's Elise Frame in the final reckoning. If we redistribute Cooper's votes, the beneficiary would be Labour's Susan Kerr with a narrower two-candidate preferred score of 52.2-47.8 over Frame. However, May 2022 is a long time ago - as we saw at the last South Lanarkshire by-election in neighbouring Larkhall two months ago, which was won by Reform UK.

One new independent candidate has come forward to succeed Cooper: he is Andrew Forde, who appears to be an IT professional from Hamilton. The SNP have reselected Leigh Payne, who was their second candidate here in 2022 and was runner-up to Reform UK at the Larkhall by-election in July. Labour have nominated first-time candidate Hannah Miller. Standing for the Conservatives is Ade Aibinu, a doctor of molecular pharmacology and lecturer who gives an address in this ward but has previously stood in Glasgow local elections, for Westminster in Glasgow North West in 2019 and for Holyrood in Glasgow Anniesland in 2021. Completing a list of nine candidates are Michael Grant for the Greens, Gary Allan in a rare local by-election outing for Your Party, David Glennie for the Lib Dems, Reform UK's George Hobbins who was their Holyrood candidate for Uddingston and Bellshill in May, and Ken Thomson of the Alliance for Democracy and Freedom who will not be helped by giving an address in East Ayrshire on his nomination papers. This is a Scottish local election, so Votes at 16 and the Alternative Vote apply.

Walkley

Sheffield council, South Yorkshire; caused by the resignation of Green Party councillor Andy Davies.

We now travel south to Yorkshire for the second of two recent by-elections in the city of Sheffield. Last month Reform UK took a seat off Labour in Southey ward in the north of the city; but this by-election promises to be very different.

Walkley ward covers a rather hilly area to the north-west of Sheffield city centre, with Sheffield's north-western tramline generally forming the northern boundary of this ward. Much of the housing here dates from the Victorian era and was erected by the Freedom Land Society with later terracing filling in the gaps. To the south of here are the suburbs of Upperthorpe and Netherthorpe and a corner of the city centre, including the fast-regenerating St Vincent's Quarter and rather a lot of student flats: this gives Walkley ward a very unusual demographic for Yorkshire, although the new academic year's university students won't have arrived here yet.

Much of Netherthorpe's Victorian housing was cleared and replaced by towerblocks in the 1960s, creating an area of open space which became known to the local kids as "The Ponderosa", after the ranch in Bonanza. This now officially its name, although Sheffield council haven't officially designated the Ponderosa as a park. The city council do, however, run Ruskin Park to the north: this is named after the Victorian polymath John Ruskin, who founded a museum in Walkley to display his collection of art and scientific items. The museum moved away from Walkley in 1890, and after some wandering Ruskin's collection is now displayed in the Millennium Galleries in the city centre.

Running steeply downhill from the southern end of Ruskin Park is Blake Street, which was a filming location for The Full Monty some years before it was nominated in a March 2017 BBC article on England's steepest residential streets (link). The Ordnance Survey measured Blake Street as having an angle of 16.6° (or, to convert this into road sign units, 29.8% or 1 in 3.35). For their story the BBC interviewed a local resident:

"The fittingly named Olivia Blake, who moved here a year ago, said she had already come to the aid of three or four people whose cars had become stuck on the snow-covered hill.

'Once a parked car rolled down the hill and into the railings on the other side,' she said."

The Olivia Blake quoted here is now the MP for Sheffield Hallam, having been elected to that seat in 2019, and she was a local councillor for Walkley ward from 2014 until she stood down following her election to Parliament. The COVID-19 pandemic meant that her council seat was left vacant until May 2021, when it was gained by the Green Party who have vied with Labour for the three seats in Walkley ward over the last decade. Three elections here during that period have been decided by fewer than 100 votes - in 2019, 2022 and 2026.

The Labour-Green local battle here is typical of the wider constituency of Sheffield Central. This seat is represented by first-term Labour MP Abtisam Mohamed, who is originally from the Yemen but has lived in Sheffield since she was two years old: her father and grandfather worked in the city's steel industry. Mohamed defeated Eddie Izzard for the 2024 Labour nomination here.

Labour have normally had the better of the recent contests in Walkley ward and they easily gained the Green seat in 2024. But the councillor up for re-election here in May 2026 was the Labour council leader Tom Hunt, who lost his seat to the Greens' Andy Davies by just 73 votes. Davies' winning vote share was 40%, with Hunt on 39% and Reform UK best of the rest on 14%. The post of council leader in Sheffield is not as strong as it is in most other cities because the council here runs on the committee system, but this was still a very embarrassing result for Labour: this and other losses have left the city council very balanced, with significant council groups for Labour, the Lib Dems, the Greens and Reform UK in that order.

The new Walkley councillor Andy Davies handed in his notice just three months after his election, citing his mental health and associated personal reasons. Defending a difficult resulting by-election for the Green Party is Seb van Bilsen Irias, a data analytics manager who is originally from Latin America and studied in France and Italy before ending up in Sheffield. Tom Hunt is not trying to get his seat back: instead Labour have selected Hannah Cawley, who finished as a rather more distant runner-up to the Greens in Burngreave ward in May. The Reform UK candidate Andrew Telford, a Royal Corps of Signals veteran who now runs his own business, was also an unsuccessful candidate in May when he contested Fulwood ward. Also on the ballot are Alex Purvis for the Lib Dems who were competitive in this ward in the pre-Coalition years, Sonny Ullah for the Conservatives and Isabelle France for the Trade Unionist and Socialist Coalition.

Wreake Valley

Charnwood council, Leicestershire; caused by the resignation of Green Party councillor Laurie Needham.

Today's other Green Party defence is in the very different territory of rural Leicestershire, just off the northern corner of Leicester's built-up area. The Wreake Valley ward covers a small part of the Leicester suburb of Syston plus seven parishes located to the north and east of Syston, most of which are in the valley of the eponymous River Wreake. This river flows in a southwesterly direction from Melton Mowbray, before merging into the Soar west of Syston. The valley is followed by the main road and railway line from Leicester to Melton, while the rather more ancient route of the Fosse Way - and its modern successor, the A46 dual carriageway - runs in a straight line northwards towards Newark.

The largest settlement here is the village of Queniborough, with its picture-postcard thatched cottages on the main street. Queniborough's parish church, dedicated to St Mary, is described by Pevsner as having "one of the finest spires in the whole of Leicestershire". No doubt Russian spies will have taken note. Not far away is the village of East Goscote, which takes its name from an ancient Hundred of Leicestershire but is of a much newer vintage: East Goscote was built in the 1960s and 1970s, on land which had been requisitioned by the Ministry of Defence during the war for use as an ordnance factory.

Until 2023 East Goscote was a Charnwood ward of its own, and in the 2021 census it made the top 60 wards in England and Wales for residents with apprenticeship qualifications (8.9%). The ward had a bizarre electoral record. In 2007 and 2011 East Goscote returned a British National Party councillor, Catherine Duffy, who lost her seat to the Conservatives in 2015 but put up a strong fight in doing so. Then in 2019 the Conservatives lost East Goscote to Laurie Needham, who became Charnwood's first elected councillor from the Green Party.

This was completely different electoral behaviour to the other predecessor wards of Queniborough and Wreake Villages, which had more of a commuter demographic and both of which were Conservative bankers (as discussed in this column when we covered a by-election in Wreake Villages in September 2013). But in the 2023 Charnwood elections Laurie Needham clearly had enough of a personal vote not only to overcome these disadvantageous boundary changes but to get both of her Green running-mates over the line as well: vote shares were 51% for the Green slate, with Needham topping the poll, and 40% for the Conservatives. This and other losses meant that the Conservatives lost control of Charnwood council in 2023: the Greens found themselves holding the balance of power, and they put Labour in with a minority administration.

Syston joined the parliamentary map in 2024 with the creation of the Melton and Syston constituency. This new seat safely returned the Conservatives' Edward Argar, who had previously represented this area from 2015 as part of the Charnwood constituency. Argar had a long frontbench career in the last Conservative government, which peaked during the short-lived Truss administration in which he sat in Cabinet as Paymaster General and minister for the Cabinet Office.

The Conservatives also represent this area at county council level, in the form of the Syston Fosse county division which, unusually, is smaller than this district ward. Wreake Valley ward also takes in the parish of South Croxton which is part of the Thurmaston Ridgemere county division: that seat is also Conservative-held. Eastern Leicestershire in general was an area where Reform UK made very little headway last year.

Laurie Needham stepped down from Charnwood council last month after seven years in office, citing changes in her personal circumstances and ongoing health issues. Defending the resulting by-election for the Greens is Hamzah Sheikh, who is a trustee of the Charnwood branch of Citizens Advice. The Conservatives have reselected James Poland, who has been the county councillor for Syston Fosse since 2017 and is trying to get his seat back after being defeated here in 2023: Poland had previously served for Wreake Villages ward since winning the 2013 by-election. Jay Dyas stands for Labour, the Lib Dem candidate is Murray Sinclair and the Reform UK candidate is Michael Slade, who gives an address in Queniborough and is retired from a long career in professional sales.
