Bray

Windsor and Maidenhead council, Berkshire; caused by the death of Conservative councillor Leo Walters.

"In good King Charles's golden days,
When loyalty no harm meant;
A zealous High Churchman was I,
And so I got preferment.
To teach my flock I never missed,
Kings were by God appointed,
And damned are those who do resist,
Or touch the Lord's anointed.

And this is law, I will maintain
Until my dying day, Sir,
That whatsoever King shall reign,
Still I'll be the Vicar of Bray, Sir!"

Satire goes back a long way. The song The Vicar of Bray dates from the eighteenth century, as we shall see from its lyrics which describe how the titular vicar somehow managed to keep his job through the turbulent years of the UK's wildly changing religious politics in the late 17th century. Helpfully for the song there was a real-life long-serving vicar of Bray during this period: Francis Carswell took the job on in 1667, during the reign of Charles II, and remained as the parish's incumbent for 42 years until his dying day in 1709 - when Queen Anne was on the throne.

But even before Carswell took over, the living of Bray had a reputation for attracting religious turncoats. Thomas Fuller's 1662 book The History of the Worthies of England remarked that "The Vicar of Bray, will be the Vicar of Bray still". Fuller was referring to the parish's priests during the even wilder religious ride of the House of Tudor, in which the state religion lurched between Catholicism and Protestantism during the reigns of Henry VIII (Catholic), Edward VI (Protestant), Mary I (Catholic) and Elizabeth I (Protestant).

Vicars of Bray through the centuries have been unusually prominent on the religious frontline for geographical reasons. The village of Bray lies in Berkshire, on the south bank of the River Thames just a few miles upstream from the royal residence of Windsor. Indeed, the modern town of Windsor has spilled over the eastern edge of Bray parish - although this area is excluded from the present Bray ward. The ward boundaries do take in the north-west corner of Windsor Great Park, together with most of Legoland Windsor's car park.

Bray parish covers quite a large area between Windsor and Maidenhead, but the old riverside village of Bray itself is quite small. Most of the ward's population lives along the Windsor Road in the Bray Wick and Holyport areas, while other hamlets here include Braywoodside, Fifield and Oakley Green. Functionally the ward is a southern suburb of Maidenhead.

Today the village of Bray is probably best known for fine dining. The 2025 Michelin guide lists ten UK restaurants with the maximum three Michelin stars, and two of them are in Bray. First to open in 1972 was the Waterside Inn, which was founded by Michel and Albert Roux and is now run by Michel's son Alain; it has retained its three stars continuously since 1985. Expensive fare of a different style can be found at the Fat Duck, where Heston Blumenthal has been experimenting with "molecular gastronomy" since 1995; the Fat Duck has been on the 3-star list every year since 2004 with the exception of 2016, when it was closed for refurbishment.

Bray has the affluent commuter demographic to support all this fine dining, with the 2021 census placing the ward within the top 60 for residents working in the information and communication sector (13.0% of adults). The present-day vicar will have noted that 51.8% of Bray ward's population gave their religion as Christian, which is nothing to write home about but is above the average for England and Wales. Quite which flavour of Christianity this is was not made clear by the census return, but it might be important.

"When royal James obtained the crown,
And Pop'ry came in fashion,
The penal laws I hooted down,
And read the Declaration;
The Church of Rome I found would fit
Full well my constitution;
And had become a Jesuit,
But for the Revolution.

And this is law, &c."

There were and indeed still are a number of country houses on the south bank of the Thames between Bray and Windsor. One of them is the Victorian Gothic pile of Oakley Court, which was built in 1859 for Sir Richard Hall-Say and passed through a number of owners over the years. In 1949 Oakley Court was taken over by a film company which had been founded by a comedian and businessman called William Hinds, who came from the Hinds jewellery family: his stage name was Will Hammer, because he lived in Hammersmith. Hammer Films had hit on the idea of filming in country houses, because doing so was cheaper than hiring proper film studios, and after being chased away from a country house near Maidenhead by complaints from local residents they ended up at Oakley Court. The first Hammer film made here was The Man in Black, a 1950 B-movie thriller which was based on a radio series and starred Betty Ann Davies and Sid James. Other films followed, not all of them made by Hammer: for example, Oakley Court was Frank N Furter's castle in The Rocky Horror Picture Show. If you want to follow in the footsteps of Brad and Janet and spend the night at Oakley Court, you can (for a price): it's now a luxury hotel.

In 1951 Hammer moved a few hundred yards upstream to a semi-derelict riverside pile called Down Place, which eventually developed into Bray Film Studios. 1955 saw the release of a film adaptation of the BBC TV series The Quatermass Xperiment - with its name deliberately misspelt because Hammer were going for an X certificate for their film. Its financial success spawned a whole genre of Hammer Horror films which came out of Bray in the late 1950s and early 1960s, many of which starred Peter Cushing, Christopher Lee or both.

Hammer eventually left Bray in 1966 - the final movie they made here was The Mummy's Shroud - but its sound stages continued to be in demand for film and television work and for rehearsals for musical tours for many years afterwards. A long list of productions to come out of Bray in the post-Hammer years includes much of the special effects work for Alien, the ITV adaptations of Poirot, and the story of a local lad from down the river in Staines who somewhat unexpectedly found himself elected to Parliament. I speak, of course, of Ali G Indahouse.

In 2010 Bray Studios went dark with its owner citing competition from Pinewood and Shepperton Studios. The site was subsequently sold to a property development company which applied for and got planning permission to knock the sound stages down and build new housing. But no redevelopment was forthcoming, and eventually the property firm reopened the site for filming so that they could get a return on their investment. The hit BBC thriller The Bodyguard was made here in 2017, followed by the Elton John biopic Rocketman and - appropriately enough - the 2019 BBC adaptation of Dracula. In fact, expansion plans for the studios went to Windsor and Maidenhead council's planning committee in 2020 and were passed with not a single complaint from the local residents - who clearly preferred the site to be used for filming rather than built on for housing.

Well, they got their wish. Amazon MGM Studios - after making the second series of the Lord of the Rings spinoff Rings of Power here in 2022 -bought Bray Film Studios from the property developer. They have major expansion plans of their own, and a planning application has been submitted to Windsor and Maidenhead council for enough new sound stages for Bray to support two major productions at the same time. The future for filming in Bray looks bright - unless the council do a U-turn.

"When William was our King declared,
To ease a nation's grievance
With this new wind about I steered,
And swore to him allegiance;
Old principles I did revoke,
Set conscience at a distance;
Passive obedience was a joke,
A jest was non-resistance.

And this is law, &c."

The Bray ward is linked to the outside world by the M4 motorway via the Holyport interchange, which has the curious junction number 8/9. This represents a U-turn on the part of the planners. The original motorway here was the A4(M) Maidenhead Bypass, which was intended to become part of the M4 which would pass north of Reading, and there was a junction here (numbered 8) with the A308 Windsor Road.

But plans changed, and the M4 to the west was eventually built on a line to the south of Reading, leaving the existing route at Holyport. The western half of the Maidenhead Bypass was then renumbered as A404(M), with junction 9 linking the new roads together. But this plan meant that junction 8 had to be closed because it was too close to junction 9, so a new link road was built from junction 9 to the A308 to replace junction 8. The junction 8 number then got transferred to the new junction, resulting in the confusing number 8/9. The link road opened in 1971 with the number A308(M) and it is a contender for England's shortest motorway, at just over half a mile long. It is entirely within Bray ward.

"When gracious Anne became our Queen,
The Church of England's glory,
Another face of things was seen,
And I became a Tory;
Occasional Conformists base,
I damned their moderation,
And thought the Church in danger was
By such prevarication.

And this is law, &c."

The Vicar of Bray becoming a Tory is very much in line with the political preferences of his flock. Since single-member constituencies became the norm in 1885 Bray has been in the constituencies of Wokingham (1885-1918), Windsor (1918-74), Windsor and Maidenhead (1974-97), Windsor again (1997-2010) and currently Maidenhead (since 2010). Every parliamentary election here between 1885 and 2019 returned the Conservative candidate, usually with large majorities: and from 2010 that Conservative candidate was Theresa May, who was Prime Minister at the time of the 2017 general election. Windsor and Maidenhead council also had a Conservative majority until 2023, when there were further changes.

"When George in pudding-time came o'er
And mod'rate men looked big, Sir,
I turned a cat-in-pan once more
And so became a Whig, Sir;
And thus preferment I procured
From our new faith's defender,
And almost ev'ry day abjured
The Pope and the Pretender.

And this is law, &c."

The end of the Queen's reign was followed by a step-change in Windsor and Maidenhead's politics with a swing to Whiggery - or its modern equivalent, Liberalism. In 2023 the Liberal Democrats gained overall control of Windsor and Maidenhead council from the Conservatives, winning 22 seats to 7 Conservatives, 7 seats for the curiously-capitalised independent group "the Borough first", four representatives of Windsor residents' associations and the UK's only councillor for the National Flood Prevention Party. the Borough first topped the poll in Bray ward with 42% of the vote, against 35% for the Conservatives and 15% for the Lib Dems, with tBf and the Tories splitting the two seats. Bray's the Borough first councillor, Suzanne Cross, has recently defected to the Conservatives.

The Tory collapse then continued in the 2024 general election when they lost the Maidenhead constituency to the Lib Dems' Joshua Reynolds, who is now the party's frontbench spokesman for investment and trade. Reynolds combines this with continuing to sit on Windsor and Maidenhead council, where he represents Furze Platt ward in Maidenhead.

This by-election is to replace one of the UK's longest-serving local councillors, a man who successfully navigated the UK's local government for over 50 years from his first election in 1974, and who until his dying day was a councillor for Bray - without ever straying from his Conservative politics. Leo Walters, who passed away in August at the age of 94, had twice served as mayor of Windsor and Maidenhead. He will be a hard act to follow.

Defending Walters' seat for the Conservatives is Clare Stuart-Adams, who is an IT project manager and gives an address in the ward. the Borough first have selected John Baldwin, who is trying to get back onto the council after some years away: he previously served from 2019 to 2023 as a Lib Dem councillor for Belmont ward. The Lib Dems have selected Bray parish councillor Joanna Korczak. Also on the ballot here are Nigel Smith for Labour and parish councillor William Sennett for Reform UK. Whoever wins here will need to seek re-election next May, at which point things might well change again - but the new councillor for Bray will no doubt be ready to change with the times.

"Th'illustrious house of Hanover,
And Protestant succession,
To these I do allegiance swear

And George my loyal king shall be -

And this is law, I will maintain

Queen's Park

Brighton and Hove council, East Sussex; caused by the resignation of Labour councillor Milla Gauge.

This week's two Labour defences both fall in big cities - one of very recent vintage, the other on the south coast. We're going to move a century forward from the time of the Vicar of Bray, to a time when the House of Hanover was still in possession of the British throne and the upper classes were on a building spree. One fashionable location for the great and the good to congregate at the time was Brighton: George IV had built the exotic Royal Pavilion as a seaside retreat, and Regency houses were sprouting up all over the town. But Brighton lacked one important element of the aristocratic Regency/Georgian experience: there were no natural springs here suitable for spa water.

That didn't stop one enterprising German. Frederick Struve, a doctor and chemist from Dresden, had developed a way to reproduce mineral waters by treating ordinary water with chemicals, and in 1825 he opened a pump room in Brighton from which wealthy patrons could obtain the waters from a large number of German spas (but actually from a local well). It was a great success, and William IV and Queen Adelaide became regular customers. Subsequent attempts to pass off treated local water as something more exotic have not always worked, though.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wD79NZroV88

Struve also sold bottled water and fizzy drinks, and when the German Spa pump room closed in the 1850s the building was turned into a bottling plant. Hooper Struve became one of the UK's largest soft drinks firms, and production continued here until 1963. After this the pump room was eventually demolished, although its neoclassical façade was saved, and its site is now occupied by a nursery school.

The German Spa was surrounded by open space in 1825, but that didn't last for long. An enterprising developer bought up the land north of Eastern Road and had grand plans for high-status detached housing set around a park. The park was duly opened next to the spa and named Queen's Park, after Queen Adelaide; but the housing part of the plan didn't really come to fruition at the time. No matter: another 200 years of development has solved that. For the locals and anyone visiting, Queen's Park is described by the council as "perfect if you want to take a short stroll and relax".

Those taking a short stroll to the local polling station tend to be young and left-wing. This ward is part of the Brighton Kemptown and Peacehaven constituency, which has been in Labour hands since 2017: in 2024 Chris Ward took over as the seat's Labour MP following the effective deselection of the previous incumbent Lloyd Russell-Moyle. Ward had previously worked for Sir Keir Starmer as a parliamentary researcher, and he spent the Starmer premiership working for his old boss at the bottom of the ministerial ladder before being sent to the backbenches by Andy Burnham.

In the 2023 local elections Labour gained overall control of Brighton and Hove council, and Queen's Park was one of the wards they won in doing so: the Labour slate of Tristram Burden and Chandni Mistry won with 51% of the vote against 24% for the Green Party and 13% for a Brighton and Hove Independents candidate. The previous Queen's Park ward, which was larger than the present one and included a large chunk of the Kemptown seafront, had been closely fought between Labour and the Greens.

In December 2023 Queen's Park ward councillor Chandni Mistry, who was at the time the youngest member of Brighton and Hove council, was thrown out of the Labour party alongside her mother, Kemptown ward councillor Bharti Gajjar. The pair had been the subject of negative publicity over allegations that they did not live in the city: Mistry was at the time a student reading medicine at Lancaster University, while a Companies House filing for Gajjar gave a correspondence address in Leicester. The pair had also become embroiled in legal action in India. They both resigned from the council in early 2024 and the resulting by-elections were successfully defended by Labour in May 2024: the Queen's Park by-election saw a reduced Labour majority, with 46% for the new Labour candidate Milla Gauge, 28% for the Greens and 18% for the Brighton and Hove Independents.

A second Queen's Park ward by-election was then forced in September 2025 by the resignation of the ward's other Labour councillor Tristram Burden, who was leaving the council in order to take up a politically restricted job with the Care Quality Commission. By this point Labour were in government and their urban vote was clearly on the slide, and in the 2025 by-election the former Brighton and Hove Independents vote collapsed in favour of the Greens. The Green candidate Marina Lademacher ended up gaining the seat from Labour by a 48-31 margin.

The electors of Queen's Park are now being called out for their third by-election in as many years to replace the 2024 by-election winner Milla Gauge, who submitted her resignation in August over safeguarding issues affecting gender-confused children. Her resignation came on the day that the EHRC's new guidance on single-sex spaces came into force: Gauge had sought but not received assurances from the council on how they would comply with this guidance.

This time the defending Labour candidate is Steve Gladwin, who has recently retired from an NHS career; he has also worked as a journalist and as political editor for a local newspaper. The Green Party are hoping for their second by-election gain here in a year with their candidate Mark Strong, a transport planner who unsuccessfully stood here in 2023. An all-male ballot paper also features John Shepherd for Reform UK, Rudy Dikty-Daudiyan for the Lib Dems and Stefan Speckesser for the Conservatives. Appropriately enough for an area with strong historical associations with the House of Hanover, the candidate list is half-German: Dikty-Daudiyan and Speckesser are both of that nationality, while Strong is half-German.

New Bradwell

Milton Keynes council, Buckinghamshire; caused by the death of Labour councillor Shanika Mahendran.

We now come bang up to the date with this week's second Labour defence, which lies on the north edge of Milton Keynes. The New City's urban sprawl ends at the floodplain of the River Great Ouse, and it's here that we find the village of New Bradwell which existed here long before Milton Keynes was a twinkle in a town planner's eye. This is a village which basically dates from the mid-19th century, when New Bradwell greatly expanded as housing for the London and North Western Railway's locomotive works in nearby Wolverton: there was a station here on a branch line from Wolverton to Newport Pagnell. New Bradwell also lies on the Grand Union Canal, which crosses the V6 arterial road on an aqueduct.

The modern New Bradwell ward also includes the majority of the parish of Stantonbury, including the Bradville estate with its old windmill, most of the present Stantonbury estate and the privately-developed 21st-century housing of Oakridge Park to the north. Also here is the open space of Stanton Low Park to the north of Oakridge Park: this was where the original village of Stantonbury used to be before it was abandoned in the mediaeval period.

New Bradwell ward was drawn up for the 2026 local elections: Stantonbury was the main predecessor ward, although New Bradwell itself had previously been part of Wolverton ward. Wolverton ward was (and indeed still is) a very working-class part of New City and was safe Labour in local elections, while Stantonbury ward was a marginal area which swung regularly between Labour and the Conservatives.

However, come May 2026 the Conservatives finished a poor fourth here and the main battle in New Bradwell was between Labour and Reform UK: shares of the vote finished up as 35% for the winning Labour slate, 26% for Reform UK, 22% for the Greens and 11% for the Conservatives. Labour lost their majority on Milton Keynes council in May, and they are now the junior partners in a Lib Dem-led coalition running the New City.

All this is rather different to the parliamentary result two years ago in the key marginal seat of Milton Keynes North. This was a Labour gain in 2024 for Chris Curtis, a native of Milton Keynes (yes, the New City is old enough to have notable native sons and daughters now) who had been a prominent opinion pollster before entering Parliament.

Curtis had employed in his office Labour councillor Shanika Mahendran, who had joined in the Milton Keynes council cabinet in 2025 with the planning and placemaking portfolio. Mahendran, who had also been born and raised in the New City, had been first elected in 2022 for Loughton and Shenley ward before transferring to New Bradwell in the 2026 boundary changes; she won the second of the ward's two seats (this is Milton Keynes' only two-seat ward), winning a two-year term which was due to run until 2028. Sadly, she died two months later following a short illness, at the appallingly early age of 28.

Defending the resulting by-election for Labour is Tracey Bailey, a retired scientist who is trying to get straight back onto Milton Keynes council: she was elected in 2023 in Danesborough and Walton ward, gaining her seat from the Conservatives, but lost re-election in Danesborough ward in May. Reform UK's Julian Camies, a retired engineer, is standing again after finishing as runner-up in this ward four months ago - he lost out on that occasion by just 88 votes. Another unsuccessful candidate here in May who is back for another go is the Greens' Alan Francis, who has previously served as chair of both New Bradwell and Stantonbury parish councils: Francis has stood as a Green candidate in all of the last five general elections, most recently contesting Milton Keynes North in 2024. The Conservatives' Gabriel Costa, who chairs the Milton Keynes branch of the Young Conservatives, stood in May in Walton ward. Martin Clarke completes the New Bradwell ballot paper for the Lib Dems.

Blockley

Cotswold council, Gloucestershire; caused by the resignation of Green Party councillor Clare Turner.

We now travel west from Milton Keynes into the very different landscape of the Cotswolds. The village of Blockley can be found between Chipping Campden to the north-west and Moreton-in-Marsh to the south-east and it currently lies in the north-east corner of Gloucestershire, although this wasn't always the case: until 1931 Blockley parish was a detached part of Worcestershire, and it was included in the Evesham parliamentary seat until the 1950 redistribution. The present Blockley ward also includes the parish of Ebrington to the east of Chipping Campden, which has always been part of Gloucestershire. It has to be said that the ancient county boundaries around the northern end of the Cotswolds were somewhat bizarre: in the map below Blockley is the middle of the three areas marked "Worcs. (det.)".

The main demographic peculiarity of Blockley ward is that it makes the top 40 wards in England and Wales for residents in the 45-64 age bracket (35.3%) - a list which tends to be dominated by rural wards in nice-looking areas which are slightly off the beaten track. Visitors to this area might be tempted by Hidcote Manor Garden, which was established in the early 20th century by Lawrence Johnston and is described by the National Trust, which runs it, as "a world-famous Arts and Crafts-inspired garden". Blockley's main export comes out of the Watsonian Squire factory, which is the UK's leading manufacturer of motorbike sidecars and trailers.

At election time this has traditionally been the sort of rural area where the Conservative vote is weighed rather than counted. Blockley has a very long-serving Conservative member of parliament: Sir Geoffrey Clifton-Brown, the chair of the Commons public accounts committee, has represented this area continuously since 1992. His constituency has had a number of names over that period, with the current name being "North Cotswolds". In the 2025 Gloucestershire county council election the Conservatives had no trouble seeing off Reform UK in the Campden-Vale county division which covers this ward.

So it must have been a bit of a shock to the local Tories when Blockley ward fell to the Green Party in the 2023 Cotswold district elections. Clare Turner, who had stood here in 2019 and finished a poor third on that occasion, defeated the previous Conservative councillor Sue Jepson by 56-44 in a straight fight; there was no candidate here for the Liberal Democrats, who increased their majority on Cotswold council that year.

This is the eighth by-election of the current Cotswold council term, which is rather a lot. The seven previous polls have seen the Lib Dems hold five seats, lose one to the Conservatives (Tetbury with Upton ward) and gain one seat from the Green Party (The Rissingtons ward) whose vote completely collapsed in that by-election in January. So the Greens might well look at this poll with some concern after Blockley ward councillor Clare Turner stood down in August due to new work commitments.

Bob Eastoe will be well aware of how badly the Greens performed in The Rissingtons ward in January, because he was their defending candidate in that by-election; he will be hoping for a better result as the defending Green candidate in Blockley. Eastoe is a retired accountant who sits on Moreton-in-Marsh town council, and in the 2024 general election he was the Green candidate for South Cotswolds. The Conservative candidate is Tom Bradley, who gives an address within the ward in the village of Paxford and is the county councillor for Campden-Vale; Bradley has a few failed candidacies under his belt from previous years, including in the 2019 general election when he stood in Liverpool West Derby. After the all-female ballot paper here in 2023, we now have an all-male ballot of five candidates with the interventions of Reform UK's Matt Fincham who placed third in The Rissingtons by-election in January, Labour's Josh Littler-Jennings who has contested four previous local by-elections in this district over the last two years and finished last on each occasion, and the Lib Dems' Danny Loveridge who last appeared in this column when he stood in a February 2022 by-election for the neighbouring Campden and Vale ward.

Wombourne North

South Staffordshire council; caused by the death of Conservative councillor Barry Bond.

Today's final by-election comes in the South Staffordshire council area, which wraps around the western edge of the Black Country and basically forms a belt of commuter villages for Wolverhampton and Dudley to the west. One of those commuter villages is Wombourne, which is located to the south-west of Wolverhampton just outside the West Midlands metropolitan area and has a population of well over 10,000 - big enough to be a town, if it wants that status. The village is close enough to the Black Country to have some industry thanks to its location on the Staffordshire and Worcestershire Canal; historically it was a small centre for nail-making, while today one of the major employers in Wombourne is McCain's who have a plant here making frozen potato products.

South Staffordshire is consistently one of England's most right-wing local government districts, and even amid the carnage of 2023 the Conservatives still held on to a large overall majority here. Wombourne North ward easily returned the Tory slate with 42% of the vote, against 20% for the Green Party and 19% for Labour; further down the ballot, the Heritage Party candidate beat the Lib Dem slate and one of the Greens.

Wombourne is part of the Kingswinford and South Staffordshire constituency, which has been represented since its 2024 creation by Mike Wood who is the shadow minister for the Cabinet Office. But the Conservatives finally met their match here in the Staffordshire county council election the following year, when the wider Wombourne county division was one of the many, many Conservative seats which fell to Reform UK.

So this could be an interesting by-election to replace Conservative councillor Barry Bond, who served from a December 2012 by-election in the previous Wombourne North and Lower Penn ward until his death in June this year. He had been appointed MBE in the 2021 Birthday Honours for services to education, honouring his 40-year career supporting children and young people. A more personal award came last year when Westfield Primary School in this ward erected a blue plaque to mark Bond's seventeen years of service as a school governor: the plaque described Bond as a "community champion, advisor, raconteur, friend, font of all knowledge".

Defending for the Conservatives is Elizabeth Keeling, who is the chair of Wombourne parish council. The Green Party have selected Claire McIlvenna, a psychotherapist and counsellor who is a regular election candidate in Wombourne: she has stood for Parliament on at least four occasions, and in her latest previous outing she finished fourth in the Wombourne county division last year. The Labour candidate is Anil Singh, who in 2023 stood in Cheslyn Hay on the far side of Wolverhampton. Also on the ballot are Michael Jones for the Lib Dems and Reform UK's Marie Shortland, who is the county councillor for Wombourne and is now hoping to double up at district and county level.
