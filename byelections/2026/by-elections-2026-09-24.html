Five by-elections on 24th September 2026:

Queen's Park

Brighton and Hove council, East Sussex; caused by the resignation of Labour councillor Milla Gauge.

Bray

Windsor and Maidenhead council, Berkshire; caused by the death of Conservative councillor Leo Walters.

New Bradwell

Milton Keynes council, Buckinghamshire; caused by the death of Labour councillor Shanika Mahendran. This week's second Labour defence lies on the north edge of Milton Keynes. The New City's urban sprawl ends at the floodplain of the River Great Ouse, and it's here that we find the village of New Bradwell which existed here long before Milton Keynes was a twinkle in a town planner's eye. This is a village which basically dates from the mid-19th century, when New Bradwell greatly expanded as housing for the London and North Western Railway's locomotive works in nearby Wolverton: there was a station here on a branch line from Wolverton to Newport Pagnell. New Bradwell also lies on the Grand Union Canal, which crosses the V6 arterial road on an aqueduct. The modern New Bradwell ward also includes the majority of the parish of Stantonbury, including the Bradville estate with its old windmill, most of the present Stantonbury estate and the privately-developed 21st-century housing of Oakridge Park to the north. Also here is the open space of Stanton Low Park to the north of Oakridge Park: this was where the original village of Stantonbury used to be before it was abandoned in the mediaeval period. New Bradwell ward was drawn up for the 2026 local elections: Stantonbury was the main predecessor ward, although New Bradwell itself had previously been part of Wolverton ward. Wolverton ward was (and indeed still is) a very working-class part of New City and was safe Labour in local elections, while Stantonbury ward was a marginal area which swung regularly between Labour and the Conservatives. However, come May 2026 the Conservatives finished a poor fourth here and the main battle in New Bradwell was between Labour and Reform UK: shares of the vote finished up as 35% for the winning Labour slate, 26% for Reform UK, 22% for the Greens and 11% for the Conservatives. Labour lost their majority on Milton Keynes council in May, and they are now the junior partners in a Lib Dem-led coalition running the New City. All this is rather different to the parliamentary result two years ago in the key marginal seat of Milton Keynes North. This was a Labour gain in 2024 for Chris Curtis, a native of Milton Keynes (yes, the New City is old enough to have notable native sons and daughters now) who had been a prominent opinion pollster before entering Parliament. Curtis had employed in his office Labour councillor Shanika Mahendran, who had joined in the Milton Keynes council cabinet in 2025 with the planning and placemaking portfolio. Mahendran, who had also been born and raised in the New City, had been first elected in 2022 for Loughton and Shenley ward before transferring to New Bradwell in the 2026 boundary changes; she won the second of the ward's two seats (this is Milton Keynes' only two-seat ward), winning a two-year term which was due to run until 2028. Sadly, she died two months later following a short illness, at the appallingly early age of 28. Defending the resulting by-election for Labour is Tracey Bailey, a retired scientist who is trying to get straight back onto Milton Keynes council: she was elected in 2023 in Danesborough and Walton ward, gaining her seat from the Conservatives, but lost re-election in Danesborough ward in May. Reform UK's Julian Camies, a retired engineer, is standing again after finishing as runner-up in this ward four months ago - he lost out on that occasion by just 88 votes. Another unsuccessful candidate here in May who is back for another go is the Greens' Alan Francis, who has previously served as chair of both New Bradwell and Stantonbury parish councils: Francis has stood as a Green candidate in all of the last five general elections, most recently contesting Milton Keynes North in 2024. The Conservatives' Gabriel Costa, who chairs the Milton Keynes branch of the Young Conservatives, stood in May in Walton ward. Martin Clarke completes the New Bradwell ballot paper for the Lib Dems. Blockley

Cotswold council, Gloucestershire; caused by the resignation of Green Party councillor Clare Turner. We now travel west from Milton Keynes into the very different landscape of the Cotswolds. The village of Blockley can be found between Chipping Campden to the north-west and Moreton-in-Marsh to the south-east and it currently lies in the north-east corner of Gloucestershire, although this wasn't always the case: until 1931 Blockley parish was a detached part of Worcestershire, and it was included in the Evesham parliamentary seat until the 1950 redistribution. The present Blockley ward also includes the parish of Ebrington to the east of Chipping Campden, which has always been part of Gloucestershire. It has to be said that the ancient county boundaries around the northern end of the Cotswolds were somewhat bizarre. The main demographic peculiarity of Blockley ward is that it makes the top 40 wards in England and Wales for residents in the 45-64 age bracket (35.3%) - a list which tends to be dominated by rural wards in nice-looking areas which are slightly off the beaten track. Visitors to this area might be tempted by Hidcote Manor Garden, which was established in the early 20th century by Lawrence Johnston and is described by the National Trust, which runs it, as "a world-famous Arts and Crafts-inspired garden". Blockley's main export comes out of the Watsonian Squire factory, which is the UK's leading manufacturer of motorbike sidecars and trailers. At election time this has traditionally been the sort of rural area where the Conservative vote is weighed rather than counted. Blockley has a very long-serving Conservative member of parliament: Sir Geoffrey Clifton-Brown, the chair of the Commons public accounts committee, has represented this area continuously since 1992. His constituency has had a number of names over that period, with the current name being "North Cotswolds". In the 2025 Gloucestershire county council election the Conservatives had no trouble seeing off Reform UK in the Campden-Vale county division which covers this ward. So it must have been a bit of a shock to the local Tories when Blockley ward fell to the Green Party in the 2023 Cotswold district elections. Clare Turner, who had stood here in 2019 and finished a poor third on that occasion, defeated the previous Conservative councillor Sue Jepson by 56-44 in a straight fight; there was no candidate here for the Liberal Democrats, who increased their majority on Cotswold council that year. This is the eighth by-election of the current Cotswold council term, which is rather a lot. The seven previous polls have seen the Lib Dems hold five seats, lose one to the Conservatives (Tetbury with Upton ward) and gain one seat from the Green Party (The Rissingtons ward) whose vote completely collapsed in that by-election in January. So the Greens might well look at this poll with some concern after Blockley ward councillor Clare Turner stood down in August due to new work commitments. Bob Eastoe will be well aware of how badly the Greens performed in The Rissingtons ward in January, because he was their defending candidate in that by-election; he will be hoping for a better result as the defending Green candidate in Blockley. Eastoe is a retired accountant who sits on Moreton-in-Marsh town council, and in the 2024 general election he was the Green candidate for South Cotswolds. The Conservative candidate is Tom Bradley, who gives an address within the ward in the village of Paxford and is the county councillor for Campden-Vale; Bradley has a few failed candidacies under his belt from previous years, including in the 2019 general election when he stood in Liverpool West Derby. After the all-female ballot paper here in 2023, we now have an all-male ballot of five candidates with the interventions of Reform UK's Matt Fincham who placed third in The Rissingtons by-election in January, Labour's Josh Littler-Jennings who has contested four previous local by-elections in this district over the last two years and finished last on each occasion, and the Lib Dems' Danny Loveridge who last appeared in this column when he stood in a February 2022 by-election for the neighbouring Campden and Vale ward.
