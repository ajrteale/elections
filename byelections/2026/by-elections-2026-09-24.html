New Bradwell

Milton Keynes council, Buckinghamshire; caused by the death of Labour councillor Shanika Mahendran. This week's second Labour defence lies on the north edge of Milton Keynes. The New City's urban sprawl ends at the floodplain of the River Great Ouse, and it's here that we find the village of New Bradwell which existed here long before Milton Keynes was a twinkle in a town planner's eye. This is a village which basically dates from the mid-19th century, when New Bradwell greatly expanded as housing for the London and North Western Railway's locomotive works in nearby Wolverton: there was a station here on a branch line from Wolverton to Newport Pagnell. New Bradwell also lies on the Grand Union Canal, which crosses the V6 arterial road on an aqueduct. The modern New Bradwell ward also includes the majority of the parish of Stantonbury, including the Bradville estate with its old windmill, most of the present Stantonbury estate and the privately-developed 21st-century housing of Oakridge Park to the north. Also here is the open space of Stanton Low Park to the north of Oakridge Park: this was where the original village of Stantonbury used to be before it was abandoned in the mediaeval period. New Bradwell ward was drawn up for the 2026 local elections: Stantonbury was the main predecessor ward, although New Bradwell itself had previously been part of Wolverton ward. Wolverton ward was (and indeed still is) a very working-class part of New City and was safe Labour in local elections, while Stantonbury ward was a marginal area which swung regularly between Labour and the Conservatives. However, come May 2026 the Conservatives finished a poor fourth here and the main battle in New Bradwell was between Labour and Reform UK: shares of the vote finished up as 35% for the winning Labour slate, 26% for Reform UK, 22% for the Greens and 11% for the Conservatives. Labour lost their majority on Milton Keynes council in May, and they are now the junior partners in a Lib Dem-led coalition running the New City. All this is rather different to the parliamentary result two years ago in the key marginal seat of Milton Keynes North. This was a Labour gain in 2024 for Chris Curtis, a native of Milton Keynes (yes, the New City is old enough to have notable native sons and daughters now) who had been a prominent opinion pollster before entering Parliament. Curtis had employed in his office Labour councillor Shanika Mahendran, who had joined in the Milton Keynes council cabinet in 2025 with the planning and placemaking portfolio. Mahendran, who had also been born and raised in the New City, had been first elected in 2022 for Loughton and Shenley ward before transferring to New Bradwell in the 2026 boundary changes; she won the second of the ward's two seats (this is Milton Keynes' only two-seat ward), winning a two-year term which was due to run until 2028. Sadly, she died two months later following a short illness, at the appallingly early age of 28. Defending the resulting by-election for Labour is Tracey Bailey, a retired scientist who is trying to get straight back onto Milton Keynes council: she was elected in 2023 in Danesborough and Walton ward, gaining her seat from the Conservatives, but lost re-election in Danesborough ward in May. Reform UK's Julian Camies, a retired engineer, is standing again after finishing as runner-up in this ward four months ago - he lost out on that occasion by just 88 votes. Another unsuccessful candidate here in May who is back for another go is the Greens' Alan Francis, who has previously served as chair of both New Bradwell and Stantonbury parish councils: Francis has stood as a Green candidate in all of the last five general elections, most recently contesting Milton Keynes North in 2024. The Conservatives' Gabriel Costa, who chairs the Milton Keynes branch of the Young Conservatives, stood in May in Walton ward. Martin Clarke completes the New Bradwell ballot paper for the Lib Dems.
