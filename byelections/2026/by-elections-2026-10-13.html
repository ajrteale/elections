One by-election on Tuesday 13th October 2026:

Rodborough

Stroud council, Glocuestershire; caused by the resignation of Labour councillor Cathyrn Hofmann. This is one of those weeks where subscribers to Andrew's Previews get two pieces for the price of one, because we have a Tuesday by-election to bring you this week as well as the usual Thursday diet. This is a poll to Stroud council, which has a full council meeting scheduled for Thursday evening so the winner of today's poll will be able to hit the ground running straight away. Our by-election today is in Rodborough, a village which lies immediately to the south of Stroud on the southern side of the River Frome. Rodborough and the connected village of Lightpill lie on the Bath Road going south from Stroud, and they surround the high ground of Rodborough Common which is part of the Cotswolds National Landscape. Up on the Common can be found Rodborough Fort, which is not of great antiquity: this is an eighteenth-century folly which has been turned into a private residence. In the 2021 census Rodborough ranked in the top 70 wards in England and Wales for residents working in the "electricity, gas, steam and air conditioning supply" sector. This not a reference to the Revd Wilfred Awdry, creator of the Thomas the Tank Engine characters, who retired to Rodborough in the 1960s and died here in 1998: instead, this employment pattern is likely driven by the green energy firm Ecotricity, whose head office is in Stroud town centre just a few hundred yards outside the ward boundary. Stroud has always been a rather left-wing and generally "alternative" place politically: this was the first area where the Green Party had enough support to win council seats, and the Greens currently run Stroud council as a minority with 24 out of 51 seats. But the Greens haven't yet cracked Rodborough ward, which has voted Labour at every Stroud council election from 2011 onwards. The Conservatives regularly won Rodborough before then, but they were also-rans here at the last Stroud council election in 2024: Labour topped the poll with 34% of the vote and won both seats, the Green Party slate polled 30%, and outgoing Labour councillor Robin Layfield sought re-election as an independent candidate and finished on 25%. The Stroud parliamentary seat has voted for the government at every election since 1979 with the exception of a Labour win in 2017. Its current Labour MP is Simon Opher, a GP who attracted attention before his political career for prescribing creative activities in art and nature to patients with anxiety or stress-related conditions: this won him an MBE in 2016. The most recent election here was to Gloucestershire county council in 2025. The Rodborough county division, which is larger than this ward and takes in the town of Cainscross to the west, turned in a very close result on that occasion with the Greens defeating Labour by 1,150 votes to 1,138, a majority of jutst twelve votes. This was a gain from Labour, although boundary changes confuse the issue slightly. This by-election is to replace Katy Hofmann, who had been the leader of the opposition Labour group on Stroud council until she resigned on health grounds in September. Defending the seat is John Bloxsom, who was the county councillor for Rodborough from 2021 until he lost his seat by 12 votes last year: he was leader of the Labour group on Gloucestershire county council going into that election, and he has also served on Stroud town council in the past. The Greens have recruited Rodborough parish councillor Robin Layfield, who was elected as a Labour councillor for this ward in 2021 and polled respectably when seeking re-election as an independent candidate in 2024. Also standing here on an all-male ballot are the Conservatives' Christopher Easton, the Lib Dems' George James and Reform UK's Gerald O'Brien, who has previously twice stood for Parliament as a UKIP candidate in Kent.
