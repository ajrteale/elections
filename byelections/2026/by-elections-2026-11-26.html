"All the right votes, but not necessarily in the right order"

Six by-elections, for eight seats, on 26th November 2026:

Castle Bromwich

Solihull council, West Midlands; caused by the resignations of Reform UK councillors Jamie Campbell, Alan Feeney and Chris Whelan; and

Shard End

Birmingham council, West Midlands; caused by the resignation of Reform UK councillor Alan Feeney.

We're going to start this week with a bumper entry from the Councillors Behaving Badly file. Our first two by-elections this week are the result of Reform UK selecting racist election candidates, and the electorate voting them into office.

Full marks are due to the ITV News undercover team for exposing the views of Alan Feeney, Jamie Campbell and Chris Whelan, all of whom were elected in May as Reform UK councillors for Castle Bromwich ward in Solihull. Campbell and Whelan were new faces, but Feeney was already a councillor for the ward, having been elected in 2022 on the Conservative ticket before defecting to Reform UK. He was the senior man in the campaign and he knew the ward well.

Om the campaign trail, Feeney was secretly recorded by ITV's reporter saying that “if it's an Asian name, I'm not even knocking at the door, they’re not voting Reform and I ain’t wasting my time". He opined that his area had been "infested" with Asian families who were attracted by the large houses available in Castle Bromwich and Shard End.

At a meeting between the three candidates in the pub, Campbell was filmed discussing the prospect of a mosque being built in Castle Bromwich: “There's got to be a way of stopping that, like go and buy some pork chops. We'll bury it in the ground.” Meanwhile Whelan brought a different offensive angle to the table: referring to a female Reform UK candidate for Solihull council, he suggested that "if she was my daughter, it would still be bath night on a Thursday".

When polling day came, the Reform UK slate of Feeney, Campbell and Whelan won the three seats in Castle Bromwich ward very comfortably, with 50% of the vote to 25% for the Conservatives and 12% for the Green Party (who had won councillors here in 2018 and 2019). Alan Feeney was also elected to Birmingham council for the neighbouring ward of Shard End, where he won by an even more convincing margin: he polled 51% against 20% for Labour and 13% for the Greens; he took the seat over from the former Birmingham Labour council leader Ian Ward, who had not sought re-election.

Feeney was installed as deputy mayor of Solihull and seemed set for a four-year term on both councils - until ITV aired their documentary in October. Following their exposure Feeney, Campbell and Whelan all resigned their council seats, and Feeney was also suspended from his day job as a senior prison officer. The gruesome threesome were far from being the only Reform UK activists featured in the ITV programme, but they are the only ones who were ashamed enough by their behaviour to have left public life and triggered by-elections.

This brings us to Castle Bromwich, an old village overlooking the River Tame a few miles to the east of Birmingham. This was once the centre of its own rural district, and to this day Castle Bromwich proper has never been incorporated into Birmingham even though it's long been part of the urban sprawl. Back in the day the village lay on the Chester Road, which was a major turnpike route; this has now been superseded by the M6 motorway which is the northern boundary of the modern Castle Bromwich ward.

The housing within the curve of the motorway is almost entirely post-war, but there are some exceptions to this rule. Just off the old Chester Road is Castle Bromwich Hall, a country house which dates from the 16th century: it was built for Sir Edward Devereux, 1st Baronet, who represented Tamworth in the Elizabethan parliaments. It ended up in the hands of the Bridgeman family, who became the Earls of Bradford and occupied the house until the death of countess Ida Bridgeman (a great-grandparent of the Duke of Gloucester) in 1935. The hall itself is now a hotel. Its gardens are described as the last surviving example of a formal English garden from the 18th century, and they are open to the public along with 30 acres of parkland.

The original Castle Bromwich parish was larger than it is now, and some parts of it have been annexed by Birmingham over the years: the modern city limits lie just to the south of Bradford Road and Chester Road. Here we find the 1940s Birmingham overspill estate of Shard End, which was named after a farm in this area and which in due course swallowed up the Yorkwood scout camp. There is still a fair amount of open space in the south of the ward, where the area around the River Cole (which is the southern boundary) forms the Kingfisher country park.

Shard End's parish church, which is dedicated to All Saints, is notable for being the first Anglican parish church to be built and dedicated after the Second World War: it opened for divine service in 1955. It is within the area enclosed by Shard End Crescent, which was the childhood home of two noted Birmingham musicians: the Duran Duran drummer Roger Taylor grew up at number 350, a few doors down from Electric Light Orchestra's Jeff Lynne at number 368. Lynne put a reference to Shard End into the lyrics of ELO's song All Over the World.

Alan Feeney had wanted the UK, or at least his patch of it, to be 1% non-white. The 2021 census places the non-white population in Shard End ward at 22.7%, with the ward ranking 3rd in the West Midlands region for residents of mixed race (7.4%). Decades of Right to Buy has eroded the proportion of social housing here, which now stands at 40.0% to 44.8% owner-occupation. The ward as a whole is a seriously deprived area. This is a stark contrast to next-door Castle Bromwich ward, which is a lower middle-class enclave: Castle Bromwich has high levels of owner-occupation, low deprivation levels and a lower non-white population of 14.5%.

Both of these wards are part of the cumbersomely-named constituency of Birmingham Hodge Hill and Solihull North. This is represented by Labour MP Liam "I'm afraid there is no money" Byrne, who served in Gordon Brown's cabinet as Chancellor of the Duchy of Lancaster and now chairs the Commons business and trade select committee. Byrne, who was also the losing Labour candidate for Mayor of the West Midlands in 2021, has represented Shard End since he won the Birmingham Hodge Hill by-election in 2004; Castle Bromwich was transferred into his seat in 2024 from the former Meriden constituency.

In Birmingham council elections Shard End ward had been consistently Labour-voting for many years before the Reform UK breakthrough in May, although the potential for the radical right was shown when UKIP were a handful of votes away from winning a seat here in 2014. The winner of Shard End ward on that occasion was John Cotton, who then shared the ward's representation with Ian Ward; both of them went on to become leader of Birmingham city council, with Ward holding the top job from 2017 until 2023 and Cotton then leading Birmingham Labour through bankruptcy and bin strikes into the 2026 election.

Birmingham Labour reaped what they had sown in the 2026 Birmingham election, which returned a council shattered into many different political pieces with no party anywhere near the 51 seats required for a majority. Reform UK finished up as the largest party on just 23 seats, with the Greens on 19, Labour on 17, the Conservatives 16, independent candidates on 13, the Lib Dems on 12 and the Workers Party of Britain on 1: the latter party defeated John Cotton in Glebe Farm and Tile Cross ward, but they had to wait for this win because the result there was not declared until the Monday evening after the election. A precarious minority coalition of Green Party, Liberal Democrat and independent councillors was eventually put together, under a Lib Dem leader.

The political context is very different in Solihull, which has been under Conservative leadership for all but four years since the metropolitan borough was created in 1974. This May all 51 of Solihull's councillors were up for re-election as a result of ward boundary changes, and the Conservatives lost their majority after 15 years of overall control but still finished as the largest party with 24 seats, against 17 for Reform UK, 6 Lib Dems and 4 Greens. The Tories have since picked up a defector from Reform UK, and they will be back in majority control if they can gain any of the three seats up for election in the Castle Bromwich triple by-election.

Triple by-elections are extremely rare events, and this is only the fourth example this century. The last one happened in October last year in the Maidstone district of Kent, when all three independent councillors for Harrietsham, Lenham and North Downs ward resigned in protest at garden village development plans for the area; Reform UK won all three seats comfortably at the resulting by-election. The other two took place in July 2005 in the Aston and Bordesley Green wards of Birmingham, after all six Labour councillors in those wards had been disqualified for postal vote fraud in the notorious "banana republic" Election Court case; Labour ended up losing five of those seats, three to the Lib Dems in Aston and two to the People's Justice Party in Bordesley Green. One of the Lib Dem winners in Aston on that occasion was Ayoub Khan, who is now the independent MP for Birmingham Perry Barr.

The fractured nature of Birmingham politics is reflected in a long ballot paper in Shard End ward. Trying to pick up the pieces for Reform UK is Sarah Myers, who stood in May in Sutton Mere Green ward in Sutton Coldfield. The Labour candidate Gerald Dickson is a paramedic and reservist in the Royal Army Medical Corps. Standing for the Greens is Shaun Allan-Dooley, who has experience in the education sector as a teacher, SEND tutor and education researcher. The candidate list also includes the Conservatives' Clifton Welch who won a by-election in Kingstanding ward in 2024 but lost his seat in May, independent candidate Duncan Riley who stood in May for Reform UK in Bromford and Hodge Hill ward, Jack Brookes for Restore Britain and Fahad Hussain for the Workers Party.

Mind, if you thought that was a long ballot paper take a look at Castle Bromwich where the electors have three votes and 21 candidates to choose from. The defending Reform UK slate consists of Mark Evans, Jacqueline Howse and Garry Taylor: Evans is described as a local business owner who provides medical services across the borough, Howse is a businesswoman who works with children and families in the ward, while Taylor is a former engineer who now works in the social care sector by day and behind the scenes in local theatre productions by night. In May Evans stood in Blythe ward, Howse contested Meriden and Arden and Taylor was a candidate in Balsall and Berkswell. With overall control of the council at stake, the Conservatives have reselected former councillor Martin McCarthy who represented this ward from 2021 until he lost his seat in May; he is an electrical engineer with over 30 years experience in the motor industry. Joining McCarthy on the Conservative slate are Georgina Hartland and Ethan Potter who both work in audit and compliance, Hartland in the care sector and Potter in IT; in May Hartland stood in Elmdon ward while Potter was a candidate in Kingshurst and Smith's Wood. The Green Party have nominated two former councillors who lost their seats in other wards in May, Mark Wilson (Smith's Wood 2015-26) and Shesh Sheshabhatter (Chelmsley Wood 2021-26), together with new face Dee Boyce. Standing for Labour are Kashif Ahmed, Hazel Dawkins and Tom Gilmore; the Liberal Democrat slate is Bob Carleton, Christopher Johnson and Matt Purssell; Michael Craven is standing on the Restore Britain ticket; and we have five independent candidates. Three of them - Michael Gough, Bhavan Pemmaiah and Paul Thompson - were the Reform UK candidates for Silhill ward in May, and Gough is a former Conservative councillor for that ward who was first elected in 2018; Qasim Ali is a "digital creator" who describes himself on Facebook as "an empathetic, progressive and positive person that wants to do the right thing at all times"; and 19-year-old Elliot Astley runs a darts league in the ward.

Marlbrook

Bromsgrove council, Worcestershire; caused by the resignation of Conservative councillor Helen Jones.

We'll stay in the West Midlands for this week's Conservative defence, which is on the southern slopes of the Lickey Hills not too far to the south of Birmingham. Indeed the main thoroughfares through Marlbrook village are the A38 Birmingham Road (which is the western boundary of this ward) and the Old Birmingham Road.

The latter road passes by Lickey Grange, a Victorian country house which was home from 1910 until his death in 1941 to the car designer Herbert Austin. One of his best-selling models, the Austin 7, was designed in the billiard room at Lickey Grange. As well as his business activities, Austin was also the Conservative MP for Birmingham King's Norton from 1918 to 1924 (although he never spoke in the Chamber) and he was elevated to the Lords in 1936 as the first and only Lord Austin. In 1953 Lickey Grange was acquired by the Birmingham Royal Institution for the Blind, which ran a boarding school for blind pupils here until 1990; the grounds were then redeveloped for housing, although Austin's old house still survives.

We can surmise from Marlbrook ward's census return that the Lickey Grange housing is overwhelmingly owner-occupied. Marlbrook ranks 6th of all the wards in England and Wales for owner-occupation, at 92% of households. The ward is clearly an affluent area with high levels of managers and professionals, and Lord Austin's ghost might be happy to hear that 64% of households here have two or more cars.

Marlbrook is separated from Birmingham by the summits of the Lickey Hills and it remains part of rural Worcestershire, administered from Bromsgrove down the hill. Bromsgrove council has been run since 2023 by a coalition of Conservative and independent councillors, which included Marlbrook's outgoing ward councillor Helen Jones: she was comfortably re-elected for her second term of office in 2023 with 51% of the vote, against 25% for Labour and 16% for the Greens.

The Conservatives have held the Bromsgrove parliamentary seat continuously since its re-creation in 1983, and Bromsgrove had a high-profile MP in 2010-24: Sajid Javid was Theresa May's last Home Secretary and Boris Johnson's first Chancellor, although Javid never delivered a budget before resigning from government in February 2020 over a demand from Johnson to sack all his special advisors. In 2024 the Conservatives successfully passed the Bromsgrove seat on to Bradley Thomas, who had previously been leader of Wychavon council elsewhere in Worcestershire. Thomas himself joined the frontbench in September as a junior shadow business minister and Conservative whip.

The Marlbrook ward last went to the polls at the 2025 Worcestershire county council election, in which it is part of the Woodvale division which covers a series of commuter villages north of Bromsgrove. This seat went to Reform UK last year by a 44-35 margin over the Conservatives. Reform UK fell short of a majority on the county council, and their minority administration was ousted after a year by a curious coalition of Conservative, Lib Dem, Green and independent councillors under a Green council leader.

Meanwhile the Conservatives need to defend the Marlbrook ward by-election after Helen Jones stood down from Bromsgrove council in October for health reasons. Their defending candidate is Cathy Perruzza, who is fighting her first election campaign. Other first-timers on the ballot include Labour's Geraint Lewis and the Green Party's Lockie Carbery. Reform UK have selected Nik Price who is the county councillor for Bromsgrove West; and the Lib Dems have entered the fray with their candidate Kim Routley.

Caversham Heights

Reading council, Berkshire; caused by the resignation of Labour councillor Jenny McGrother.

The remaining three by-elections this week are Labour defences which we will take from south to north. This week's southernmost poll takes place in Caversham, which is the part of Reading that lies north of the River Thames: it was incorporated into Reading in 1911, having previously been part of Oxfordshire. Caversham Heights ward is in the north-west of Caversham, along the road towards Wallingford, and as the name suggests it occupies high ground going down to the Thames. It is generally built up, with the main remaining open space being the curiously-named chalk valley of Bug's Bottom.

In the 2021 census Caversham Heights ward ranked 9th in England and Wales for residents working in the information and communication sector, at 16.4%. This is a major industry in the Reading area, and Berkshire as a whole accounts for all of the top 14 wards (and 18 of the top 20) for this statistic. Like Marlbrook in the previous section, this is an affluent commuter area.

This ward was created in 2022 and covers the whole of the former Mapledurham ward together with the majority of the former Thames ward. (Reading council's present Thames ward has nothing in common with the pre-2022 ward of that name.) Unusually for Reading whose council has had a Labour majority since 2011, both of these wards consistently voted Conservative in the period 2004-21. However, at its first election in 2022 Caversham Heights ward split its seats two to the Conservatives and one to Labour, who gained a second seat from the Conservatives in 2023.

Since 2024 Caversham has been included in the Reading Central constituency represented by Labour's Matt Rodda, who had gained the predecessor seat of Reading East from the Conservatives in 2017. Rodda had served on the Labour frontbench under Corbyn and Starmer for over six years leading up to that election, latterly as shadow minister for the curious combination of pensions, AI and intellectual property; but there has been no place for him in government to date.

In May this year there were two seats up for election in Caversham Heights ward. Veteran Conservative Isobel Ballsdon was up for re-election in ordinary course, while there was also a by-election to replace Labour councillor Sam Juthani who had gained his seat from the Conservatives in 2023. The topline figures show a convincing Conservative win with 39% of the vote, against 22% for Labour, 18% for the Green Party and 12% for Reform UK; however, much of the Conservative score appears to be a personal vote for Ballsdon, who outpolled her running-mate by nearly 700 votes. One wonders how much of this was driven by the fact that the other Conservative candidate was called Saadia Saadat, but she successfully overcame any racist vote in the ward to gain the by-election seat from Labour.

Now Labour are defending their second casual vacancy in this ward in six months, after their remaining ward councillor Jenny McGrother tendered her resignation in October for health reasons. She was still in her first term of office, having gained her seat from the Conservatives in May 2024.

Defending this seat for Labour is Wrishi Basu, who has a background in software engineering. The Conservatives' Dave Luckett is seeking to return to Reading council after some years away: he previously represented Caversham ward from 2008 to 2012. The Green Party have reselected Adil Khan who stood here in May on their ticket. Reform UK's Clarence Mitchell is a former BBC journalist and newsreader who now works as a communications professional, and he is also seeking to get back onto the council: Mitchell was elected as a Conservative Reading councillor in 2021, but he lost re-election in the neighbouring Emmer Green ward under his new Reform UK colours in May. The Lib Dems' Jo Ramsay and Restore Britain's Tom Hadow complete a ballot paper of six candidates.

Mold: Broncoed

Flintshire council, North Wales; caused by the death of Labour councillor Teresa Carberry.

We now move from the county town of Berkshire to what was until very recently the county town of Flintshire. Last year Flintshire county council vacated its county hall on the northern outskirts of Mold and moved into new premises at Ewloe; the old 1960s county hall is not much longer for this world, with demolition plans having recently been approved.

What this means for Mold remains to be seen. The town lies in the valley of the River Alyn, which winds its way through the Clwydian hills to eventually flow into the Dee north of Wrexham. Surprisingly the name "Mold" comes from Norman French, being a shortening of the words mont hault meaning "high hill", which presumably refers to Bailey Hill on which the mediaeval Mold Castle once stood. The town's Welsh-language name is completely different: it's Yr Wyddgrug, or "the burial mound".

Mold town centre lies a little to the south of Bailey Hill, and Broncoed ward takes in the western part of the town centre and generally covers the south of the town. It includes the town's secondary schools, the Alun School and its Welsh-speaking partner Ysgol Maes Garmon. The Alun School's former pupils include the Welsh Labour leader Ken Skates and the Coldplay lead guitarist Jonny Buckland, while the actor (and briefly lead singer of the Super Furry Animals) Rhys Ifans was educated at Ysgol Maes Garmon.

Mold: Broncoed had been represented since 2022 by Labour councillor Teresa Carberry, who gained her seat rather narrowly from independent Haydn Bateman. Carberry polled 40% of the vote to 35% for Bateman and 16% to another independent, Gareth Williams. She was part of a minority Labour administration on a rather fragmented Flintshire council.

At Westminster level Mold is included within the Clwyd East constituency, whose Labour MP Becky Gittins is another Alun School former pupil. However, Labour drew a blank across the wider Clwyd constituency at the May 2026 Senedd election, with Reform UK winning three seats to two for Plaid Cymru and one for the Conservatives. Top of the Plaid list was Llyr Gruffydd, who was briefly interim leader of the party in 2023 and now sits in the Welsh Cabinet, with the rural resilience and sustainability portfolio; meanwhile Clwyd's Conservative MS is the Welsh Conservative leader Darren Millar. Hannah Blythyn, who had previously represented Mold as the Labour MS for the former Delyn constituency, lost her seat.

Teresa Carberry had been a teacher at the Alun School before entering politics, which was reflected during the pandemic years when she set up a scheme distributing school uniforms in Mold. Carberry had been a Flintshire councillor since 2022 but she was first elected in 2017 to Mold town council, and she served twice as mayor of Mold, in 2020-21 and again in 2023-24. She died from cancer in September, aged 64.

Defending the last Flintshire by-election of this council term for Labour is Laura Murton, who is married to the former Labour MS Hannah Blythyn. This time there are no independent candidates standing so quite a lot of votes are up for grabs. The only other party to stand here in 2022 was the Conservatives who have nominated Kathy Cracknell, while we have interventions from five new parties: Richard Clarke stands for Reform UK, Nanette Davies for the Lib Dems, Emily Hart for the Greens, Ted Jenkins for Restore Britain and Kristina Renshaw for the Heritage Party.

Harpurhey

Manchester council; caused by the death of Labour councillor Patrick Karney.

We'll finish this week with one of Manchester's more bizarre sights. Travellers on the tram north out of Victoria will soon dive into a tunnel that curves around to the north. The line then quickly climbs onto a viaduct, on the left-hand side of which is a public artwork: the end of a submarine sticking out of the ground 25 feet high. This the "Dreadnowt", a sculpture designed by Jonathan Woolfenden and maintained by the adjacent HMG Paints factory, which gives it a makeover from time to time.

Just to the north of here is Irk Valley Junction, where the Bury and Rochdale tram lines diverge. This was once a mainline railway junction and it was the scene of a major accident in 1953, when two trains collided at the junction and one of the carriages fell 40 feet off the viaduct into the River Irk below; nine passengers died. The accident report placed the blame equally on the signalmen, who had developed some distinctly unsafe working practices, and on train driver Hardman who had passed a signal at danger and was also killed. At the time Hardman was an alderman of Bury, and he had previously served as mayor of that town.

The River Irk generally forms the western boundary of Harpurhey ward, which runs along the Rochdale Road through north Manchester. The ward takes in the green spaces of Moston Vale to the east and Queen's Park to the west: the latter is one of Britain's oldest municipal parks, having opened in 1846.

At the southern end of Harpurhey ward we find Collyhurst, a distinctly inner-city area which is being regenerated. A development of new homes called Collyhurst North is taking place next to the main road, although it's fair to say that the development pace here isn't as quick as it is closer to Victoria. We can see this effect with Central Park, a business estate which includes the Greater Manchester Police headquarters. This is served by a striking tram stop on the Rochdale line, which was built in 2005 - seven years before the Rochdale trams actually started running.

Harpurhey was the birthplace of the Clockwork Orange author Anthony Burgess, and although it's not a centre for ultraviolence in real life this is an area with more than its fair share of social problems with high unemployment and low qualification levels. The ward is in the top 50 wards in England and Wales for residents under the age of 16 (28.0%). For those who are old enough to work, in the 2021 census Harpurhey ranked 2nd in the North West, and in the top 50 wards in England and Wales, for residents working in administrative and support service activities (9.5%). Harpurhey was also 2nd in the North West for its black population (25.9%) and for residents born in Africa (15.6%), in both cases behind Moss Side; its African-born population is in the top 20 wards in England and Wales.

The local MP here since 1997 has been Labour backbencher Graham Stringer, who was leader of Manchester city council before his election to Westminster and represented Harpurhey ward on the council. He represents a seat which has had a few names over the years: Stringer's constituency is currently called Blackley and Middleton South.

This by-election is to replace another local political veteran. Pat Karney, who died in October at the age of 78, was Manchester's longest-serving councillor having been first elected in 1979. He originally represented the city-centre Collegiate Church ward, transferring to Harpurhey in 1982 following ward boundary changes. Karney was originally from Dublin but he had moved to Collyhurst when he was a boy, and he had spent his 47 years on the council on a series of high-profile campaigns ranging from the regeneration of the city centre to the repeal of section 28. He was also the driving force behind the Manchester Christmas markets, which started in 1998 and seem to get bigger and bigger every year.

Karney was rarely seriously challenged in local elections, and his result in 2023 when he won his fourteenth and final term of office - a 72-13 lead over the Conservatives - was typical. But in May 2026 Manchester Labour found themselves caught in a pincer movement between the Greens in the inner-city wards and Reform UK in the more run-down peripheral areas of the city. In Harpurhey those forces largely cancelled out and Labour held on in May with 38% of the vote, to 26% for Reform UK and 25% for the Greens. More normal service was resumed in the Greater Manchester mayoral by-election over the summer, when Harpurhey gave 54% of the vote to Labour, 16% to Reform UK and 13% to the Greens; in the runoff Labour led Reform UK here 72-28.

Pat Karney's shoes are big ones to fill, and the defending Labour candidate in this by-election has not got off to a good start in filling them. Abdul Sarfraz was forced to apologise during the campaign after posts were found on his social media which were blatantly taken from behind the wheel in stationary motorway traffic. The Reform UK candidate is Ian Carter, who stood in the rather different territory of Chorlton ward in May. Standing for the Greens is Sean Duggan, who contested Clayton and Openshaw ward six months ago. The electors here can also choose from Sarah Haynes for the Conservatives, Peter Matthews for the Lib Dems, independent candidate Declan Gleeson and Restore Britain's James Harvie.
