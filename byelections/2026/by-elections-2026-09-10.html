"There is a theory which states that if ever anyone discovers exactly what UK local government is for and why it is here, it will instantly disappear and be replaced by something even more bizarre and inexplicable.

There is another theory which states that this has already happened."

So, this week plans for local government reorganisation in England (outside Surrey, where the process is more advanced and elections to the new councils have already been held) fell apart. Colour me stunned. One reason you've not heard a peep from me in this column about prospective new local government arrangements is that I have seen far too many reorganisation plans fall apart to assume that they will go ahead until the legal orders get laid. And even that rule of thumb came to grief over postponements of this year's local elections. The fact that this reorganisation process was clearly driven by people who didn't know what they were doing and had no idea what they were trying to achieve was another warning sign. So it's still business as usual for Andrew's Previews going forward.

Four by-elections on 10th September 2026:

Hutton and Locking

North Somerset council; caused by the death of Conservative councillor Terence Porter.

This week's four by-elections split evenly into two pairs: two Labour defences in the North and two Conservative defences in the South, with which we start. Last week we saw the Green Party successfully defend a seat in Somerset; this week's tour of England will therefore start in that county.

The villages of Hutton and Locking lie close to the Somerset coast, just inland from Weston-super-Mare. Locking is bypassed by the main road going east from Weston towards Bristol Airport, while the ward also includes the parish of Bleadon which lies on the only road going south from Weston over the River Axe: this village overlooks the Somerset Levels beyond. In between is a western ridge of the Mendip Hills, with the village of Hutton located on the northern slopes of this ridge.

Extensive building work is currently underway to the north of Locking village, on an area which was previously occupied by the Ministry of Defence in the form of RAF Locking: this was established during the Second World War as a training centre for aircraft engineers, and it operated until 2000 after which the site was levelled. It is now being redeveloped for housing as a new village called Locking Parklands, with construction of a projected final figure of 1,450 homes well underway. This might well force a ward boundary review for North Somerset in the near future.

Since 2015 Hutton and Locking ward has returned two North Somerset councillors, and since 2019 it has been politically split between Conservative councillor Terence Porter and independent Michael Solomon, who had previously been a strong runner-up in the ward in 2015 on the ticket of a now-defunct localist group called the North Somerset First Independents. This party was led and bankrolled by a multimillionaire dairy farmer called Derek Mead who was elected to North Somerset council in 2015, but the money dried up after Mead died in a freak accident in 2017: he was crushed by his own tractor, after he left his dog in the cab and the dog accidentally knocked the tractor's gear lever into forward gear. Vote shares here in the 2023 election were 36% for the Conservatives, 27% for Solomon and 20% for the Lib Dems, although this is somewhat skewed by only Labour putting up a full slate in the ward.

This ward is one of the few rural parts of the Weston-super-Mare parliamentary seat, which is relatively tightly drawn around the town. Weston-super-Mare has voted for all three of the main Westminster parties this century, with the town returning its first Labour MP in 2024: he is Dan Aldridge, who defeated five-term Conservative incumbent John Penrose. Labour also participate in the anti-Conservative coalition which runs the seriously hung North Somerset council: the Tories are the largest party on the council, but at the last count they only hold 12 out of 50 seats plus this vacancy.

Today's by-election here is to replace long-serving ward councillor Terry Porter following his death in July. He had been first elected at a by-election in June 2009, giving him just over 17 years' service.

Defending this seat for the Conservatives is John Standfield, who is a self-employed IT systems consultant and Weston-super-Mare town councillor. This time there is no independent on the ballot so quite a lot of votes are up for grabs. The Lib Dems have reselected Hutton parish councillor Georgina Barry, who was runner-up in this ward in 2023. Also standing are Yue He Parkinson who was an unsuccessful Lib Dem candidate in Portishead in 2023 but has the Labour nomination here, William Jones who last appeared in this column in June 2023 when he was an unsuccessful Conservative candidate in a Plymouth council by-election but has the Reform UK nomination here, and James Willis-Boden who was an unsuccessful Labour candidate in Weston-super-Mare in 2023 but has the Green Party nomination here. Confused? You might be.

Haverhill West

West Suffolk council; caused by the resignation of Conservative councillor Margaret Marks.

This week's other Conservative defence is in East Anglia, close to the point where Suffolk, Cambridgeshire and Essex all meet. Here we find the town of Haverhill, which has seen large-scale housing development in this century that has expanded the town far beyond its old mediaeval core. Originally this was London overspill development, which was completed just in time for the railway line to Haverhill to fall victim to the Beeching Axe. More recent development - such as that in Haverhill West ward - has been driven by the town's proximity to Cambridge and the jobs available there, and the A1307 road from Haverhill to Cambridge is heavily congested.

Haverhill is one of the three main towns (alongside Newmarket and Mildenhall) in the West Suffolk parliamentary seat, which has only returned Conservative MPs since its creation in 1997. Since 2024 the incumbent here has been Nick Timothy, who is in Kemi Badenoch's shadow cabinet as shadow justice secretary; before entering the Commons, he had been best known as joint Downing Street chief of staff to Theresa May.

The most recent election here was in May to Suffolk county council, whose division boundaries split Haverhill West ward between divisions. Haverhill North West and Withersfield division was a comfortable Conservative hold, while Haverhill South division voted for Reform UK. In previous years this area had been part of the two-seat Haverhill Cangle county division, which was normally Conservative but returned a UKIP councillor in 2013, with UKIP gaining the other seat at a by-election in May 2016. The outgoing Conservative councillor here, Margaret Marks, was the losing Tory candidate in that by-election which had been triggered by the death of her husband.

The present ward boundaries date from 2019 when West Suffolk council was created. On that occasion Haverhill West ward voted Conservative, but the ward split its representation in 2023 when vote shares were 38% for Labour, 35% for the Conservatives and 28% for an independent candidate. Labour and the Conservatives shared the two seats on that occasion, with Margaret Marks holding her seat just eight votes ahead of the second Labour candidate.

Marks had first been elected in 2015, when Haverhill West was a ward of the predecessor St Edmundsbury council; she was the last mayor of St Edmundsbury before that council's abolition in 2019. She tendered her resignation in June, citing poor health and a complaint which had been made against her by a fellow councillor.

Defending this set for the Conservatives is Graham Cone, who has previous local government experience as a South Cambridgeshire councillor from 2015 to 2026 (representing Fulbourn ward 2015-18, and then Fen Ditton and Fulbourn ward 2018-26); after relocating to Haverhill last year, Cone was an unsuccessful candidate for Haverhill South in May's Suffolk county council election. The Labour candidate is Quinn Cox, who stood in 2023 in Haverhill North ward and in May in Haverhill East and Rural division. This time there is no independent candidate, but standing instead are Clare Higson for the Greens, James Porter for the Lib Dems and Reform UK's Heike Sowa, who was elected in 2021 as a Conservative county councillor for Haverhill Cangle but lost re-election in May in Haverhill North West and Withersfield.

Eckington South and Renishaw

North East Derbyshire council; caused by the resignation of Labour councillor Stuart Fawcett.

We now travel north to this week's two Labour defences, starting with a ward which is within the economic orbit of Sheffield but located within the East Midlands region. Eckington is a former Derbyshire pit village which lies just outside the Sheffield city boundary: the Halfway tram terminus is only a mile or two to the north. On the far side of the Rother valley - which is also traversed by the old Chesterfield-Sheffield railway line and by the now-closed Great Central main line and Chesterfield Canal - is the smaller settlement of Renishaw, which had a steelworks until the 1990s.

In between Eckington and Renishaw is the country house of Renishaw Hall, whose extensive gardens are open to the public. This was the home of the Sitwell family - who had owned the steelworks - for nearly four centuries until the death of Sir Reresby Sitwell, 7th Baronet in 2009: he bequeathed the house to his daughter in his will. Sir Reresby was from the family of the three literary Sitwells of the early 20th century: his father was Sir Sacheverell Sitwell, 6th Baronet, while Sir Osbert Sitwell, 5th Baronet and Dame Edith Sitwell were his uncle and aunt. Among other things, the Sitwells were patrons of the composer William Walton: Osbert selected and arranged the text for Walton's cantata Belshazzar's Feast, while Walton also provided the musical accompaniment for Edith's Façade poems.

The Labour façade in this corner of Derbyshire has been crumbling for some years, as the memory of coalmining fades and Eckington becomes more and more a commuter centre for Sheffield. Mind, the Labour party did have a brief revival in the middle of this decade, as they gained the North East Derbyshire parliamentary seat from the Conservatives in 2024. The new Labour MP Louise Sandher-Jones, who is a former Army captain, has quickly joined the ranks of government: in June she succeeded Al Carns as Armed Forces minister at the MoD. Sandher-Jones was a Labour councillor in Loughborough before entering the Commons, and she still has a link to that town: last year she married Jeevun Sandher, the Labour MP for Loughborough.

In 2023 Labour gained overall control of North East Derbyshire council from the Conservatives - but only narrowly, with 28 seats out of 53. The Conservatives have since gained a seat from Labour in a by-election in (of all places) Clay Cross, so Labour need to hold this by-election in order to preserve their council majority.

Eckington South and Renishaw ward had a Labour lead of 60-34 over the Conservatives in 2023, but the Conservatives had won a previous by-election here in May 2021 after one of the ward's Labour councillors, Clive Hunt, had fallen off the council for non-attendance during the pandemic. Labour had nominated Hunt for re-election in the resulting by-election, but he failed to get his seat back. The ward is split between two divisions of Derbyshire county council, Eckington and Coal Aston, and Killamarsh and Renishaw: both of those were won by Reform UK last year, with Labour finishing third behind the Conservatives in each division .

This crucial by-election follows the resignation of first-term Labour councillor Stuart Fawcett at the end of July. Fawcett is a Royal Navy veteran who had been North East Derbyshire council's Armed Forces champion. He is also an occasional contributor to GB News, and his social media suggests that he has left not only the council but also the Labour Party.

Defending for Labour is another military veteran: Eckington town councillor David Landall spent ten years in the Army before being discharged on medical grounds, and then worked as an ambulance driver. The Conservatives have reselected town councillor Philip Wheelhouse, who won the 2021 by-election here but lost re-election in 2023. The Green Party candidate is Hannah Court, Jackie Foster is standing as an independent candidate after an unsuccessful independent run at the county council last year, and the Reform UK candidate is Jamie Hodgson who is the county councillor for Eckington and Coal Aston.

Burnage

Manchester council; caused by the election of Labour councillor Bev Craig as Mayor of Greater Manchester.

The electors in our final poll this week may well be getting sick of by-elections by now, as this is their third by-election of the year - and the fourth poll here in six months, including the ordinary election for Manchester city council in May.

Burnage lies in south Manchester, generally to the east of the arterial road of Kingsway. The king referred to in the name is George V, and the construction of this road in 1928 opened up a large amount of land which Manchester Corporation quickly developed as a council estate. A large majority of Burnage ward's housing is semi-detached houses from that era, and even after decades of Right to Buy 33% of households here are still socially rented. Originally there was a tramline running along Kingsway's central reservation; today the quickest way into the city centre is the Styal railway line, with Burnage and Mauldeth Road railway stations serving the ward. The ward has a significant Asian population of around 33%; this will probably be higher at the northern end of the ward, which takes in parts of Levenshulme.

This was the part of Manchester where Noel and Liam Gallagher of Oasis grew up. In their youth the Gallaghers were regulars at the Sifters Records shop, which lies on Fog Lane at the southern end of Burnage ward. A couple of murals of them have been painted onto the shop wall in their honour.

In 2024 Burnage ward was included within the Gorton and Denton parliamentary seat, a decision which in due course set off a chain of by-elections here. The first one was in February 2026 after the seat's Labour MP Andrew Gwynne resigned, ostensibly on health grounds but his position had become untenable following revelations about a bad-taste WhatsApp group he was running with a number of Tameside councillors. The resulting by-election saw Labour lose the Gorton and Denton seat to Hannah Spencer, a plumber who had previously been the leader of the Green group on Trafford council and is now the first-ever Green Party MP in the north of England.

The following Manchester council elections in May 2026 saw the Green Party win the most seats, including all four of the Manchester wards in Spencer's constituency. All of those Green gains - and gains by Reform UK in Blackley to the north and Wythenshawe to the south - came at the expense of Labour, who nonetheless still hold a majority on Manchester council based on dominant results in 2023 and 2024. Burnage ward gave 46% to the Green Party, 29% to Labour and 17% to Reform UK - the first tine Labour had lost the ward since 2010, when (on different boundaries) it voted Lib Dem. Curiously the Green winning score of 2,257 votes was exactly the same total as the Labour council leader Bev Craig had polled here in 2024.

In the wake of this May's local elections, Labour MP Josh Simons tendered his resignation for the purpose of allowing the Mayor of Greater Manchester Andy Burnham to return to Parliament. Burnham - who had been blocked from the Labour nomination in Gorton and Denton - duly won the Makerfield by-election in June, after which he was required to resign his previous post as Mayor of Greater Manchester.

For the resulting county-wide mayoral by-election at the end of July Labour selected as their defending candidate Bev Craig, who had been a Manchester city councillor for Burnage ward since 2011 and leader of the council since 2021. Craig came close to winning the by-election in the first round, polling 47% to 21% for Reform UK's Sian Astley and 12% for the Greens' Geraldine Cogggins; after votes from Coggins and the other candidates had been redistributed, Craig beat Astley in the run-off by the lopsided margin of 66-34. Once she took office as mayor, her Manchester council seat was automatically vacated.

The Greater Manchester returning officer broke the count down into Greater Manchester's 216 wards, and the resulting figures were distributed to the party agents and have been obtained by Ben Walker of Britain Elects, who has published them at britainelects.substack.com. He deserves a lot of credit for this. We can see from this breakdown that Bev Craig performed well in her own ward, with a first-preference 58-26 lead over the Greens - rather different from Labour's 29-46 deficit less than three months previously.

With such wild recent electoral swings, who knows what will happen in this by-election for Bev Craig's former council seat? Defending for Labour is Carl Austin-Behan, who was previously a councillor for this ward from 2011 until 2018 when he was deselected by Labour's Momentum faction. Austin-Behan, who is apparently notable enough for Wikipedia, was lord mayor of Manchester in 2016-17 and had previously won the Mr Gay UK beauty contest in 2001; before then he had been kicked out of the RAF in 1997 for homosexuality, which at the time was banned in the UK armed forces. Austin-Behan has been a deputy lieutenant of Greater Manchester since 2019, and in the 2020 New Year Honours he was appointed OBE for services to charity, to LGBTQ+, to equality and to the community in Greater Manchester. The Greens have selected first-time candidate Daoud Nawaz, who gives an address at the north end of the ward. Reform UK's Heather McDonagh is back for another go after her third-place finish here in May. Completing the Burnage ballot are Abdullah Vavdiwala for the Lib Dems, Gaz Ranjha for the Workers Party and Bhupinder Kumar for the Conservatives.
