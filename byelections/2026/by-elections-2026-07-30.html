Bro'r Llynnoedd

Isle of Anglesey council, North Wales; caused by the death of Plaid Cymru councillor Ken Taylor. We'll now travel west and go offshore to the Isle of Anglesey. The ward name "Bro'r Llynnoedd" can be translated as "the District of the Lakes", which is a reference to a number of freshwater lakes at the western end of Anglesey: Llyn Dinam, Llyn Penrhyn and Llyn Traffwil close to the village of Llanfihangel yn Nhowyn, and Llyn Llywenan some distance to the north. There is also open water on the western boundary of Bro'r Llynnoedd ward, which follows the shallow strait that separates Anglesey proper from Holy Island to the west. All communication links to and from Holy Island - and on from there to Ireland, via the ferry from Holyhead - converge on the village of Valley, which is the railhead for this ward. Valley is home to the largest of the three polling stations for this by-election, the other two being at Caergeiliog on the old A5 and at Bodedern to the north. Possibly the most famous person who grew up in this area is the now-retired Welsh rugby star George North, who was actually born in England but moved to Anglesey at the age of two. North attended the secondary school in Bodedern which also educated Gareth Williams, who went on to become a mathematician with GCHQ. Williams was found dead in his London flat in 2010 in circumstances which have never been satisfactorily explained: his body was found inside a sports bag which had been locked from the outside. Defence employment of a different kind dominates the economy of Bro'r Llynnoedd ward, which ranks in the top 70 wards in England and Wales for residents employed in public administration and defence (16.7%). This is thanks to the presence of RAF Valley, a large airfield which has been in operation since 1941; it is presently used for pilot training and as the headquarters of the RAF's mountain rescue service. Among the many airmen who have passed through Valley over the years are Prince William, who learned to fly Sea King search and rescue helicopters here in 2010. RAF Valley's accommodation blocks are mostly located next to Llyn Penrhyn. Bro'r Llynnoedd ward was created in 2022, mostly from territory which was previously in Llifon ward. At the only election held here to date the ward split its two seats between independent councillor Gwilym Jones and Plaid Cymru's Ken Taylor, with topline figures of 43% for Jones, 35% for Plaid and 22% for the Conservatives. This by-election is to replace Plaid Cymru's Taylor, who suddenly died in May. The Isle of Anglesey council is run by Plaid Cymru, who topped the poll in May's Senedd election across the larger constituency of Bangor Conwy Môn: that seat returned 3 Plaid MSs, 2 Reform UK and 1 Conservative. Top of the Plaid list here was the party leader Rhun ap Iorwerth, who is now the First Minister down in Cardiff Bay. Plaid also gained the Ynys Môn Westminster seat in 2024, with the then Anglesey council leader Llinos Madi gaining her seat from the Conservatives. Anglesey council's Plaid Cymru group has slipped from 21 out of 34 councillors at the 2022 election to 17 now following defection losses and a by-election loss to Reform UK earlier this year in the neighbouring Ynys Gybi ward, so Plaid need to hold this seat to preserve their council majority. Their defending candidate is Jon Taylor, who is a grandson of the late Ken Taylor. This time there is no independent candidate so quite a lot of votes are up for grabs. The Conservatives have picked Margaret John, who was their losing candidate in the Ynys Gybi by-election in February, and we have interventions from two parties who were not on the ballot haere in 2022: the Greens' Nick Bounds and Reform UK's Richard Jones were both unsuccessful candidates for the Senedd here in May. Long Sutton

South Holland council, Lincolnshire; caused by the death of independent councillor David Wilkinson. Now for something completely different, as we transfer into the flat land of the Fens. The small market town of Long Sutton lies not far off the south coast of the Wash, the large bay which lies between Lincolnshire and Norfolk, and it is on the main road running along the south side of the Wash. Its largest employer is a factory making tinned food for the Princes group, and this gives Long Sutton a rather working-class economic profile. Other villages within the ward include Lutton to the north and Tydd St Mary to the south, close to the tripoint of Lincolnshire, Norfolk and Cambridgeshire which lies in the River Nene. Since the present ward boundaries were introduced in 2007, Long Sutton ward has been entirely fought in South Holland council elections between Conservatives and independent candidates. The independents normally have the upper hand, including at the last by-election here in August 2012, but Long Sutton ward generally returns a mix of Conservatives and independents - and that political split was guaranteed when the 2023 candidate list came out and there were two Conservatives and two independent candidates standing for the three available seats. In the end the votes split 50-50 between the two groups and the three outgoing councillors - independents Andrew Tennant and David Wilkinson, and the Conservatives' Jack Tyrrell - were all re-elected. Tenannt and Wilkinson were not affiliated to the South Holland Independents, which emerged as the main opposition to the Conservatives here at the 2023 election. Several of the South Holland Independent and Conservative councillors have since joined Reform UK, which did well in this area at the 2025 Lincolnshire county elections. Long Sutton itself is part of The Suttons division, which was close three ways between RUK, the Conservatives and the South Holland Independents; Reform UK won the Holbeach Rural division which covers the outlying villages of this ward more convincingly. Reform UK were also second in July 2024 in the South Holland and The Deepings constituency, which has only had one MP since its creation in 1997: the Conservatives' Sir John Hayes was re-elected two years ago for his eighth term of office. Before then we need to replace David Wilkinson as a South Holland councillor following his death in May at the age of 71. Wilkinson was a long-serving member of the council who was first elected for Long Sutton ward in 2007; he lost his seat to the Conservatives in 2015, but got it back in 2019. Wilkinson was also a parish councillor in Lutton at the time of his death. This by-election will see a change in council composition, because no new independent candidate has come forward to replace Wilkinson. The Conservatives, who will recover their overall majority on South Holland council if they gain this seat, have selected Naomi Andrews who is a local business owner. The Greens' James Timpson is an actor and former farmworker, and Reform UK's Robbie Woods already represents most of this ward as the Lincolnshire county councillor for The Suttons. He completes a ballot paper of three candidates. Great Dunmow North

Uttlesford council, Essex; caused by the death of Residents for Uttlesford councillor Alex Armstrong. Now, here's a challenge for those of you who are madly in love with your partner. Go to Dunmow for the next quadrennial Flitch trials in 2028 and claim your prize. All you and your other half have to do is convince a jury that you have been married for twelve months and never had an argument or regretted your marriage in that time, and you could win a Flitch of Bacon. The Dunmow Flitch Trials have been going for an extremely long time - there are references to them in the fourteenth-century Wife of Bath's Tale from Chaucer's Canterbury Tales - but bringing home the bacon is not as easy as it sounds, because the organisers will hire proper legal counsel to cross-examine you. And, this being the 21st century rather than the 14th, you might also end up as a star of YouTube. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ATOb7MPCnBM The Flitch trials take place in the Essex town of Great Dunmow, a market town located on a Roman road running west from Colchester. The Roman road has been superseded by the A120, which now bypasses Great Dunmow to the south to provide faster connections from eastern Essex to Stansted Airport. Great Dunmow is divided into two electoral wards, with North ward being residential - and getting more so, as the current map of this ward shows a lot of new housing development. In the 2021 census - which might well be out of date by now given all that new development - Great Dunmow North just made the top 100 wards in England and Wales for Level 2 qualifications (5+ GCSEs or equivalent), which are held by 17.8% of the adult population. Stansted Airport is the major employer in the Uttlesford local government district, which is named after an ancient hundred of north-western Essex and is based in Saffron Walden. Uttlesford district has similar boundaries to the North West Essex parliamentary seat represented by the Conservative Party leader Kemi Badenoch, who was first elected in 2017 for the predecessor seat of Saffron Walden. Badenoch's Conservatives haven't done so well here in recent local elections. In May the Dunmow division of Essex county council voted for Reform UK. And the Conservatives haven't run Uttlesford council since 2019, when it was gaiend by a localist party called R4U - or, to give the party its full title, Residents for Uttlesford. R4U have recently lost their majority on the council as a reuslt of defections to Reform UK, but they continue to run the show. Residents for Uttlesford have topped the poll in Great Dunmow North ward at the last two local elections, but in 2023 the ward split its representation: R4U and the Conservatives both polled 39% of the vote and won one seat each, with Labour coming in some way behind on 12%. The re-elected R4U councillor was Alex Armstrong, who died in May at the age of 77. Armstrong's political career was most definitely not pointless: he was one of the founder members of Residents for Uttlesford, and during their first term in 2019-23 he was the Uttlesford councillor responsible for sports and leisure. The prize in this by-election might not be a flitch of bacon, but that hasn't put off our five candidates. The defending Residents for Uttlesford candidate is Sandi Merifield, who is seeking to get back onto the council after losing her seat in the neighbouring Felsted and Stebbing ward in 2023; Merifield was also the R4U candidate here in May's Essex county council election, and she will need to seriously improve on her third-place performance then to be in contention here. The Conservatives' Will Church, who runs a contracting firm, was the runner-up here in 2023 and he is back for another go. Labour have selected Oliver Rogers, a former civil servant who now has a senior role in the NHS and is the clerk to a number of parishes in the area. Also on the ballot are Lorraine Flawn for the Lib Dems and Rod Jones, who was a Residents for Uttlesford councillor for Great Dunmow South and Barnston ward from 2019 to 2023 but now has the Reform UK nomination. Hedge End South

Eastleigh council, Hampshire; caused by the death of Liberal Democrat councillor Cynthia Garton. Our final by-election is a repeat of a ward which appeared in this column in May 2025. It's in the Eastleigh district of Hampshire, which has a very long history of Liberal Democrat control. Indeed Keith House is one of the UK's longest-serving council leaders, having run Eastleigh council continuously since 1994. House is one of the three councillors for Hedge End South ward. Lying immediately to the east of Southampton, Hedge End has grown very rapidly in population since the completion of the M27 motorway in the 1970s: it was only promoted to the status of a town in 1992, and it is still expanding. Most of the space in Hedge End South ward has now been developed for housing, and the ward has high levels of owner-occupation. The explosive population growth in Hedge End and other Southampton suburbs has often caused problems for the Parliamentary boundary commission, and the boundaries of the Eastleigh and Winchester constituencies tend to change dramatically at each redistribution. The most recent boundary review in 2024 transferred Hedge End out of the Eastleigh seat into a new parliamentary constituency called Hamble Valley, which also takes in the western half of Fareham borough. Fareham borough has traditionally voted strongly for the Conservatives, and this boundary change was good news for the outgoing Conservative MP for Eastleigh Paul Holmes: he got the Conservative nomination for Hamble Valley, and he was re-elected fairly comfortably in 2024 while his old seat fell to the Liberal Democrats. Local elections here tell a rather different story. This is a strongly Lib Dem ward of Eastleigh council, which in May gave 43% of the vote to the Lib Dems against 27% for Reform UK and 21% for the Conservatives. At the simultaneous election in May for Hampshire county council, the larger county division of Hedge End and West End South turned in a similar result. Those polls took place a few days after the death of Eastleigh councillor Cynthia Garton, who was first elected in 2016 for the predecessor ward of Hedge End St John's and transferred here in 2018 following boundary changes. Garton, who had also been a Hedge End town councillor since 1999, was the borough's mayoress in 2008-09 and mayor in 2021-22. The mayoral chain of office does come with responsibilities, and Garton was in the chair for the marathon Eastleigh council meeting in 2021 to consider the planning application for Southampton Airport's runway extension: that meeting eventually ran for 22 hours over two days of debate. Defending the resulting by-election for the Liberal Democrats is Jim Paine, who is the vice-chair of Hedge End town council. Reform UK have changed their candidate to Neil Fleming, who stood in May for Eastleigh council and for the county council in Hamble without success. The Conservatives have again selected Jerry Hall, a businessman who has been contesting local elections here since 1995: Hall previously represented the Hedge End St John's ward on Eastleigh council from 2000 to 2006 and again from 2015 to 2018. The Greens' Keith Day - who was the Labour candidate here in last year's by-election - and Labour's Will Banks complete an all-male ballot paper.
