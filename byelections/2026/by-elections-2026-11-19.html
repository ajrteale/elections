Portchester Wicor

Fareham council, Hampshire; caused by the resignation of Liberal Democrat councillor Rob Turner. This week's other Liberal Democrat defence is on the coast of Portsmouth Harbour. This is overlooked from the east by Portsmouth itself, from the west by Gosport, and from the north by Portchester which is older than either of those settlements. Portchester was known to the Romans as Portus Aderni, a Saxon Short fort on the waterfront which is described as the best-preserved Roman fort north of the Alps: the Roman fort walls now enclose a Norman church and a mediaeval castle with plenty of room to spare. But Portchester has faded somewhat into obscurity over the centuries. Royal Mail don't even recognise it as a town, listing all addresses here as "Fareham"; and only locals can discern the dividing lines between Portchester, Fareham and Portsmouth within the built-up area. Portchester Wicor ward takes its name from a primary school and a recreation ground, which lies at the western end of the ward overlooking Portsmouth Harbour. It generally covers the west of the town but it has little resemblance to the previous Portchester West ward. Fareham council got new ward boundaries in 2024 which were quite radical in the Portchester area, and Wicor ward takes in territory from both of the previous Portchester West and East wards - rather more from East ward, which had three councillors despite the fact that Fareham is one of the few English districts which elects half of its councillors every two years. As this column noted when previewing the old Portchester East in October 2022 (Andrew's Previews 2022, page 458), this meant that that ward had to alternate between returning one and two councillors. Fareham is now one of the longest-standing Conservative-run councils, having had a Tory majority continuously since 1999: the latest composition has 22 Conservatives against 6 Lib Dems plus this vacancy, and one seat each for Labour, Reform UK and independent. Reform UK's failure to break through here in May must have been rather frustrating for the Fareham and Waterlooville MP Suella Braverman, who had defected to Farage's party in January. Braverman was first elected in 2015 for the predecessor Fareham constituency, and she served in Cabinet from 2020 to 2023 (with breaks of six months in 2021 for maternity leave and six days in the chaos between the Truss and Sunak governments): she was Attorney General for most of the Johnson Administration, before rising to become Truss's and then Sunak's first Home Secretary. Portchester is generally the Lib Dem hotspot in Fareham borough, and Wicor ward has returned Lib Dems in both elections held here to date. The Lib Dems also hold the wider Hampshire county division of Fareham Portchester. However, the Lib Dem hold of Wicor ward in May rested on a perfect right-wing vote split: the new Lib Dem candidate Rob Turner won with just 33% of the vote, while the Reform UK and Conservative candidates polled 30% each. Rob Turner did not last long as a Fareham councillor. He quit the Lib Dem council group in July, citing the demands of group membership as detrimental to his health. Turner had indicated at the time that he inteded to stay on to serve his constituents as an independent councillor, but in the end he tendered his resignation from the council in October. The Liberal Democrats will want their seat back and they have selected Steve Shaw, who is a retired Royal Navy commander. The Reform UK candidate is Paul Kimber, who stood in Avenue ward in May and has previously worked as a bus driver, driving instructor and delivery driver. The Conservatives' Harry Davis previously appeared in this column when he won the 2022 Portchester East by-election while he was still a teenager; Davis lost re-election in 2024 in Wallington and Downend ward, and he is now seeking to return. Two other candidates have come forward: they are Lorraine Drinkwater for Labour and George Ponsford for Restore Britain. Bryn

Conwy council, North Wales; caused by the resignation of Conservative councillor Jacob Williams. Today's other Welsh by-election is on the north coast, where the mountains of Snowdonia fall down to the sea. This happens most dramatically at the headland of Pen-y-clip between Penmaenmawr and Llanfairfechan, which is cut through by a series of tunnels carrying the North Wales Coast railway line and the two carriageways of the A55 road. Above here, the hillside bears the scars of the granite quarrying which was the traditional industry of Llanfairfechan. The ward name bryn is a generic Welsh word for a hill, but here it may also be a reference to the Bryn y Neuadd hospital, a mental health unit which is Llanfairfechan's largest employer. The hospital propels Bryn ward into the top 40 in England and Wales (and the top 10 in Wales) for residents working in the health and social sector, at 26.0% of adults. Bryn is the western of Llanfairfechan's two wards, taking in the whole of the seafront and also the town's railway station. Much of the upland, southern part of the ward is part of the Eryri national park. The local authority here is Conwy council, which has been hung ever since Welsh local government was last reorganised in 1996. The 2022 elections here returned 22 independent councillors, 11 Labour, 10 Conservatives, 7 Plaid Cymru councillors, 4 Lib Dems and a Green; the current administration is a coalition of the main independent group, Labour and Plaid Cymru. This means that Bryn ward is currently in the opposition on the council. This ward was a rare Conservative gain in the 2022 Welsh local elections, with Jacob Williams levering out long-serving Labour councillor Andrew Hinchliff by just seven votes, 230 to 223. Hinchliff had been unopposed in 2017 and enjoyed big majorities in this ward before then. Vote shares here in 2022 were 34% for the Conservatives, 33% for Labour and 17% for an independent candidate. Things have gone less well for the local Conservatives since then. In 2024 the Bangor Aberconwy Westminster seat was won by Labour candidate Claire Hughes, who is now a junior Welsh Office minister. However, Labour failed to win a seat here in the 2026 Senedd election, when the Bangor Conwy Môn constituency returned three MSs from Plaid Cymru, two for Reform UK and one Conservative; top of the Plaid Cymru list was the party leader, and now First Minister, Rhun ap Iorwerth. The Tories have already lost two Conwy council seats to Reform UK in by-elections this term, and they now have a third seat to defend after Jacob Williams resigned from the council in October. He is taking up a new job with the emergency services. Defending Bryn ward for the Conservatives is Rodney Davies. Interestingly there is no Labour candidate in this by-election so quite a lot of votes are up for grabs. Two new independent candidates have come forward, both of whom are Llanfairfechan town councillors: Rhys Griffiths is a lecturer and coffee shop owner, while Nia Jones was mayor of Llanfairfechan in 2025-26. Town councillor Leena Farhat is the Liberal Democrat candidate, and we have interventions from the right in the form of Robert Craven for Reform UK and Tony Priestley for Restore Britain. With the exception of Farhat - who finished in fifth and last place here in 2022 and was top of the Lib Dem list for Bangor Conwy Môn in May - none of the candidates have previously sought election to Conwy council or higher office. Craghead and South Moor

Durham council; caused by the resignation of Reform UK councillor Aaron Hillam. We'll finish with the week's only Reform UK defence, which is on the north Durham coalfield. Craghead and South Moor are two former mining villages just to the south of the town of Stanley, and they were incorporated into Stanley in the 1930s. South Moor in particular is a southern extension of Stanley, with the housing ending at the Memorial Park which was dedicated in 1920 to commemorate employees of the South Moor Colliery Company who had died in the First World War. This ward also includes the Stanley suburb of Oxhill together with the intriguingly-named villages of Quaking Houses (which consists of one long street with terraced housing either side) and New Kyo (kyo is an old Durham dialect word for cow). South Moor and Quaking Houses in particular form a seriously deprived census district which has never really recovered from the end of coalmining. Stanley had attempted in the 1960s to diversify away from coalmining with the opening of a large factory making Ever Ready batteries, but this factory also failed to make it into the 21st century. Previous elections for this area form a good example of how far Labour have fallen in County Durham. Craghead and South Moor returned only Labour councillors from at least 2005 up until 2021, normally very comfortably: in the days when Derwentside council was the local authority, South Moor ward was often left uncontested. But the 2025 Durham council election turned that on its head, as Craghead and South Moor became part of the Reform UK majority on the council - and very comfortably so. The winning Reform UK slate here last year thrashed Labour by 58-26. Stanley has been part of the North Durham parliamentary seat since 1983. The last general election was before the Reform UK surge in County Durham really got going, meaning that North Durham retained its status in 2024 as a safe Labour seat. It is currently represented by first-term backbencher Luke Akehurst, a long-standing figure on the right of the Labour party who has been on and off the party's National Executive Committee over the years. Akehurst might well have entered Parliament some years ago had he not developed some serious health problems, which have left him using a walking stick to get about. Second place here in 2024 went to Reform UK's Andrew Husband, who is now the leader of Durham council; his deputy leader is GB News favourite Darren Grimes, who represents the neighbouring ward of Annfield Plain and Tanfield. The 2025 Durham elections returned 65 Reform UK councillors against 14 Lib Dems, 12 independents, just 4 Labour (who had come second in the popular vote), 2 Greens and 1 Conservative. Reform UK have subsequently lost two by-elections, one each to the Lib Dems and Labour, and it's fair to say that their council group has not remained united. A series of defections have left the Reform UK group on 50 out of 98 seats at the last count, plus this vacancy. They can afford to lose this by-election, but one more defection loss will tip Durham into no overall control. The outgoing councillor here is Aaron Hillam, who resigned last month because he has moved away from the county. To replace him in this by-election Reform UK have selected Mark Taylor, whose first job was in management at the Ever Ready factory. The Labour candidate is Les Timbey, who was a rather distant runner-up here last year but did manage to beat his party colleague (and sitting councillor) from higher up the alphabetical ballot paper. Also standing are Victoria Aymard for the Lib Dems, Dorothy Luckhurst for the Conservatives, Callum Hutchinson for the Greens and Jean Thomson for Restore Britain.
