Letchworth North

Hertfordshire county council; and

Letchworth Norton

North Hertfordshire council; both caused by the resignation of Tina Bhartwas.

We're going to start this week's column proper with a preview of two by-elections in the world's first Garden City. Letchworth began life in 1903 under design principles laid out by Ebenezer Howard, at a handy location on the railway line from London King's Cross to Cambridge and close to the Great North Road.

Although it has to be said that not all of Letchworth's housing was built on the original garden-city principles. This particularly applies to the Grange council estate in the north of the town, which was built in the late 1940s on land which Letchworth Urban District Council had compulsorily purchased from the garden city developers. The Grange is one of several UK housing estates inspired by the housing of Radburn, New Jersey, an American variant of the garden-city idea dating from 1929. Radburn's original planners had wanted to separate road traffic from pedestrian spaces to reduce accidents, but in practice the design has lent itself to crime and antisocial behaviour giving Radburn-style housing a bad reputation. Ebezener Howard would surely not have approved.

The Grange estate is divided from the town centre by the open space of Norton Common, which is home to a large population of black squirrels. These are an unusual sight in the UK, and are seen as sufficiently locally distinctive that a black squirrel appears on Letchworth's coat of arms. The original village of Norton, which was here long before the garden city development, now lies at the north-east corner of the town's built-up area.

There's clearly a lot of local pride in Letchworth and its history, with the town and the railway station officially changing their name to "Letchworth Garden City" around the time of the town's centenary in 2003. However, this only gets you so far. In 2005 a town council was created for Letchworth Garden City, but this was then abolished just eight years later after candidates opposed to the town council won a majority at the 2009 election.

The abolition of Letchworth Garden City town council brought control back to North Hertfordshire council, which is based in the town and also takes in Hitchin, Baldock, Royston and associated rural areas to the north and west of Stevenage. North Hertfordshire had an all-out council election in 2024, after which it did not go back to its previous thirds elections cycle. The council has been hung since 2019. A Labour-Lib Dem coalition was in control going into the 2024 elections, but Labour came out of that election one seat short of an overall majority and promptly dumped their coalition partners to rule alone as a minority.

Letchworth Norton ward was a new creation for the 2024 election, taking in territory which had previously been mostly in Letchworth East or Letchworth Grange wards. It returned the Labour slate of Daniel Allen and Tina Bhartwas quite comfortably in May 2024, with 45% of the vote against 29% for the Conservatives and 15% for the Green Party.

Two months later Labour gained the North East Hertfordshire parliamentary seat for the first time, after long-serving Conservative MP Sir Oliver Heald retired. The Conservatives put up as their candidate Nikki da Costa, who had been a Downing Street special advisor to May and Johnson and had also been an unsuccessful candidate for North Hertfordshire council in May 2024; but she ended up losing to Labour's Chris Hinchliff, who was then a councillor for Royston and was working as the campaigns and policy officer for the Campaign to Protect Rural England. Hinchliff lost the Labour whip for a time in 2025 for rebelling against the Starmer government's welfare and planning policy, but he is now back in the fold.

Tina Bhartwas was a new face on North Hertfordshire council in 2024, but she had served since 2021 as the county councillor for the tight marginal division of Letchworth North - gaining her seat from the Conservatives with a majority of just 46 votes or 1 percentage point. She was re-elected for a second term on the county council in May 2025 with a much reduced vote share but an increased majority, thanks to vote fragmentation: Bhartwas had 30% of the vote to 26% for Reform UK, 22% for the Conservatives and 12% for the Liberal Democrats. Letchworth North was one of five seats which Labour won on Hertfordshire county council last year.

This didn't last. Just three weeks after her re-election to the county council Tina Bhartwas defected from Labour to the Liberal Democrats, again blaming the Starmer government's welfare policy. In October this year she resigned both of her council seats and moved to Scotland, where she is taking up a postgraduate degree course in ancient cultures at the University of Glasgow.

The Local Elections Archive Project will take Labour as the defending party in these by-elections, based on the results of the last ordinary elections. In the Letchworth North county by-election the Labour candidate is Laura Williams, who is a North Hertfordshire councillor for the Stevenage suburb of Great Ashby and sits on that council's cabinet. The Lib Dems will be hoping to convert their defection gain into a by-election gain with Claire Winchester, who represents Letchworth South West ward on the district council and finished in fourth place here last year. On that occasion Winchester defeated Reform UK candidate Gary Warren, who subsequently fought Letchworth South in last year's county elections and is now standing here; Warren was previously the Brexit Party candidate for Luton South at the 2019 general election. Hoping to make a political comeback in Letchworth North is the Conservatives' Simon Bloxham, who represented this county division from 2017 to 2021 and also sat on the district council for Letchworth Grange ward from 2021 to 2024. A ballot paper of six candidates is completed by Brett Dietrich for the Greens and Neil Almond for Restore Britain.

We also have a field of six for the North Hertfordshire council by-election in Letchworth Norton ward. Here the Labour candidate is Indie Sunner, who stood in 2024 in the grandly-named ward of Graveley, St Ippolyts and Wymondley; she then fought Hitchin South in last year's county elections. Or if we take the defending party as the Lib Dems, then their candidate to hold this seat is Timothy Oughton who is an engineer working in the food industry. Simon Bloxham does double duty for the Conservatives here. The Greens have selected Hannah Tattersall, who is a teaching assistant. And intervening from the right-wing side of politics are Reform UK's Steven Adelantado, a train driver who was Reform UK's parliamentary candidate for North East Hertfordshire in 2024; and Neil Almond again for Restore Britain.

Emsworth

Havant council, Hampshire; caused by the resignation of Green Party councillor Charles Robert.

We now come to two by-elections in wards which face onto the harbours of the Solent coast, either side of Portsmouth. To the north-east of the city we find Emsworth, which is located on the A27 road and the West Coastway railway line towards Brighton and is the last village in Hampshire before the border into Sussex is crossed. Emsworth was a small port at the point where the River Ems (which is named after the town) empties into Chichester Harbour. It has an old history - a field off the Havant Road in the west of the town hides the remains of a Roman villa - but those who have read PG Wodehouse and are looking for Blandings Castle here will be disappointed. Lord Emsworth was entirely fictional.

Emsworth has a town hall, but it has never been an independent town. In 1895 it was included in an urban district named after nearby Warblington, before being incorporated into Havant in the 1930s. Indeed, Emsworth Town Hall has never been in municipal use. It was originally built in 1790 as a chapel, and for much of the 20th century the building was home to a cinema. The Town Hall building currently trades as the Greenhouse Café.

Havant council continues to be the local authority, and this is an area where the Conservative vote collapse has been unusually recent and unusually fast. The Tories kept control of Havant council continuously until 2024, when an all-out council election resulted in a hung council and a Labour-led traffic-light coalition taking over. Those Havant elections also saw the return of the first Reform UK local councillors who were elected under that label.

If anything Havant council is even more fragmented following the 2026 local elections, in which Reform UK won nine of the 12 seats up for election. That puts Reform UK as the largest party on the council, with 9 seats at the last count out of a possible 36; there are 7 Labour councillors, 5 Greens plus this vacancy, 5 Lib Dems, 5 Conservatives and 4 independents (two ex-Conservative, one ex-Lib Dem, one ex-Reform UK).

These numbers mean that control of the council could well rest on this by-election. With 19 seats needed for a majority, a Conservative or Reform UK gain might give one of the right-wing parties the numbers to take control if they can win enough of the independent councillors over to that idea. The traffic-light coalition of Labour, Lib Dems and Greens had initially continued in office after the May 2026 election, but the Green councillors have recently been sacked from the administration for failing to meet a Labour demand to repudiate the controversial "Zionism is Racism" motion passed at the Green Party conference last month.

Which puts a little bit of extra spice into this by-election in Emsworth, which is Havant's Green Party hotspot. Emsworth had been a safe Conservative ward until the Greens suddenly gained it in 2023; the Green Party slate then won all three seats very comfortably at the 2024 all-out elections.

In May, Emsworth ward's Green councillor Charles Robert was re-elected with 49% of the vote against 28% for the Conservatives and 22% for Reform UK. There was an electoral pact in force at this year's Havant elections, with the (then) three parties in the ruling coalition not standing candidates against each other. This poll was combined with the Hampshire county council election in the larger division of Emsworth and St Faiths, which was the only Hampshire division to return a Green county councillor this year. Charles Robert tendered his resignation in October, five months after being re-elected for his second term.

Having won every single Havant ward in 2021, the Conservatives lost the lot in 2026. This must be concerning for Havant's Conservative MP Alan Mak, who was re-elected for his fourth term of office in 2024 only 92 votes ahead of the Labour candidate, in one of the closest results of the election. Mak had served on the lower rungs of government in the Treasury under Boris Johnson and in the business department under Sunak, and Badenoch promoted him to the Shadow Cabinet in late 2024 as shadow DSIT secretary, but he left the frontbench last year to focus on constituency matters. It would appear from these results that he hasn't made much progress on that front so far.

Defending this seat for the Green Party is Katharine Hulls, who contested Hayling East ward over the water in May. The Conservatives have reselected Andrew Proctor who was runner-up here in May: Proctor has recently moved to Havant from Norfolk where he has long experience in local government, representing Blofield and Brundall division on Norfolk county council from 2009 to 2026 and also sitting for Brundall ward on Broadland council from 2004 to 2019. Reform UK have recruited Lucy Lawrence, who was a losing Conservative candidate for this ward in 2023 and 2024; she stood for Reform UK in Havant St Faith's ward in May. And it would appear that the disintegration of Havant council's ruling coalition has also ended the electoral pact, as Labour have nominated Simon Hagan while the Lib Dems have selected Iain Sutherland for this by-election.

Portchester Wicor

Fareham council, Hampshire; caused by the resignation of Liberal Democrat councillor Rob Turner.

We now travel a few miles to the west along the A27 and M27, from the northern shore of Chichester Harbour to the northern shore of Portsmouth Harbour. This body of water is overlooked from the east by Portsmouth itself, from the west by Gosport, and from the north by Portchester which is older than either of those settlements. Portchester was known to the Romans as Portus Adurni, a Saxon Shore fort on the waterfront which is described as the best-preserved Roman fort north of the Alps. The Roman fort walls, which mostly still stand, now enclose a Norman church and a mediaeval castle with plenty of room to spare.

But Portchester has faded somewhat into obscurity over the centuries. Royal Mail don't even recognise it as a town, listing all addresses here as "Fareham"; and only locals can discern the dividing lines between Portchester, Fareham and Portsmouth within the continuously built-up area. Perhaps it doesn't help that, unlike in Emsworth, Portchester has no room to grow to the north where the land rises steeply to the chalk ridge of Portsdown Hill.

Portchester Wicor ward takes its name from a primary school and a recreation ground, which lies at the western end of the ward overlooking Portsmouth Harbour. It generally covers the west of the town but it has little resemblance to the Portchester West ward which existed at the time of the 2021 census. Fareham council got new ward boundaries in 2024 which were quite radical in the Portchester area, and Wicor ward takes in territory from both of the previous Portchester West and East wards - rather more from East ward, which had three councillors despite the fact that Fareham is one of the few English districts which elects half of its councillors every two years. As this column noted when previewing the old Portchester East in October 2022 (Andrew's Previews 2022, page 458), this meant that that ward had to alternate between returning one and two councillors.

Fareham is now one of the longest-standing Conservative-run councils, having had a Tory majority continuously since 1999: the latest composition has 22 Conservatives against 6 Lib Dems plus this vacancy, and one seat each for Labour, Reform UK and independent. Reform UK's failure to seriously break through here in May must have been rather frustrating for the Fareham and Waterlooville MP Suella Braverman, who had defected to Farage's party in January. Braverman was first elected in 2015 for the predecessor Fareham constituency, and she served in Cabinet from 2020 to 2023 (with breaks of six months in 2021 for maternity leave and six days in the chaos between the Truss and Sunak governments). She was Attorney General for most of the Johnson administration, before rising to become Truss's and then Sunak's first Home Secretary.

Portchester is generally the Lib Dem hotspot in Fareham borough, and Wicor ward has returned Lib Dems in both elections held here to date. The Lib Dems also hold the wider Hampshire county division of Fareham Portchester. However, the Lib Dem hold of Wicor ward in May rested on a perfect right-wing vote split: the new Lib Dem candidate Rob Turner won with just 33% of the vote, while the Reform UK and Conservative candidates polled 30% each.

Rob Turner did not last long as a Fareham councillor. He quit the Lib Dem council group in July, citing the demands of group membership as detrimental to his health. Turner had indicated at the time that he intended to stay on to serve his constituents as an independent councillor, but in the end he tendered his resignation from the council in October.

The Liberal Democrats will want their seat back and they have selected Steve Shaw, who is a retired Royal Navy commander. The Reform UK candidate is Paul Kimber, who stood in Avenue ward in May and has previously worked as a bus driver, driving instructor and delivery driver. The Conservatives' Harry Davis previously appeared in this column when he won the 2022 Portchester East by-election while he was still a teenager; Davis lost re-election in 2024 in Wallington and Downend ward, and he is now seeking to return. Two other candidates have come forward: they are Lorraine Drinkwater for Labour and George Ponsford (who gives an address over the border in Winchester district) for Restore Britain.

Risca East

Caerphilly council, Gwent; caused by the death of Labour councillor Nigel George.

We have two by-elections to bring you this week in Wales, one of which is in the Sirhowy valley. Here we find the town of Risca, which lies a few miles to the north-west of Newport. Like many Valleys towns Risca was a coalmining centre back in the day, but the coal here had been all but exhausted by the 1960s when the Ty-Sign estate which forms the basis of Risca East ward was built; Ty-Sign was instead intended to provide housing for workers at the Llanwern steelworks in Newport. Risca East continues to be quite a long way down the social scale when compared with Rogerstone, a Newport suburb which is the next town down the valley.

The derelict Monmouthshire and Brecon Canal winds its way through the Ty-Sign estate. The southern boundary of Risca East ward is the old railway line to Ebbw Vale, which was reopened for passengers in 2008. Risca and Pontymister railway station now has regular trains to both Newport and Cardiff.

This area is politically associated with the former Labour Party leader Neil Kinnock, who represented Risca from 1970 to 1995 as part of the Bedwellty and then the Islwyn constituency. The latter seat was broken up by the 2024 boundary review and Risca was transferred into an expanded Newport West seat, which was renamed as Newport West and Islwyn: this is represented by Labour MP Ruth Jones, who is chair of the Commons Welsh Affairs select committee.

Risca East ward is part of the Labour majority on Caerphilly council, and it has returned a full slate of three Labour councillors at every local election this century. There were two by-elections here in the 2012-17 term, after the 2012 election in Risca East had returned Labour councillors Stan Jenkins, Rhianon Passmore and new face Nigel George. Jenkins died in 2013 and the by-election to replace him returned Philippa Leonard. In 2016 Rhianon Passmore was elected as the Labour member of what was then the Welsh Assembly for Islwyn and resigned her council seat; the resulting by-election in October 2016 (Andrew's Previews 2016, page 226) returned her daughter Arianna Passmore. Arianna subsequently married Philippa's son, becoming Arianna Leonard. Nigel George and the two Leonards were re-elected as Risca East's Labour councillors in 2022, defeating the Plaid Cymru slate by 63-37 in a straight fight.

The most recent poll here was the 2026 Senedd election in the Casnewydd Islwyn constituency, which combines the Sirhowy valley with the whole of the city of Newport. This split its six seats four ways, with two seats each for Reform UK and Plaid Cymru, and one seat each for Labour and the Conservatives. The surviving Labour MS was Jayne Bryant, who had been the cabinet secretary for housing and local government going into the election; Rhianon Passmore, who had been ranked second on the Labour list, lost her seat. Top of the Reform UK list here was Dan Thomas, a former Conservative leader of Barnet council in London who briefly became leader of the Senedd Reform UK group; but he was forced to stand down from the top job four months later after being arrested on suspicion of assault and controlling or coercive behaviour.

The electors of Risca East are now being called out for a by-election to replace Nigel George, who died in September. Like many residents of Ty-Sign, George had spent his working life in the steel industry; while his 14 years of service on Caerphilly council included over a decade in the council's cabinet. This will be the last poll for Risca East ward, as boundary changes for the 2027 Caerphilly council election will merge this ward with Risca West. Awkwardly, the new single Risca ward will have four councillors whereas the two predecessor wards have a total of five, so whoever wins this by-election might well need to do some fancy footwork to get a berth on the ballot paper for next May's election.

Labour are seeking to keep this in the family by selecting as their defending candidate Marcus Leonard, a civil servant and former postman who is Philippa's son and Arianna's husband. Plaid Cymru have reselected Brandon Ham, who was runner-up here in 2022 and subsequently stood in Newport West and Islwyn at the 2024 Westminster election. This time there is much more choice for the electors with interventions from the Greens' Tracy Barrington (who has the Welsh-only ballot paper description "Plaid Werdd"), Reform UK's Darren Crandon, the Conservatives' Ceithin Dennes and independent candidate Peter Heathcote, a retired business owner who has recently been co-opted to Risca East community council.

Bryn

Conwy council, North Wales; caused by the resignation of Conservative councillor Jacob Williams.

Today's other Welsh by-election is on the north coast, where the mountains of Snowdonia fall down to the sea. This happens most dramatically at the headland of Pen-y-clip between Penmaenmawr and Llanfairfechan, which is cut through by a series of tunnels carrying the North Wales Coast railway line and the two carriageways of the A55 road. Above here, the hillside bears the scars of the granite quarrying which was the traditional industry of Llanfairfechan.

The ward name bryn is a generic Welsh word for a hill, but here it may also be a reference to the Bryn y Neuadd hospital, a mental health unit which is Llanfairfechan's largest employer. The hospital propels Bryn ward into the top 40 wards in England and Wales (and the top 10 in Wales) for residents working in the health and social care sector, at 26.0% of adults. Bryn is the western of Llanfairfechan's two wards, taking in the whole of the seafront and also the town's railway station. Much of the upland, southern part of the ward is part of the Eryri national park.

The local authority here is Conwy council, which has been hung ever since its creation in 1996. The 2022 elections here returned 22 independent councillors, 11 Labour, 10 Conservatives, 7 Plaid Cymru councillors, 4 Lib Dems and a Green; the current administration is a coalition of the main independent group, Labour and Plaid Cymru.

This means that Bryn ward is currently in the opposition on the council. This ward was a rare Conservative gain in the 2022 Welsh local elections, with Jacob Williams levering out long-serving Labour councillor Andrew Hinchliff by just seven votes, 230 to 223. Hinchliff had been unopposed in 2017 and enjoyed big majorities in this ward before then. Vote shares here in 2022 were 34% for the Conservatives, 33% for Labour and 17% for an independent candidate.

Things have gone less well for the local Conservatives since then. In 2024 the Bangor Aberconwy Westminster seat was won by Labour candidate Claire Hughes, who is now a junior Welsh Office minister. However, Labour failed to win a seat here in the 2026 Senedd election, when the Bangor Conwy Môn constituency returned three MSs from Plaid Cymru, two for Reform UK and one Conservative; top of the Plaid Cymru list was the party leader, and now First Minister, Rhun ap Iorwerth.

The Tories have already lost two Conwy council seats to Reform UK in by-elections this year (both in Llandudno, and both results confirming defection losses), and they now have a third seat to defend after Jacob Williams resigned from the council in October. He is taking up a new job with the emergency services.

Defending Bryn ward for the Conservatives is Rodney Davies. Interestingly there is no Labour candidate in this by-election so quite a lot of votes are up for grabs. Two new independent candidates have come forward, both of whom are Llanfairfechan town councillors: Rhys Griffiths is a lecturer and coffee shop owner, while Nia Jones was mayor of Llanfairfechan in 2025-26. Town councillor Leena Farhat is the Liberal Democrat candidate, and we have interventions from the right in the form of Robert Craven for Reform UK and Tony Priestley for Restore Britain. Apart from Farhat, who finished in fifth and last place here in 2022 and was top of the Lib Dem list for Bangor Conwy Môn in May, none of the candidates have previously sought election to Conwy council or higher office.

Huntington and Hatherton

South Staffordshire council; caused by the death of Labour councillor Jeff Ashley.

We now cross into Staffordshire for another ward which lies on the road to Holyhead - in this case the A5, which connects that port to London. Most of the A5 is an upgrade of the old Roman road of Watling Street, which takes a route to the north of the Black Country through the old mining town of Cannock. Immediately to the west of Cannock, the A5 passes through the parish of Hatherton.

The name of Hatherton recalls a politician of yesteryear. Edward Littleton was a major landowner in Staffordshire in the early 19th century, and he served as an MP for Staffordshire from 1812 until 1832. Littleton was originally a Tory, but he crossed the floor to the Whigs after George Canning died in 1827 and became involved in the various reforms of the late 1820s and early 1830s. He was an advocate of Catholic emancipation, introduced the bill which became the Truck Act 1831, and was involved in the passage of the Great Reform Act of 1832. This eventually ended up as five Acts, rather than one. Parliament passed three Representation of the People Acts (one each for England and Wales, Scotland and Ireland) which gave effect to the franchise reform, abolished many of the rotten boroughs and created new parliamentary boroughs to cover towns which had previously been unrepresented; these Acts were accompanied by two Parliamentary Boundaries Acts (one for England and Wales, the other for Ireland) which gave effect to the detail of the redistribution of seats. The latter two Acts built on the work of a boundary commission headed by Thomas Drummond, which Littleton served on.

Littleton's own Staffordshire was a winner from the 1832 reform, which enfranchised Stoke-on-Trent (two MPs) and Walsall (one MP) as new boroughs and divided the county of Staffordshire into northern and southern divisions returning two MPs each, with the South Staffordshire constituency of 1832-68 defined in the Parliamentary Boundaries Act 1832 as the Hundreds of South Offlow, Seisdon and Cuttlestone. Littleton himself was successfully re-elected there in 1832, and after fending off an unwanted attempt to install him as Speaker he joined the Grey government as chief secretary for Ireland, serving for just over a year in Cabinet. In 1835 the Melbourne government promoted Edward Littleton to the House of Lords, in which he took the hereditary title of Lord Hatherton after his Staffordshire estate of Hatherton Hall.

The modern-day Hatherton parish is quite small, and in practice today's election in Huntington and Hatherton ward will be dominated by the votes cast in Huntington. This is effectively a northern suburb of Cannock and it is socially similar to the Cannock Chase district over the border, in that Huntington is a former coal-mining village - and one of quite recent vintage, because the Littleton Colliery here was still in operation up to 1994. Most of the undeveloped parts of Huntington parish are part of the Cannock Chase National Landscape.

This old coalfield Labour vote eventually turned into a personal vote for a long-serving councillor. Leslie Jeffrey Ashley, known to all as Jeff Ashley, was first elected for Huntington ward unopposed in 1991, transferring to Huntington and Hatherton ward following boundary changes in 2003. He lost his seat to the Green Party in 2019, but got it back in 2023 and served until his death in September. Ashley also had five decades of service on Huntington parish council.

Since 2007 Jeff Ashley had shared representation of the ward with the Conservatives: first Patricia Williams from 2007, then David Williams from the July 2010 by-election following her death; David Williams had won that by-election by just two votes. Topline vote shares in 2023 were 40% for Labour, much of which was a personal vote for Ashley, 38% for the Conservatives and 22% for the Greens.

These results are not typical of South Staffordshire council, which has a Conservative majority. Huntington and Hatherton ward also has a Conservative MP in the form of Sir Gavin Williamson, who won the newly-drawn seat of Stone, Great Wyrley and Penkridge in 2024. Williamson was first elected in 2010 for the predecessor seat of South Staffordshire (a much smaller seat than the constituency of that name which elected Edward Littleton in 1832), and he had a number of controversial spells in Cabinet under May, Johnson and Sunak. Even his 2024 re-election drew controversy: Williamson was not opposed by Reform UK, whose candidate for Stone, Great Wyrley and Penkridge withdrew at the last moment amid allegations of dirty tricks.

Nonetheless Reform UK swept this area in the 2025 Staffordshire county elections. The Reform UK administration in Staffordshire has burned through a number of council leaders since then, including Chris Large who represented Brewood division (which includes Hatherton) and was briefly in the top job in January and February this year before resigning due to a family bereavement. By coincidence Large had also attracted controversy for being racist on social media, which was unhelpful given that he had taken the leadership over from Ian Cooper after Cooper was also busted for being racist on social media. Large then quit Reform UK shortly afterwards, and he tendered his resignation from the county council earlier this month on health grounds, so the voters of Hatherton parish will be called out for another by-election just before Christmas to replace him. Huntington parish is part of the Penkridge county division, whose Reform UK county councillor Craig Humphreyson has not attracted any such controversy as far as I am aware; he took that seat over from Conservative ward councillor David Williams, who retired at the 2025 county election.

This by-election is to replace the late Jeff Ashley on South Staffordshire council, and the defending Labour candidate Daniel Costigan will no doubt be well aware that he faces an uphill struggle here. Costigan does have the advantage of being the only left-wing option on the ballot, because the Greens are not standing this time. The Conservatives have selected Nathaniel Williams, who has previously been a regular local election candidate in Wolverhampton. And completing a ballot paper of just three candidates is Reform UK's Craig Humphreyson, who was elected in May 2025 as the county councillor for Penkridge division (which, as stated, includes Huntington) and contested a South Staffordshire council by-election in Great Wyrley Landywood ward a month later without success.

Anston and Woodsetts

Rotherham council, South Yorkshire; caused by the death of Conservative councillor John Blackham.

Our next poll takes place on the southern edge of Yorkshire, in a district - Rotherham - that is synonymous with heavy industry and used to have plenty of collieries back in the day. But in the case of the Anston area it's not just coal that has been extracted from the ground here. The bedrock here is a distinctive sand-coloured limestone, which was chosen by Charles Barry in 1839 for his rebuilding of the Palace of Westminster following the 1834 fire. One reason for this choice was Anston's proximity to the Chesterfield Canal, which allowed the stonework to be moved to London relatively cheaply and easily by boat. Unfortunately the Anston stone didn't stand up to the polluted air of London, and much of the decayed exterior stonework of the Palace was replaced in the mid-20th century by similarly-coloured stone sourced from Rutland.

The Anston Brook forms the dividing line between the twin villages of North and South Anston, which are effectively suburbs of the town of Dinnington to the north. However, the census shows that Anston is more middle-class and less deprived than Dinnington, which was a colliery town until the 1990s. The ward is within commuting distance of Sheffield - South Anston lies on the A57 road between Sheffield and Worksop - and it has relatively high levels of owner-occupation. The 2021 census shows that 97.6% of the population here were born in the UK, which is a figure exceeded by only three other wards in Yorkshire.

The village of Woodsetts lies to the east of the Anstons, not far from the border with Worksop, while the ward boundary also includes the parish of Thorpe Salvin to the south and a small part of Dinnington town. Tourists might well be drawn here by the Tropical Butterfly House in North Anston, which isn't just about butterflies: it's a full-blown zoo with a wide range of birds, animals and reptiles on display.

It should be noted that the right to vote in Anston and Woodsetts ward is vested in the local human residents. The ward is part of the Rother Valley parliamentary seat, which covers the small towns and villages in the south of Rotherham borough with Dinnington being its largest town. This seat voted Conservative in 2019 for the first time since its creation 101 years earlier, and the Tories held up relatively well here in 2024: while Labour did gain the seat, their majority was under 1,000 votes. The Labour MP for Rother Valley is Jake Richards, who has quickly joined the ranks of government as a junior justice minister. He is the son of the political broadcaster and journalist Steve Richards.

The Conservatives had followed up on their 2019 parliamentary gain in Rother Valley by coming from nowhere to win 20 seats on Rotherham council in May 2021, including all three seats in Anston and Woodsetts ward. However, they were only just ahead of the Liberal Democrats, and when one of the Conservative councillors resigned a few months later the Lib Dems gained the resulting December 2021 by-election (Andrew's Previews 2021, page 557). The most recent Rotherham council election in May 2024 saw no changes to the seat totals in Anston and Woodsetts ward: topline figures were 35% and 2 seats for the Conservatives, 33% and 1 seat for the Lib Dems, and 20% for Labour. Labour have a small majority on Rotherham council, which is not at stake in this by-election.

Now the Tories have to defend an Anston and Woodsetts by-election for the second time in five years, after ward councillor John Blackham died in September slightly over halfway through his first term of office. Blackham was a retired auditor and accountant, who had attended a full council meeting just two days before his death.

The Conservatives are hoping to continue Blackham's work by selecting another retired financial services professional, Mike Hemingway, as their defending candidate. Standing for the Liberal Democrats is Bev Thornley, who previously stood here in 2021 and 2024 and was less than 100 votes away from winning a seat on the latter occasion. The Labour candidate is Ashley Gregory, who has previously represented Rotherham in the Youth Parliament. Also standing are Annie Blench for the Green Party, Andy Damms for Reform UK who took a Rotherham council by-election off Labour last year, and Paul Vernon for Restore Britain.

Craghead and South Moor

Durham council; caused by the resignation of Reform UK councillor Aaron Hillam.

We'll finish with the week's only Reform UK defence, which is on the north Durham coalfield. Craghead and South Moor are two former mining villages just to the south of the town of Stanley, and they were incorporated into Stanley in the 1930s. South Moor in particular is a southern extension of Stanley, with the housing ending at the Memorial Park which was dedicated in 1920 to commemorate employees of the South Moor Colliery Company who had died in the First World War.

This ward also includes the Stanley suburb of Oxhill together with the intriguingly-named villages of Quaking Houses (which consists of one long, straight cul-de-sac with terraced housing either side) and New Kyo (kyo is an old Durham dialect word for cow). South Moor and Quaking Houses in particular form a seriously deprived census district which has never really recovered from the end of coalmining. Stanley had attempted in the 1960s to diversify away from coal with the opening of a large factory making Ever Ready batteries, but this factory also failed to make it into the 21st century.

Previous elections for this area form a good example of how far Labour have fallen in County Durham. Craghead and South Moor returned only Labour councillors from at least 2005 up until 2021, normally very comfortably: in the days when Derwentside council was the local authority, South Moor ward was often left uncontested. But the 2025 Durham council election turned that on its head, as Craghead and South Moor became part of the Reform UK majority on the council - and very comfortably so. The winning Reform UK slate here last year thrashed Labour by 58-26.

Stanley has been part of the North Durham parliamentary seat since 1983. The last general election was before the Reform UK surge in County Durham really got going, meaning that North Durham retained its status in 2024 as a safe Labour seat. It is represented by first-term backbencher Luke Akehurst, a long-standing figure on the right of the Labour party who has been on and off the party's National Executive Committee over the years. Akehurst might well have entered Parliament some years ago had he not developed some serious health problems, which have left him using a walking stick to get about. Second place here in 2024 went to Reform UK's Andrew Husband, who is now the leader of Durham council; his deputy leader is GB News favourite Darren Grimes, who represents the neighbouring ward of Annfield Plain and Tanfield.

The 2025 Durham elections returned 65 Reform UK councillors against 14 Lib Dems, 12 independents, just 4 Labour (who had come second in the popular vote), 2 Greens and 1 Conservative. Reform UK have subsequently lost two by-elections, one each to the Lib Dems and Labour, and it's fair to say that their council group has not remained united. A series of defections have left the Reform UK group on 50 out of 98 seats at the last count, plus this vacancy. They can afford to lose this by-election, but one more defection loss will tip Durham into no overall control.

The outgoing councillor here is Aaron Hillam, who resigned last month because he has moved away from the county. To replace him in this by-election Reform UK have selected Mark Taylor, whose first job was in management at the Ever Ready factory. The Labour candidate is Les Timbey, who was a rather distant runner-up here last year but did manage to beat his party colleague (and sitting councillor) from higher up the alphabetical ballot paper. Also standing are Victoria Aymard for the Lib Dems, Dorothy Luckhurst for the Conservatives, Callum Hutchinson for the Greens and Jean Thomson for Restore Britain.
