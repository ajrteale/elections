"All the right votes, but not necessarily in the right order"

There is just one by-election on 5th November 2026. This Preview was written before I went on holiday last week, so if anything written here has gone out of date in the last 7 days of the campaign I hope it has gone out of date in an amusing way.

Penn Hill

Bournemouth, Christchurch and Poole council, Dorset; caused by the resignation of Liberal Democrat councillor Jo Clements.

It's Bonfire Night, the evening when fireworks are set off across the UK to mark the time in 1605 when a plot to blow up King James I and most of his court at the state opening of Parliament was foiled. On the evening of the 5th November 1605 Guy Fawkes was caught red-handed in the House of Lords cellars, with enough gunpowder to blow the place sky high. Some places - notably the town of Lewes in East Sussex - still burn Fawkes in effigy each year.

This year's electoral firework display is not a large one, with just one event taking place. Our venue today is the town of Poole, which is an old port located on the sheltered waters of Poole Harbour. This is still important for shipping - there are regular ferries from Poole to the Channel Islands - but recreational boating and yachting is also a major market here, and waterfront housing has long been in demand.

The Lilliput area, on the north-eastern shore of Poole Harbour, ticks both of those boxes. The boating centre of Salterns Marina juts out into the harbour, while there is waterfront housing next to the adjacent Blue Lagoon. This housing isn't as expensive as Sandbanks nearby, but it's expensive enough that Penn Hill ward has quite an affluent and educated demographic. The haves and have-yachts, you might say.

The Penn Hill ward of Poole runs north-east from Lilliput up to Penn Hill itself, which lies roughly halfway between Poole and Bournemouth town centres: it is on the Dorset side of the old Dorset-Hampshire county line which once ran between Poole and Bournemouth. This line is now lost in the urban sprawl, but it still exists in the heads of locals to the extent that the ONS recognises Poole and Bournemouth as the centres of separate Travel to Work Areas.

Despite this, since a 2019 reorganisation Bournemouth, Poole and Christchurch have rather uneasily shared a single council. The two elections to BCP council to date have turned in fragmented results, with the Liberal Democrats emerging as the largest party following the 2023 elections with 28 seats out of a possible 76. They run the council as the leading party in the "Three Towns Alliance", a coalition which also includes the Christchurch Independents, independent councillors from Bournemouth and a Poole localist group with the alliterative name of Poole People.

Penn Hill ward had voted Conservative in the 2019 election and when it was a ward of Poole council, but the Lib Dems were close behind in 2019 and ended up gaining the ward comfortably in 2023. Shares of the vote here three years ago were 43% for the Liberal Democrats, 25% for the Conservatives and 12% for another localist party, Poole Engage.

In July 2024 Poole returned its first ever Labour MP, in one of the closest results of the election. After multiple recounts Sir Robert Syms, who had been Poole's Conservative MP since 1997, lost his seat to Labour candidate Neil Duncan-Jordan by 14,168 votes to 14,150, a majority of 18 votes or 0.04 percentage points. Duncan-Jordan, who had been a Unison officer before his election, lost the Labour whip for a time last year for rebelling over the Starmer government's welfare policies but he is now back in the fold.

The most recent polls here were in May, for the inaugural elections of a brand-new Poole town council. Penn Hill ward is divided between two parish wards: the waterfront section is in Parkstone, Lilliput and Sandbanks ward which split its two seats between Reform UK and the Lib Dems, while the Liberal Democrats won both seats in Branksome West and Penn Hill ward with the Reform UK candidate close behind. Both of these parish wards include territory from neighbouring BCP council wards. Neither of them had candidates from the Conservatives, who were opposed to the creation of the town council and eventually boycotted the election. Poole town council ended up with a Reform UK majority, with 15 Reform UK and 6 Lib Dem councillors.

There have been two BCP council by-elections since July 2024, both of which resulted in a seat change: in October 2024 the Conservatives gained a seat from an independent councillor in Muscliff and Strouden Park ward, but they then lost a seat to the Lib Dems in Talbot and Branksome Woods ward in September 2025. This time the Lib Dems are on the defensive after ward councillor Jo Clements stepped down in September, citing personal circumstances for her resignation.

Defending this seat for the Liberal Democrats is Amanda Jellicoe, a sales and marketing professional who works in the food industry; in May she was an unsuccessful candidate for the town council, in Oakdale and South East Creekmoor ward. The Conservative candidate is Ashley Fraser, who stood in the neighbouring Parkstone ward in the 2023 BCP election. This time there is no Poole Engage candidate so it will be interesting to see where their votes go. Labour have nominated Ralph Lynch, the Green candidate is Ace Cruz, Reform UK have selected Xena Dion who is a town councillor for Parkstone, Lilliput and Sandbanks and was previously a long-serving Conservative Poole councillor for this ward (2003-19), we have independent candidacies from Louise Fielden (who gives an address in faraway Basildon) and Susan Stockwell (who was a Poole People candidate in Parkstone ward in 2023), and Restore Britain candidate Max Walker completes a long ballot paper of eight candidates.
