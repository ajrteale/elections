Frome West

Somerset council; caused by the disqualification of Green Party councillor Michael Dunk. Our last by-election this week is a controversial one. It stems from the actions of Just Stop Oil, a protest group which was active from 2022 to 2025 on the issue of man-made climate change and net zero carbon emissions. This is a topic which, in general, has broad support from the UK public. Polling from YouGov in November 2025 suggested that 60% of Britons supported the UK government's commitments to cutting carbon emissions and achieving net zero by 2050. Much work has already been done towards this target. The UK's wind and solar power generation increases year on year as fossil fuels become unable to compete with clean energy on price; electric cars make up a growing proportion of the UK's transport fleet. But much work remains to be done on this subject in the years to come. This broad popular support for net zero has been achieved despite the actions of Just Stop Oil, which did its best to bring the environmental cause into disrepute with a series of high-profile, dangerous and illegal publicity stunts. These included a series of events in which Just Stop Oil protestors climbed gantries on the M25 London orbital motorway between 7th and 11th November 2022, forcing the motorway to be closed because of the danger to members of the public caused by their actions. The disruption caused by this to people trying to go about their daily lives as normal was widespread. One of the protestors involved was Michael Dunk, who scaled an M25 gantry on 8th November 2022. Dunk was subsequently charged with intentionally or recklessly causing a public nuisance, contrary to section 78 of the Police, Crime, Sentencing and Courts Act 2022. This offence is aimed at conduct which - to quote the Act's explanatory notes - endangers the life, health, property or comfort of a section of the public or obstructs them in the exercise of their rights. It carries a maximum sentence of ten years' imprisonment. Section 78 of the 2022 Act had been passed as the result of a Law Commission recommendation from 2015 to replace the previous common law offence of "public nuisance", for which the maximum sentence was theoretically unlimited. In October 2025 Dunk went on trial at Southwark Crown Court alongside five other Just Stop Oil protestors, all of whom were found guilty by the jury. As in earlier cases involving the November 2022 M25 protests, the trial judge proceeded to throw the book at the defendants. When Judge Perrins passed sentence in December 2025, Michael Dunk was sentenced to 2 years and 2 months in prison and ordered to pay court costs of £4,380. This sentence also disqualifies him from serving as a councillor for five years, but the disqualification did not take effect immediately because Dunk appealed against his conviction. Dunk was eventually ejected from Somerset council in July after the Court of Appeal rejected his appeal; in the interim he had avoided disqualification under the six-month non-attendance rule by attending council meetings while on early release from prison. This finally allows the Frome West by-election to go ahead. This is one of three Somerset wards covering the town of Frome, with West ward taking in the town centre together with a small rural hinterland to the west of the town which is part of the parish of Selwood. Frome is unusual for eastern Somerset in being an old industrial town, with textiles being the traditional industry here. It was enfranchised by the Great Reform Act in 1832, at which point the town had a population of around 11,000 with significant population growth expected as a result of the Industrial Revolution: but this never happened, and the town was disenfranchised in 1885. Today Frome's population is around 29,000. The town's time as an independent parliamentary borough did not get off to a promising start. In the 1832 election only 322 people in Frome had the right to vote, and they had a choice of two candidates: Sir Thomas Champneys (later Mostyn-Champneys) and Thomas Sheppard. There was a lot of bad blood between them. Champneys was the squire of nearby Orchardleigh, but he was in debt and neglected his estate; he was also unmarried, and his reputation was such that in 1820 he had stood trial for sodomy, ultimately being found not guilty. He also owned slaves, which in 1832 was still legal but seriously controversial. Sheppard was firmly on the anti-slavery side, and he had been a key prosecution witness in Champneys' 1820 sodomy trial. The two days of voting in Frome were marred by serious violence, most of which came from Champneys' supporters: a Sheppard supporter died, a house was destroyed, the Riot Act was read out, and the Army had to be called in to restore order. The poll was terminated as soon as Sheppard had secured enough votes to mathematically ensure his win: he was declared the victor by 163 votes to 100, and went on to serve for four terms as MP for Frome.

Sheppard's successor, Robert Boyle, was unseated in 1852 by an election petition. The Commons committee which ruled on election petitions in those days decided that he was disqualified by virtue of being secretary of the Most Illustrious Order of St Patrick, the Irish equivalent of the Order of the Garter. (The last Knight of St Patrick was the old Duke of Gloucester who died in 1974, but the Order has never been formally abolished and Charles III remains its Sovereign.) Boyle resigned his secretaryship and won the resulting Frome by-election unopposed. He died the following year from disease while serving in the Crimean War, and the resulting by-election returned his older brother Richard Boyle, Viscount Dungarvan. Richard succeeded to his father's titles in 1856 and entered the Lords as the 9th Earl of Cork and Orrery; the resulting second Frome by-election returned yet another brother, William Boyle, who defeated the Radical candidate Donald Nicoll by one vote, 158 to 157. Nicoll got his revenge in the general election the following year.

The borough's electorate was small enough that pressure could be brought to bear on the voters by the local aristocrats. In Frome's case that meant the Marquess of Bath, whose seat at Longleat is just over over the county boundary in Wiltshire. A number of members of the Bath family have served as MP for Frome, starting with Lord Edward Thynne who defeated Donald Nicoll to serve as a Conservative MP here from 1859 to 1865. In 1868 Frome had a celebrity MP: the Liberals' Thomas Hughes was a barrister who was prominent in the co-operative movement, but he is remembered today primarily for turning his education at Rugby School into the bestselling novel Tom Brown's School Days.

Since 1885 Frome has been included within a number of Somerset county seats with a variety of boundaries: for example, the 1918-50 Frome constituency included much of what are now Bristol's southern suburbs and as a result it saw strong population growth during that period. The present constituency of Frome and East Somerset was created for the 2024 general election and returned Liberal Democrat candidate Anna Sabine, who is now the party's spokeswoman for culture, media and sport. This seat had been created as a split of the previous Somerton and Frome constituency, which had been gained by the Lib Dems in a July 2023 by-election after the previous Conservative MP David Warburton was brought down by a cocaine and misconduct scandal. The 2023 Somerton and Frome by-election saw the Green Party candidate Martin Dimery finish in third place with 10.2% of the vote, which was at the time a record for the Greens in a parliamentary by-election. Much of this vote will have come out of Frome town, which now has a strong "alternative" vibe and has given high votes to Green candidates in local elections for some years now. In the 2022 Somerset council elections four of the five Green seats came out of Frome, with Dimery and Michael Dunk winning the two seats in Frome West with a 47-33 lead over the Lib Dems. These boundaries had previously been used for elections to Somerset county council from 2013, and the Greens had gained Frome West from the Lib Dems in the 2017 election; on that occasion the outgoing Lib Dem county councillor Derek Tanswell sought re-election on the ticket of the continuing Liberal Party and clearly thought he was sufficiently on first-name terms with the electorate to appear on the ballot paper mononymously as "Derek". He finished in fifth and last place with 2.7%. Before his election to the present Somerset council in 2022, Michael Dunk had previously represented Frome Market ward on Mendip council from 2019 until that council was abolished in 2023. Defending this Somerset council by-election for the Green Party is Dominic Ellison, who is the chief executive of a charity providing services and support to disabled people and their families in the West of England. The Liberal Democrats' Drew Gardner has previously served on Mendip council, representing Frome College ward from 2019 until abolition in 2023. Another all-male ballot is completed by David Quantrill-Simon for the Conservatives, Christopher Wildridge for Labour and Reform UK's Joseph Williams.
