"All the right votes, but not necessarily in the right order"
Five by-elections on 3rd September 2026:

Portobello/Craigmillar; and
Southside/Newington

Edinburgh council, Scotland; caused respectively by the resignations of Scottish National Party councillors Kate Campbell and Simita Kumar.

This is one of the editions of Andrew's Previews I like, where there's something for everyone to enjoy. We have a wide range of locations on the bill this week, ranging from city to town to countryside and with all three nations of Great Britain represented. And we also have a wide political range: with the possible exception of Plaid Cymru, all of the main GB parties in the Westminster parliament will feel they can win at least one of this week's contests.

If you want political plurality, there are few better places than the city of Edinburgh where all of the (then) five Holyrood parties were strongly represented at the last local elections in 2022. Of the 63 seats up for election the Scottish National Party won the most, with 19; there were 13 Labour councillors, 12 Lib Dems, 10 Greens and 9 Conservatives. Labour run a very precarious minority administration. There have been four by-elections in the Scottish capital since then for five seats, and none of them have been held by the defending party: net changes are Lib Dems up 2, Conservatives up 1, Labour down 1 and SNP down 2.

This five-way split is also seen in Southside/Newington ward, where the five parties were separated by just 8 percentage points on first preferences at the 2022 election. Labour's Tim Pogson finished top of the poll on 24% and the Greens' Steve Burgess scored 23%, with both of those parties clearing the 20% quota on first preferences; the SNP's Simita Kumar started on 19% and made it over the line on Green transfers, and transfers from all three of those parties then gave the final seat to the Lib Dems' Pauline Flannery, who had started on 16% and overtook the Conservatives' Cameron Rose to win. Rose had started in fourth place and scoring position on 18%, but couldn't get the transfers to get over the line: Flannery was 180 votes adrift on first preferences, but eventually won by 177.

If you've been traipsing around the Edinburgh Festivals at some point in the last month, then chances are you've visited Southside/Newington ward. Here can be found Bristo Square, the Festival Theatre, the Pleasance and a few other popular Fringe venues. Further out are the eastern half of the Meadows, the affluent housing of Newington and the Grange, the Royal Commonwealth Pool and the shopping mall at Cameron Toll.

This ward is bookended by the University of Edinburgh, taking in the King's Buildings campus on the southern edge of the city together with the complex of university buildings in the Southside around George Square, which has lost most of its original Georgian buildings in the name of progress. Typical of this - but not of the rest of the city - is the Architectural Heaven Circa 1960 which is 40 George Square, a 14-storey towerblock which was previously known as David Hume Tower until the University decided in 2020 that it no longer wanted to be associated with one of Scotland's greatest philosophers. Some years ago I had a very enjoyable day playing quiz in the higher floors of that building, and our hosts from the university quiz society took great delight in pointing out that David Hume Tower has the best view of Edinburgh. The reason for this, of course, being that you can't see David Hume Tower.

Swinging around the southern side of Arthur's Seat brings us to the very different territory of Portobello/Craigmillar ward at the eastern end of the city. Craigmillar itself and the nearby area of Niddrie Mains were largely developed as council housing, much of which has been demolished and redeveloped in this century. This was once quite an industrial area, with several breweries in operation. Portobello, which was once a burgh in its own right and from 1832 to 1918 was a constituent member of the Leith Burghs parliamentary seat, lies on the North Sea coast and traditionally functioned as Edinburgh's seaside resort. In between we find residential Brunstane, which is the first part of Edinburgh that travellers entering by the A1 will see, and the former mining village of Newcraighall; both of these are stops on the Borders railway line towards Tweedbank.

This ward also returned a four-way split of councillors in 2022, but it's much less pluralistic than Southside/Newington. The SNP topped the poll with 37% of the first preferences, Labour polled 24%, the Greens 16% and the Conservatives 15%. 37% really should have been enough votes for two SNP seats at the expense of the Conservatives, but the Nationalists failed to balance their candidates with Kate Campbell topping the poll on 3,438 first preferences and Simon Shedden starting in fifth place and outside scoring position on 744. On the decisive 7th count Shedden was 276 votes behind the Conservatives' Tim Jones, with a Green surplus of 112 votes still to throw which meant that the real margin was closer.

If we rerun the 2022 counts here for a single seat, then Portobello/Craigmillar would have been comfortably SNP with Kate Campbell beating the lead Labour candidate Jane Meagher by 57.8-42.2 after transfers. Southside/Newington would have been much closer, with Labour's Tim Pogson beating the Greens' Steve Burgess in a head-to-head by 51.2-48.8. This was actually a step back for the Greens in Southside/Newington, as on the 2017 results the Greens would have comfortably beaten all comers there in a single-seat contest despite starting in third place on first preferences.

In the Westminster Parliament, Portobello/Craigmillar and the Southside part of Southside/Newington ward lie within the Edinburgh East and Musselburgh constituency, which was gained for Labour in 2024 by Chris Murray. He is the son of the former Scottish Labour MP and now peer Margaret Curran. The majority of Southside/Newington ward is located within the seat of Edinburgh South, which has been in Labour hands since 1987 and was the only Scottish Labour seat to resist the SNP high tides in 2015 and 2019. This has been represented since 2010 by Ian Murray, who was Scottish secretary in the first Starmer Cabinet; Murray is now a junior minister at the Department of Culture, Media and Sport.

The most recent poll here was the Scottish Parliament election in May, at which Portobello/Craigmillar ward is the Edinburgh Eastern section of the Edinburgh Eastern, Musselburgh and Tranent constituency. This newly-drawn seat was comfortably won four months ago by the SNP's Kate Campbell, who had previously been a councillor for Portobello/Craigmillar since 2017 and has now given up her seat in the Edinburgh city chambers to concentrate on her duties down the hill in Holyrood. In May Campbell saw off Labour's Katherine Sangster and Reform UK's Angela Ross in the constituency section of the ballot, but Sangster and Ross both made it into the Scottish Parliament as regional MSPs for Edinburgh and Lothians East.

At Holyrood level Southside/Newington ward is divided between constituencies. Southside and Sciennes are covered by Edinburgh Central, which in 2026 was one of the first two Holyrood constituencies to return a Green constituency MSP: she is Lorna Slater, who was previously a co-leader of the Scottish Greens from 2019 to 2025. Meanwhile Edinburgh Southern, which covers the majority of Southside/Newington, re-elected its Labour constituency MSP Daniel Johnson for his third term of office.

Meanwhile the SNP councillor for Southside/Newington, Simita Kumar, was elected in May as the constituency MSP for Edinburgh South Western which covers no part of this ward: that seat is based on south-western suburbs like Slateford, Colinton, Sighthill and the Pentland Hills villages. Kumar, who was born in Fiji and came to the UK at the age of 17, was first elected for Southside/Newington in 2022 and had been leader of the SNP group on Edinburgh council. She was comfortably elected to Holyrood in May with a 10-point lead over Labour, the Greens standing down in her favour.

The last two local elections in Southside/Newington have both been contested by only five candidates, one from each of the (then) main Holyrood parties. This by-election has more candidates than the last two polls put together, with some rather lax candidate nomination rules for Scottish local elections combining with the fact that this is the capital to encourage everyone to push their luck. There are 13 candidates for the voters to rank in order of preference.

Defending Southside/Newington for the Scottish National Party is Sam Mackenzie who is originally from the Highlands, came to Edinburgh for his university studies and then settled here. Labour have turned to the next generation by selecting first-time candidate Ruth Elliott, who - slightly surprisingly given the size of the field - is top of the alphabetical ballot paper. The Green candidate is self-employed gardener and Edinburgh maths graduate Isabella Gastel Alejandre. The Conservatives needed a new candidate after their previous ward councillor walked off to join Reform UK, and Nick Layden is the new Tory standard-bearer here. The Lib Dems' Artie Khovanov is an Englishman who is reading for a PhD in informatics at Edinburgh University and does research into AI in maths. A new party called Edinburgh and East Lothian People has taken the bizarre decision to stand three candidates for the one seat available in this by-election: they are party leader Marc Wilkinson, whom we met three weeks ago as an also-ran in the Clacton by-election, and his running-mates Derrick Emms and Markham Rowbotham. Emms, Rowbotham and Wilkinson also all stood in the last Edinburgh council by-election in Fountainbridge/Craiglockhart ward last year, with Wilkinson performing best on 1.8%. We have an independent candidacy from Roisin Keirdashian, who describes herself as an independent working-class socialist; Richard Lucas is the leader of the Scottish Family Party and is a regular candidate in Edinburgh council by-elections; Sean Moffat stood for Holyrood in May on the Advance UK ticket and now has the nomination of the Alliance for Democracy and Freedom; Reform UK's Gary Neill is a sales professional who stood unsuccesfully in Edinburgh Central in May; and Richard Shillcock is the candidate of the Communist Party of Britain.

The ballot paper in Portobello/Craigmillar is only slightly shorter with ten candidates standing nominated. Here the SNP defence is led by the wonderfully-named Silas McGilvray, who has worked all over the world and is currently a project manager with Healthcare Improvement Scotland. Labour have selected Jim Wyke, who is a children's rights campaigner and a civil servant working in marine environmental protection. The Greens' Lorenzo Martinico works in the co-operative sector. Standing for the Conservatives is Haris Young, who was an unsuccessful Holyrood candidate in May in Edinburgh North Eastern and Leith. Also on the Portobello/Craigmillar ballot are Liss Owen for the Lib Dems, David Ballantine for the Alliance for Democracy and Freedom, Niel Deepnarain for the Scottish Family Party, Pete Gregson for the Workers Party of Britain, Reform UK's Andrew McLaughlin who is Canadian by birth and was an unsuccessful Holyrood candidate in May in Edinburgh Northern, and Peter Olech for the Communist Party of Britain. As in all Scottish council by-elections, Votes at 16 apply and the Alternative Vote is in use - so despite the large number of candidates the winners of these by-elections should end up with broad popular support.

Chesterton and Launton

Oxfordshire county council; caused by the resignation of Liberal Democrat councillor William Boucher-Giles.

We now travel south into England for a division which covers a large swathe of north-eastern Oxfordshire. The Chesterton and Launton county division contains a total of 17 parishes, surrounding the town of Bicester on three sides.

The two eponymous villages can be found close to Bicester on opposite sides of the town. To the west is Chesterton, which takes its name from its location on the Roman road of Akeman Street towards Cirencester: this is followed by the modern-day Green Lane through the village, which continues west in a straight line to Kirtlington. Launton is located east of Bicester on the railway line towards Bletchley, which has recently been reinstated as part of the East West Rail project; however, passenger services on this route have yet to restart and might not include the reinstatement of Launton railway station, which closed in 1967.

Further new railway construction is underway at the north-east corner of this division in the parishes of Finmere, Godington, Mixbury and Newton Purcell with Shelswell, which lie on the route of High Speed 2. Infrastructure from an earlier age can be found in the M40 motorway, which runs through the western part of the division. The division boundaries include the Cherwell Valley service area, which was opened in 1994 and rebuilt after being destroyed by fire in 2010. Despite the name, this service area is not in the Cherwell valley at all: the brook which runs next to the services is a tributary of the River Great Ouse and it lies outside the Thames catchment area.

Launton is one of only two parishes in this division with more than 1,000 electors on the roll. Despite the division name, the largest parish here is Caversfield which is now essentially a northern extension of Bicester; this village has always had a special status, as it was a detached part of Buckinghamshire until 1844. Caversfield has had a long association with the military thanks to the presence of nearby Bicester Aerodrome, which lies over the parish boundary in Launton: this was an RAF grass airfield until 2004, latterly used for glider training, and it is now run by a firm called Bicester Motion as a business park dedicated to motoring and aviation. Clients include the Audi Formula 1 team, who have a technology centre here. Vorsprung durch Technik, as some people might say.

The Bicester area in general has seen strong population growth this century, and the sort of people who have moved here have shown willingness to vote for the Liberal Democrats in recent years. Bicester joined the parliamentary map in 2024 with the creation of the Bicester and Woodstock constituency, which was won at its first contest by the Liberal Democrats' Calum Miller; he is now the party's foreign affairs spokesman, and he had previously been a senior civil servant in the Bernard Woolley role of principal private secretary.

Chesterton and Launton county division was created by a redistribution in 2025 which gave extra seats to Bicester and the surrounding area, and it turned in quite a close result. William Boucher-Giles was elected as the Lib Dem county councillor with just 33% of the vote thanks to a perfect right-wing vote split, with the Conservatives polling 28% and Reform UK 27%. The main predecessor divisions of Ploughley and Bicester North had both been strongly Conservative at the previous county elections in 2021. The Lib Dem win here was important, as the party only narrowly won control of Oxfordshire county council in 2025 with 36 seats against 12 Labour, 10 Conservative, 7 Greens and 1 each for Reform UK, the Independent Oxford Alliance (who caucus with the Conservatives), other independents (who also caucus with the Conservatives) and the Henley Residents Group. One Lib Dem county councillor has since quit the party, so the Lib Dems need to hold this by-election to keep their overall majority on the council.

The second-tier local authority here is Cherwell council, which combines the Bicester area with the Banbury area north of Oxford. The ward boundaries don't match up, but this division has similar boundaries to the Fringford and Heyfords ward which was a Lib Dem gain from the Conservatives in 2024 and 2026. That ward doesn't include Caversfield and Launton parishes, which are also in wards which voted Lib Dem in May (respectively Bicester North and Caversfield, and Launton and Otmoor).

This crucial by-election has been triggered by the resignation of William Boucher-Giles, who has relocated to Switzerland with his family. Defending for the Lib Dems is James Hartley, a Royal Navy veteran who has been a regular council candidate elsewhere in the Cherwell district in recent years: he has contested Deddington ward at each election from 2022 to 2026. The Conservatives' Sandy Dallimore stood unsuccessfully here in 2025 and in Fringford and Heyfords in May: he is a former Cherwell councillor who represented Bicester East ward from 2021 to 2024. Also back for another go after standing unsuccessfully here in 2025 is Reform UK's Ian Hodgson, who stood in Launton and Otmoor ward in May and runs the party's Bicester and Woodstock branch. Completing this ballot paper are Meri Mosharrafa for the Greens and Gregor Fish for Labour.

Llantwit Fardre

Rhondda Cynon Taf council; caused by the resignation of Conservative councillor Sam Trask.

Our Welsh by-election this week is in the county borough of Rhondda Cynon Taf, which predominantly covers the Valleys of south Wales: the three rivers in the council name are a bit of a clue. But Llantwit Fardre doesn't fit neatly into any of the valleys, because it lies on relatively high ground between the Taff to the east and the Ely to the west. The main communication link here is a relatively new link road, opened in 2010 to relieve the congested Llantrisant Road through Llantwit Fardre, which bypasses the village and provides a fast connection from the Taff valley to Llantrisant. This road separates Llantwit Fardre from the smaller village of Efail Isaf to the west.

The good communication links and the area's proximity to Cardiff have turned Llantwit Fardre into a commuter centre, with voting patterns to match. In the 2008 local elections Llantwit Fardre became the first ward of Rhondda Cynon Taf to return a Conservative councillor, Joel James, and at the most recent Welsh local elections in 2022 this area remained the only splash of blue on the local electoral map as the Tory slate defeated Labour by 49-35. The only other RCT ward the Conservatives have ever won was Pont-y-clun in 2017, but that ward was broken up by a boundary review in 2022.

Looking up to voting patterns in the two Parliaments confirms that Llantwit Fardre is not typical of the wider area. At Westminster level the village is part of the Pontypridd constituency, which has been in Labour hands for over a century. The current MP Alex Davies-Jones has represented the seat since 2019, and she is on the Ministry of Justice's frontbench team with responsibility for victims, violence against women and girls, and international justice. In May's Senedd elections the wider constituency of Pontypridd Cynon Merthyr returned three MSs for Plaid Cymru, two for Reform UK and one for Labour; top of the Plaid list was Heledd Fychan, who is now in the Welsh Cabinet as Trefnydd (or leader of the Senedd), Plaid chief whip and cabinet minister for culture and sport.

May's result suggests that the Conservatives have some work to do in this by-election. The outgoing councillor is Sam Trask, who won a previous by-election here in May 2021 and was subsequently selected as the Conservatives' prospective candidate for Bridgend in the 2024 general election - only to pull out at the last minute following a controversy over sexually crude social media posts about women. Trask has now stood down from Rhondda Cynon Taf council because he has moved away from the area. In the 2022 local elections he had beaten his Conservative running-mate Karl Johnson who was higher up the alphabetical ballot paper, which might suggest that Trask had developed something of a personal vote.

Defending this seat for the Conservatives is Joel James, who back in 2008 had become the first Conservative Rhondda Cynon Taf councillor when he was elected for this ward. James was elected in 2021 to the Senedd as a regional MS for South Wales Central and retired from the council in 2022 in consequence, but he failed to be re-elected as an MS in May when he was top of the Conservative list in Caerdydd Ffynnon Taf. Another defeated Senedd candidate from May on the ballot is Labour's Lloyd Watkins, who was number 2 on their list for Pontypridd Cynon Merthyr; Watkins is a former civil servant who has a PhD on the impact of artificial intelligence, robotics and automation on government. Also on an all-male ballot paper are Plaid Cymru's Danny Crudge who gives an address in the ward, serial independent candidate Jonathan Bishop whose long recent electoral record includes finishing in ninth and last place in this ward in 2017 and in the Pontypridd constituency in 2024, and Reform UK's Lyndon Evans who is described as retired with a professional background in commerce and logistics.

Frome West

Somerset council; caused by the disqualification of Green Party councillor Michael Dunk.

Our last by-election this week is a controversial one. It stems from the actions of Just Stop Oil, a protest group which was active from 2022 to 2025 on the issue of man-made climate change and net zero carbon emissions. This is a topic which, in general, has broad support from the UK public. Polling from YouGov in November 2025 suggested that 60% of Britons supported the UK government's commitments to cutting carbon emissions and achieving net zero by 2050.

Much work has already been done towards this target. The UK's wind and solar power generation increases year on year as fossil fuels become unable to compete with clean energy on price. Electric cars make up a growing proportion of the UK's transport fleet. But there is a lot which remains to be done on this subject in the years to come.

The broad popular support for net zero has been achieved despite the actions of Just Stop Oil, which did its best to bring the environmental cause into disrepute with a series of high-profile, dangerous and illegal publicity stunts. These included a series of events in which Just Stop Oil protestors climbed gantries on the M25 London orbital motorway between 7th and 11th November 2022, forcing the motorway to be closed because of the danger to members of the public caused by their actions. The disruption caused by this to people trying to go about their daily lives as normal was widespread.

One of the protestors involved was Michael Dunk, who scaled an M25 gantry on 8th November 2022. Dunk was subsequently charged with intentionally or recklessly causing a public nuisance, contrary to section 78 of the Police, Crime, Sentencing and Courts Act 2022. This offence is aimed at conduct which - to quote the Act's explanatory notes - endangers the life, health, property or comfort of a section of the public or obstructs them in the exercise of their rights. It carries a maximum sentence of ten years' imprisonment. Section 78 of the 2022 Act had been passed as the result of a Law Commission recommendation from 2015 to replace the previous common law offence of "public nuisance", for which the maximum sentence was theoretically unlimited.

In October 2025 Dunk went on trial at Southwark Crown Court alongside five other Just Stop Oil protestors, all of whom were found guilty by the jury. As in earlier cases involving the November 2022 M25 protests, the trial judge proceeded to throw the book at the defendants. When Judge Perrins passed sentence in December 2025, Michael Dunk was sentenced to 2 years and 2 months in prison and ordered to pay court costs of £4,380. This sentence also disqualifies him from serving as a councillor for five years, but the disqualification did not take effect immediately because Dunk appealed against his conviction. Dunk was eventually ejected from Somerset council in July after the Court of Appeal rejected his appeal; in the interim he had avoided disqualification under the six-month non-attendance rule by attending council meetings while on early release from prison.

This finally allows the Frome West by-election to go ahead. This is one of three Somerset wards covering the town of Frome, with West ward taking in the town centre together with a small rural hinterland to the west of the town which is part of the parish of Selwood.

Frome is unusual for eastern Somerset in being an old industrial town, with textiles being the traditional industry here. It was enfranchised by the Great Reform Act in 1832, at which point the town had a population of around 11,000 with significant population growth expected as a result of the Industrial Revolution: but this never happened, and the town was disenfranchised in 1885. Today Frome's population is around 29,000.

The town's time as an independent parliamentary borough did not get off to a promising start. In the 1832 election only 322 people in Frome had the right to vote, and they had a choice of two candidates: Sir Thomas Champneys (later Mostyn-Champneys) and Thomas Sheppard. There was a lot of bad blood between them. Champneys was the squire of nearby Orchardleigh, but he was in debt and neglected his estate; he was also unmarried, and his reputation was such that in 1820 he had stood trial for sodomy, ultimately being found not guilty. He also owned slaves, which in 1832 was still legal but seriously controversial. Sheppard was firmly on the anti-slavery side, and he had been a key prosecution witness in Champneys' 1820 sodomy trial. The two days of voting in Frome were marred by serious violence, most of which came from Champneys' supporters: a Sheppard supporter died, a house was destroyed, the Riot Act was read out, and the Army had to be called in to restore order. The poll was terminated as soon as Sheppard had secured enough votes to mathematically ensure his win: he was declared the victor by 163 votes to 100, and went on to serve for four terms as MP for Frome.

Sheppard's successor, Robert Boyle, was unseated in 1852 by an election petition. The Commons committee which ruled on election petitions in those days decided that he was disqualified by virtue of being secretary of the Most Illustrious Order of St Patrick, the Irish equivalent of the Order of the Garter. (The last Knight of St Patrick was the old Duke of Gloucester who died in 1974, but the Order has never been formally abolished and Charles III remains its Sovereign.) Boyle resigned his secretaryship and won the resulting Frome by-election unopposed. He died the following year from disease while serving in the Crimean War, and the resulting by-election returned his older brother Richard Boyle, Viscount Dungarvan. Richard succeeded to his father's titles in 1856 and entered the Lords as the 9th Earl of Cork and Orrery; the resulting second Frome by-election returned yet another brother, William Boyle, who defeated the Radical candidate Donald Nicoll by one vote, 158 to 157. Nicoll got his revenge in the general election the following year.

The borough's electorate was small enough that pressure could be brought to bear on the voters by the local aristocrats. In Frome's case that meant the Marquess of Bath, whose seat at Longleat is just over over the county boundary in Wiltshire. A number of members of the Bath family have served as MP for Frome, starting with Lord Edward Thynne who defeated Donald Nicoll to serve as a Conservative MP here from 1859 to 1865. In 1868 Frome had a celebrity MP: the Liberals' Thomas Hughes was a barrister who was prominent in the co-operative movement, but he is remembered today primarily for turning his education at Rugby School into the bestselling novel Tom Brown's School Days.

Since 1885 Frome has been included within a number of Somerset county seats with a variety of boundaries: for example, the 1918-50 Frome constituency included much of what are now Bristol's southern suburbs and as a result it saw strong population growth during that period. The present constituency of Frome and East Somerset was created for the 2024 general election and returned Liberal Democrat candidate Anna Sabine, who is now the party's spokeswoman for culture, media and sport. This seat had been created as a split of the previous Somerton and Frome constituency, which had been gained by the Lib Dems in a July 2023 by-election after the previous Conservative MP David Warburton was brought down by a cocaine and misconduct scandal.

The 2023 Somerton and Frome by-election saw the Green Party candidate Martin Dimery finish in third place with 10.2% of the vote, which was at the time a record for the Greens in a parliamentary by-election. Much of this vote will have come out of Frome town, which now has a strong "alternative" vibe and has given high votes to Green candidates in local elections for some years now. In the 2022 Somerset council elections four of the five Green seats came out of Frome, with Dimery and Michael Dunk winning the two seats in Frome West with a 47-33 lead over the Lib Dems. These boundaries had previously been used for elections to Somerset county council from 2013, and the Greens had gained Frome West from the Lib Dems in the 2017 election; on that occasion the outgoing Lib Dem county councillor Derek Tanswell sought re-election on the ticket of the continuing Liberal Party and clearly thought he was sufficiently on first-name terms with the electorate to appear on the ballot paper mononymously as "Derek". He finished in fifth and last place with 2.7%. Before his election to the present Somerset council in 2022, Michael Dunk had previously represented Frome Market ward on Mendip council from 2019 until that council was abolished in 2023.

Defending this Somerset council by-election for the Green Party is Dominic Ellison, who is the chief executive of a charity providing services and support to disabled people and their families in the West of England. The Liberal Democrats' Drew Gardner has previously served on Mendip council, representing Frome College ward from 2019 until abolition in 2023. Another all-male ballot is completed by David Quantrill-Simon for the Conservatives, Christopher Wildridge for Labour and Reform UK's Joseph Williams.
