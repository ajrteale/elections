"All the right votes, but not necessarily in the right order"

Higher Kinnerton

Flintshire council, North Wales; caused by the resignation of independent councillor Mike Allport.

Our first by-election this week is located in the borderlands. This is where we find Higher Kinnerton, a village which gets its post from Chester six miles to the north-east and looks to Chester for most of its services, but which is located over the border in Wales. Meanwhile the nearby village of Lower Kinnerton is in England.

This national border has political consequences. Higher Kinnerton's commuter demographic is pretty much indistinguishable from the neighbouring Cheshire ward of Christleton and Huntington which this column described in June, and if this ward were on the Cheshire side of the line it would no doubt politically be deepest blue. But this is Flintshire and the political context is different. Flintshire council is traditionally a very weak Conservative area - in the 2022 local elections the Tories won just two seats here out of a possible 67, and one of those has since been lost to Labour in a by-election - and the challenge to Labour on the council is normally led by independent councillors.

The two forces are normally quite evenly matched, and this was the case in 2022 when Labour won 31 seats and formed a minority administration against 30 independents, 4 Lib Dems and 2 Conservatives. It has to be said that these figures don't bear much resemblance to the current composition: in particular, the independent councillors are nowhere near being a united bloc. At the time of writing the Flintshire council website lists 24 Labour councillors plus three more listed separately as "Labour and Co-operative Party", 9 councillors in the independent group, 8 Reform UK councillors (six defectors and two by-election winners), six councillors in the Flintshire People's Voice group, six councillors in the True Independents group, four Liberal Democrats, three members in the Eagle group (two elected as independents, one as a Lib Dem), one Conservative, one "Independent (Unattached)", one "non aligned" and one "not specified". The "not specified" councillor is Mike Allport whose resignation prompted this by-election, while Teresa Carberry is still shown on the council website as part of the Labour group even though she died last month. A future by-election will be held for her seat in Mold Broncoed ward in due course.

Higher Kinnerton ward has returned an independent candidate to Flintshire council at every election this century. Mike Allport was first elected here in 2017, when he defeated the Labour candidate by 67-20. Nobody stood against Allport when he went for a second term in 2022, and he was re-elected unopposed. Welsh local elections can be like that.

There is more variety here at elections to other levels of government. Higher Kinnerton is part of the Alyn and Deeside Westminster constituency, which is represented by long-serving Labour MP Sir Mark Tami who has been in situ since 2001: he was the only Labour MP to survive the near-wipeout of the party in North Wales in 2019. Tami has spent most of his Westminster career in the Whips office, and he has been Labour's Deputy Chief Whip since 2023.

Following the 2026 Senedd Cymru electoral reform, Tami's Alyn and Deeside constituency is combined with the Wrexham constituency to form the Fflint Wrecsam constituency which elects six MSs. In May those six seats split two each to Reform UK and Plaid Cymru, and one each to the Conservatives and Labour. The Labour MS here is Ken Skates, who took over as Welsh Labour leader following their election drubbing as one of the few senior figures in the party left standing. The former Alyn and Deeside Labour MS Jack Sargeant, who had succeeded his late father in a 2018 by-election, lost his seat.

Whatever could this mean for today's Higher Kinnerton by-election? Hoping to keep the ward's independent streak going is Penny Brett-Roberts, who stood in 2022 in the neighbouring ward of Broughton South and is a Broughton and Bretton community councillor. Meanwhile the council's Flintshire People's Voice group have registered with the Electoral Commission as a political party, and they have nominated Josh Swash who works in the NHS and is a Higher Kinnerton community councillor. The other candidates in this by-election are all from parties on the right of politics: Reform UK's candidate is Carl Mather, Kristina Renshaw has the nomination of the Heritage Party, and the Conservatives' Oliver Wilson will improve on the seventh place he managed at a by-election in Leeswood ward in January - because this time there are only five names on the ballot paper.

Shepshed

Leicestershire county council; caused by the resignation of Reform UK councillor Helen Butler.

Two weeks ago this column was in Leicestershire, as the Conservatives had no trouble holding a by-election to North West Leicestershire council in the Long Whatton and Diseworth ward. We now come to the neighbouring county division of Shepshed, a market town to the west of Loughborough which was historically a centre for the wool trade.

Modern-day Shepshed has excellent road connections to all of the major East Midlands cities thanks to its location on the M1 motorway. The map shows new development in the ward's north-east corner, east of the motorway: this is part of the large Garendon Park housing estate which is under construction, and which will be linked to the motorway junction by a new road named after William Railton. He was the architect of Nelson's Column in London, and he also designed the country house at Grace Dieu which lies to the west of Shepshed, outside the boundary of this division.

The new development hasn't yet had much chance to affect this area's census return or its election results, which have seen rather wild swings recently. Shepshed is generally a Conservative-voting town in local elections, but Labour are capable of winning seats here in good years. The last elections to Charnwood council took place in 2023 which was a good Labour year, and both Shepshed wards split their two seats between the Conservatives and Labour on that occasion.

The town is part of the Loughborough parliamentary seat, a bellwether which has voted for the government at every election since February 1974. Its current Labour MP is Jeevun Sandher, who comfortably defeated the Conservatives' Jane Hunt in 2024.

Shepshed then swung in another direction in May 2025 when Reform UK gained the town's county council seat from the Conservatives. It was not a close result: the Reform UK candidate Helen Butler polled 44% against 25% for the Conservatives and 17% for Labour. Reform UK took minority control of the county council and Butler joined the council cabinet with the finance and resources portfolio, but she stood down from the frontline in October 2025 citing family commitments. She then submitted her resignation as a councillor in August, again to spend more time with her family.

Defending for Reform UK is Stuart Wood, a retired police inspector who wins style points for his handlebar moustache - but will that translate into votes? The Conservative candidate is Sarah Monk who represents Dishley, Hathern and Thorpe Acre ward (which covers the part of this division with all the new development) on Charnwood council. Labour have reselected Jane Lennie who appeared in this column all the way back in 2013 when she won a Charnwood council by-election in Shepshed West ward: she lost that seat in 2015 but got it back in 2023. Katy Brookes-Duncan of the Lib Dems, David Kellock of the Greens and Alex McKenzie of Restore Britain complete a list of six candidates.

Salisbury Milford

Wiltshire council; caused by the resignation of Liberal Democrat councillor Alan Bayliss.

The second half of today's Preview covers two Liberal Democrat defences in south-west England. We'll start in the city of Salisbury, which overall returns eight of the 98 members of Wiltshire council. Milford is Salisbury's eastern ward, and it has a long and thin shape taking in not just Milford but also Bishopdown in the city's north-east corner.

This is where the roads and the railway lines from London and Southampton enter Salisbury. The railway lines converge at Salisbury Tunnel Junction, which was the location of a major accident in 2021 when two trains towards Salisbury collided at the junction, fortunately without loss of life. The accident investigation found that the train from London had failed to stop as a result of low rail adhesion, caused by that curse of the railways at this time of year: leaves on the line.

The Salisbury parliamentary seat has been continuously in Conservative hands for over a century. In 2024 it re-elected Tory MP John Glen for his fifth term of office. Glen had served in the Cabinet throughout the Sunak administration, and at the time of the 2024 general election he was the Minister for the Cabinet Office and Paymaster General.

The electoral cycle here meant that it was only after the Conservatives lost power nationally that the wheels came off locally for them in Wiltshire. The 2025 Wiltshire elections left the council finely balanced. The Liberal Democrats emerged as the largest party with 43 seats, the Conservatives won 37, Reform UK 10, independents won 7 and there was one Labour councillor, who has since left the party and formed a council group with one of the independents. With 50 seats required for an overall majority the independent councillors hold the balance of power, and a Lib Dem and independent coalition has run Wiltshire since 2025. But council control may be at stake in this by-election: a gain for the Conservatives or Reform UK would give those parties half of the 98 seats on the council plus the casting vote of the chairman, Conservative councillor Laura Mayes, and they might then seek to depose the ruling coalition.

Salisbury Milford was a Lib Dem gain from the Conservatives at the 2025 election, with the party's candidate Alan Bayliss pulling off a big swing to win with 44% of the vote against 24% for the defending Conservatives and 18% for Reform UK. Bayliss then resigned his seats on Wiltshire council and on the parish-level Salisbury city council at the start of September, citing personal reasons.

This sets up a potentially crucial by-election in which the defending Lib Dem candidate is James "Mac" McIntyre Brown, who is a Salisbury city councillor and is described as a social and healthcare management specialist, whatever that means. Last year McIntyre Brown stood in Salisbury Harnham East ward. The Conservatives have selected Laura Jones, who stood in Laverstock ward just outside the city boundary in 2021 and 2025 and is involved with the Milford Area Preservation Group. Simon Lever, who was the Reform UK candidate here last year, is standing again. A ballot paper of four candidates is completed by Labour's Sony Assuon.

Bradninch

Mid Devon council; caused by the resignation of Liberal Democrat councillor Luke Taylor.

This week's final by-election is in the Devon countryside. The tiny town of Bradninch lies about ten miles north-east of Exeter, on high ground to the north of the Culm valley through which the M5 motorway and Great Western railway line run. It has a long history. In mediaeval times Bradninch was one of Devon's eight feudal baronies, and in 1170 the lord of the manor was William de Tracy, one of the four knights who murdered Thomas Becket in Canterbury Cathedral that year. As penance for this de Tracy was exiled by Pope Alexander III to the Holy Land, from which he did not return. The current lord of the manor is Prince William, Duke of Cornwall, and the Duchy of Cornwall is presently in the process of selling large amounts of land in the parish.

Bradninch never returned MPs to Parliament, but it was a borough of its own until 1886. The Bradninch Guildhall, a municipal building dating from 1835, is a relic of that era. Not far away is the parish church which is the UK's only church dedicated to St Disen, an Irish missionary who is thought to have founded the church in the 7th century.

Today the local authority here is Mid Devon council, which was the subject of a Liberal Democrat takeover at the 2023 election. This brought to power the Lib Dem group leader Luke Taylor, who had represented Bradninch ward since 2015 and turned it into one of the safest Lib Dem seats in the land. Taylor polled 91% of the vote in 2019 in a straight fight with the Conservatives, who actually performed even worse in 2023: that year the Lib Dem vote in a slightly expanded Bradninch ward was down to a mere 82%, with Labour second and the Conservatives third. Mid Devon council as a whole was almost as politically lopsided, with the Lib Dems winning 33 out of 42 seats at that election.

Despite this, Bradninch town has a Conservative MP. Since 2010 it has been part of the Central Devon constituency represented by Sir Mel Stride, who was work and pensions secretary when he was very narrowly re-elected for a fifth term of office in 2024: his majority over Labour was just 61 votes. Stride had spent most of the Conservatives' period in opposition since then as shadow chancellor, but he returned to the backbenches at a reshuffle in August. His constituency covers Bradninch Town parish ward, which returns eleven of Bradninch's twelve parish councillors; the Bradninch Rural parish ward is part of the Honiton and Sidmouth constituency represented by Lib Dem MP Richard Foord. Bradninch ward last went to the polls in 2025 as part of the Cullompton and Bradninch division of Devon county council, which was a Lib Dem gain from the Conservatives on that occasion.

Luke Taylor stood down as leader of Mid Devon council in May, and he has now left the council to take up a new job in the USA. Defending the resulting Bradninch by-election for the Lib Dems is Mary Ryan, a retired GP who last appeared on a ballot paper in the 2019 Mid Devon election when she stood in Cullompton South ward. She is opposed by Labour's Piers Kotting (who was an extremely distant runner-up here in 2023), Dirk McFarlane-Aitken for the Conservatives, Kirsty Mackenzie for the Greens and Liam O'Brien for Reform UK.
